The poster is US-flavoured.   • [Certain] The "Claude Secret Commands" GIF shows 24 command names and one-line jobs and nothing about their contents. Eight already exist here by name and the rest have close equivalents, so I mapped rather than rebuilt: inventing the original logic from a one-liner would not reproduce it. They are one person's private skills; a pack offered by commenting a keyword should be read before installation.
  • [Certain] "Deloitte-style AI marketing team" (12 departments, 60 agents) shows agent names and one-line jobs, with a mock-up "All 60 agents done" status. It shows no prompts. "Deloitte-style" is the author's phrase and implies no connection to that firm. I added the names as a reference under the existing marketing-team skill, not a new team.
  • [Certain] "25 AI governance decisions" is a useful question list with two terms marked as the author's trademarks ("Human Authority Line", "Governance Traceability Map"). I attributed them and added a 0-1-2 scorecard. It is a checklist, not a maturity model, and not legal advice.
  • [Certain] I did not open any of the 22 free websites, so none is confirmed live or free. The riskiest two for ordinary users are VirusTotal (uploaded files are shared with security vendors) and online PDF tools (your file is uploaded).
  • [Likely] The two GTM graphics and the LinkedIn GTM map are generic frameworks without data. The LinkedIn map's "comment keyword" and "lead magnet DM" elements can lead to automation that breaches platform terms and privacy rules.

3. Where I disagree with the source material

  • I disagree with a "negotiate everything" poster as a default. The risk is a rescinded offer from a rigid script or an invented competing offer. Negotiate the components that matter and never lie about numbers.
  • I disagree with 60 agents from one brief. The risk is cost and unreviewed output across ad accounts and the CRM. Run one department, read every output, keep write access off.
