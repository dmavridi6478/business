The interview replacement lines are templates, and reading them out is a risk. They contain placeholders such as "[specific skill]". Claiming a skill, tool or company initiative you do not have is worse than the weak answer it replaces. Salary talk in particular varies by country. [Likely]

The GTM stages are guideposts, as the author says. Customer-count thresholds (0 to 10, 10 to 50, 50 to 100) fit low-priced products; an expensive B2B product may never reach 100 customers. Use the "working when" tests, not the numbers. [Likely]

4. Copy-paste prompts and commands

After restarting Claude Code the commands exist: /first-100-customers, /interview-donts, /revenue-agent-pick. The three prompts below are mine.

1. Place us on the first-100-customers roadmap (mine)
Use the gtm-first-100-customers skill. We have [N] customers, our offer is [OFFER] at [PRICE], and our channels are [LIST]. Say which stage fits, give the goal, lead generation, channels, build list and the 'working when' test for it, name one thing to stop doing and one risk of moving on too early. Remind me the stages are guideposts.
2. Rewrite my interview answers (mine)
Use the job-interview-10-donts skill. I am applying for [ROLE]. My three real achievements are [A], [B], [C]. For each of the ten situations write a short answer using only these achievements and leave a placeholder where I have no evidence. Flag anything that would claim something I have not told you.
3. Pick a revenue role and write its prompt (mine)
Use the claude-revenue-system-45-agents skill. My task is [TASK] and I have [DATA]. Choose one or two roles, note if a role is duplicated in the list, write the prompt, and list what a person must verify before any message is sent. Do not contact anyone.
