1. Executive findings

  • [Certain] One of the six is a repeat. "AI Growth for Corporate Leaders and Businesses" (60 tools, six categories) is the same graphic handled in Batch 123 as ai-tools-50-directory/references/60-ai-growth-tools-corporate.md. It was not rebuilt. If you are re-sending images to be safe, the library already holds them.
  • [Certain] The AI-citation graphic mixes a verified statistic with an unverified one. I had a subagent read Promptwatch's own pages: the Reddit figures (3.83% falling to 0.52%, an 86.4% relative drop) are exactly as published on 18 and 20 August 2026, but the source says the size of the drop is provisional and does not state a sample size or model version. The "7% to 0%" for G2, Capterra and Trustpilot on GPT-5.1 and GPT-5.6 does not appear on any page read, and no source read names those model versions. A later Promptwatch post (22 October) gives different review-site shares (G2 0.73%, Capterra 0.32%) for a different window. The skill quotes the first as provisional and the second as unverified. The graphic is also a sales page for a citation-tracking vendor.
  • [Certain] The LinkedIn authority percentages (62%, 70%, 39%, 48%, 43%) have no sample size, date or population. They answer different questions and cannot be ranked against each other, and "leading primary inbound motion at 70%" is ambiguous. I kept the two-engine framework and removed the numbers from anything the skill recommends. The source is a ghostwriting agency selling a service.
  • [Likely] The team-culture roadmap is sound leadership practice but has no evidence base shown and promises direction, not culture, by day 90 (it says so itself). The risk it omits: after layoffs or a merger, distrust can be a rational response, and consultation law may apply. I added a 30-60-90 sequence, measures, and an acknowledge-first step.
  • [Certain] The GTM table was written by the CEO of an outreach-tool vendor, so its tool column includes her own product. The strategy-versus-engineering split is useful; "enrich lookalike accounts from call transcripts" raises data-protection questions the graphic never mentions, and "anyone can rebuild your table" means the moat is proof and offer, not the stack.
  • [Certain] The 48-item "Claude marketing department" is a screenshot of a table of contents. Only titles are visible; the files, author and licence are not. I catalogued the 48 titles and mapped them to existing skills (claude-code-marketing-team already has 50 roles in 10 departments, claude-marketing-jobs has 50 jobs) rather than inventing their contents. Two titles ("Muse MCP Server", and a named scorer in "Post Triage") are unexplained and are marked as such. "Comment CMO" is a lead-magnet hook. The sensible part is the "rollout ladder": read-only first, then capped spend.

3. Where I disagree with the source material

  • I disagree with quoting "-86.4%" as the new reality for AI citations. The risk is rebuilding a content strategy on a three-week window its own source calls provisional. Track your own prompt set monthly and watch whether the shift persists.
  • I disagree with presenting survey percentages with no base as if they were benchmarks. The risk is allocating weekly time on the strength of numbers nobody can check. Set a split by stage and review it against meetings booked.
  • I disagree with "48 skills, agents and playbooks" as a reason to install anything. The risk is giving agents write access to ad accounts from a pack you have not read. Read every file, start read-only, and set the spend cap in the ad platform itself.

4. Not done, and why

  • No repositories were named, so nothing was cloned or installed and there is no install script.
  • No template for the 48-item explorer: file-explorer-system-card already has that shape.
  • The contents of the 48 items were not recreated; only titles were visible.
