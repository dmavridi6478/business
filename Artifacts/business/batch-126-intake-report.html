Batch 126 Intake Report

Two inline infographics and one 60-second TikTok video processed with the seven-step intake. Result: three new skills, three commands, one design template, two themes, three workflows, thirteen prompts and a read-only install script. Nothing was installed.

1. Executive findings

  • [Certain] The Cowork cheat sheet's best claim is partly wrong in a way that will bite. It says /schedule "runs in the cloud, so the list is ready before your forecast call". Anthropic's help pages say new scheduled tasks do run in the cloud with no device online, but a task that needs files or apps on your computer runs on your computer and needs the desktop app open, and its own example prompt 4 reads a local /calls folder. The skill tells you to build the Monday sweep from connectors only.
  • [Certain] The video is a lead-magnet TikTok ("comment VIDEO and I'll send you"). I could read its on-screen captions and names but not its audio, since no speech-to-text was available. Everything in the video skill therefore comes from the captions plus the GitHub pages for each tool, checked on 1 November 2026.
  • [Certain] The riskiest tool is the one most easily missed: OneTake (feitangyuan/onetake, 3,444 stars, created 26 September 2026, one contributor). GitHub shows its licence as "Other" and a third-party listing says PolyForm Noncommercial. If you use it for client or company videos you may be in breach; read the LICENSE file first.
  • [Certain] Collage B-Roll (MIT) is not "Claude making video": it generates video with Google's Gemini Omni Flash and bills your Gemini API per clip. Claude Animation Base (MIT, 2,217 stars against 1.6k in the video) and Lemo-Opuscar (code MIT, demo films CC BY 4.0, three narration tracks excluded) are cheaper on cash and expensive on tokens; the author of Lemo-Opuscar says a film takes an agent 30 to 60 minutes.
  • [Certain] Lemo-Opuscar's style count is inconsistent between sources: 51 in the video, 59 in the README, and 66 and 98 on its site. I quote none as settled.
  • [Likely] The four-levels pyramid is textbook and fine as a map, but it pictures strategy as top-down and deliberate while its own quotation (Mintzberg: "strategy is a pattern in a stream of decisions") points at emergent strategy. I added a six-point cascade check and the limits of the picture.
  • [Certain] Duplicates: HyperFrames is already a skill in this library, so it is referenced, not rebuilt. The Cowork sheet comes from the same publisher as the Batch 120 sheet but covers different jobs, so it is a separate skill. No existing skill covered the four levels, the Cowork eight jobs, or these four video repos.

3. Where I disagree with the source material

  • I disagree with "the list is ready before your forecast call" as a promise. The risk is a Monday that silently does not run, or runs late, because the task depended on a local folder. Use connectors and check the first three Mondays.
  • I disagree with installing a video skill because it is trending. The risk is a licence you cannot use commercially, a paid API you did not notice, or hours of tokens. Read the licence, cost and requirements first.
  • I disagree with treating the strategy pyramid as how strategy gets made. The risk is a plan the front line cannot see itself in. Ask what the front line already does that nobody decided.
