5. Prompts to copy and paste

Plain text. Replace each bracket with real facts; the skills tell Claude to ask once rather than invent them.

Huge document: what matters to me 
I've been sent this document. Before summarising everything, tell me the 10 things that matter most to ME. My reason for reading it is: [YOUR GOAL] For every important point, tell me where in the document you found it.
Huge document: what is easy to miss 
Search this entire document for details someone skimming it could easily miss. Look specifically for: DEADLINES / FEES OR COSTS / EXCEPTIONS / RESTRICTIONS / AUTOMATIC RENEWALS / CANCELLATION TERMS / IMPORTANT SMALL PRINT
Huge document: what could affect me 
My situation is: [EXPLAIN SITUATION] Read the document specifically from MY perspective. Which sections could affect me? Explain why each matters and quote or reference the relevant section so I can check it myself.
Huge document: questions before agreeing 
After reading this document, give me the 10 questions I should ask BEFORE agreeing to or acting on it. Prioritise anything that is unclear, ambiguous, missing or could have important consequences later.
Huge document: challenge it 
Read this critically rather than simply summarising it. Identify: CLAIMS WITHOUT CLEAR EVIDENCE / VAGUE WORDING / IMPORTANT ASSUMPTIONS / POTENTIAL CONTRADICTIONS / QUESTIONS THE DOCUMENT DOESN'T ANSWER. Show me exactly where each issue appears.
After any summary 
What important detail could I misunderstand, lose or completely miss if I relied ONLY on your summary and didn't read the original?
Content week: build the week 
Core idea: [IDEA]. Audience: [AUDIENCE]. Proof/examples: [PROOF]. Build a 7-day content sequence where each day has a different job: problem → misconception → how-to → example → mistake → checklist → practical challenge. Give each day a hook and useful takeaway.
Content week: add the progression 
Now rewrite the sequence so no two posts teach the same point. Add one proof element to each post where possible: example, calculation, screenshot idea, mini case, demonstration or source.

Write a professional message that clearly documents my contribution, thanks the team, and connects my role to the outcome without sounding defensive or possessive: [paste contribution and context]
Workplace: /disagree 
Write a respectful response that acknowledges this idea, explains my concern using facts, and proposes a practical alternative. Make me sound collaborative, not defensive: [paste idea and concern]
Workplace: /clarify
