What you should know first

[Certain] Most of this upload is a list of things to install, and the strongest claims in it are the weakest evidence. The CodeGraph "-70% tokens" chart is labelled "Illustrative" on the slide itself. The "Anthropic lead engineer" prompt names no engineer. The replace.so star counts (Rea 266,353; Photocraft 62,967) do not match any other source I could find (about 234,000 and 27,900 in separate write-ups). Treat every star figure as unverified.

[Certain] The "save $5,508 a year" headline is true only for all twelve swaps. The summary slide lists eight swaps that add to $5,256. The "$0" also leaves out hosting, backups, patching and your time, so the real saving is lower, and for a $36 or $72 tool it is probably negative.

[Certain] Several cards promote real risk. AutoHedge, Vibe Trading and Fincept Terminal involve trading or market decisions with real money. Open Higgsfield AI advertises "no content filters". reclip downloads video from sites whose terms may forbid it. Camofox is sold as a way to fool bot detectors. REA reverse-engineers software. None of these were installed.

[Certain] Two files are not what their names say. "Deploy AI agents.gif" is an LLM tools logo map with no deployment steps, so no agent was built from it. "The Ultimate Excel Data Cleaning Cheat Sheet" actually contains a Conditional Formatting sheet; the data cleaning sheet was not supplied.

[Likely] The Craft apps and Fincept Terminal are young projects. Secondary write-ups describe the Craft apps as early software and raise questions about AI-generated code quality. Read each repo's issues before depending on it.

Deeper version, not bigger (from the slide)
I want a deeper version of this task, not a bigger one. Before rewriting it: (1) say what a top professional in this field would do here that I'm not asking for; (2) name the rawest material they would start from instead of what has already been digested for me; (3) name the specific methods and tools they would use; (4) say what, other than you or me, would show the result is right. Then rewrite my prompt to ask for exactly that.

My task: [paste]
01 Executive compression
Turn the document below into a one-page decision memo for [decision maker]. State the decision needed, the recommendation, the three facts that drive it, the main risk and what happens if we do nothing. Do not add facts that are not in the document.

Document: [paste]
02 Audience translation
Rewrite the idea below three times: for an executive (outcome and cost), for a technical reader (how it works and constraints) and for a general reader (plain language, no jargon). Keep the facts identical in all three.

Idea: [paste]
03 Argument strengthening
Read my argument below. List its premises, name the weakest one and explain why, say what evidence would support it, then suggest a stronger version. Do not flatter the argument.

Argument: [paste]
04 Voice consistency
Here is my voice guide: [paste]. Compare the draft below against it on tone, style, vocabulary and consistency. Quote each place it drifts and give a fix.

Draft: [paste]
05 Objection anticipation
For the proposal below, list what a skeptical reader could doubt, what evidence they will want, what alternative they might suggest, and how to address each upfront.

Proposal: [paste]
13-phase project plan
Plan this project using a 13-phase road map: initiation, planning, scheduling, resources, risk, procurement, stakeholders, quality, execution, cost, change, monitoring and controlling, closure. Mark each phase Needed, Light or Skip with a one-line reason for a project of this size, list the deliverables to produce, and flag phases that run in parallel. Do not invent dates, costs or names.

Project: [goal, deadline, budget range, team size]
Excel rule or pivot pick
I have an Excel sheet with data in [describe columns and where the header sits]. I want to [goal]. Tell me whether to use a conditional formatting rule or a PivotTable feature, the exact menu path, the formula with correct $ anchoring, the range to apply it to, and one pitfall. Use the simplest rule that works.
Test a token-saving claim
Help me measure whether a code-indexing tool really saves tokens. Give me a test plan: one repeated task, three fresh-session runs without the tool and three with it, which token figures to record, how to compare medians, and how to check that answer quality did not drop.
