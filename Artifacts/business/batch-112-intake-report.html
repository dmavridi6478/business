Two archives (84 images, 2 videos) turned into repository assets. Prepared as an advisory report: findings first, then evidence, then what was built.

1. Executive findings

  • The repository is already past the point where more skills help. It held 1,239 skills and 734 commands before this batch; it now holds 1,248 and 743. [Certain] Each addition is a description every session pays to load. Of the nine skills added, only three (oss-swap-part-17, oss-swap-part-12, ai-side-hustle-pilot) carry material no existing skill held; the rest are thin indexes. The better move for the next batch is to merge, not add.
  • Three swaps are 75 percent of the "$8,436" claim. RudderStack, Chromatic and Archbee account for $6,288 of $8,436. [Certain] Replacing the other nine saves $2,148 a year and creates nine things to patch. The headline is a sum of list prices, not a measured saving.
  • Two licences need a human read. Graylog and Arize Phoenix show "Other" on GitHub; os-taxonomy is ODbL (share-alike data). [Certain] Do not use them commercially on the strength of a carousel.
  • "Free" has costs the cards omit. Hosting, backups, patching and your hours. [Likely] For a solo business the only sensible swaps are the low-ops ones (Playwright, VitePress, BackstopJS, mitmproxy, GitHub Desktop, Ghostty).
  • The side-hustle series is a decent filter and a thin business case. The five questions are useful; none of the slides supports any income figure, and the slides themselves warn that product features and pricing change. [Certain]
