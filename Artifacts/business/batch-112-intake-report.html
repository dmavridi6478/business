What you should know first

[Certain] The LinkedIn graphics sell a system; none of them proves it works. Lara Acosta's "$10k" figure, the "1-5% Diehards" share and Josh Sanders' audience-growth example are the authors' own claims, with no data behind them in the images. The Lara Acosta graphic also repeats one sentence for formats 1 and 2, so the system as drawn has three distinct formats, not four. Use MAPS and the 5 P's as drafting aids, then measure your own posts.

[Certain] The "75 websites" list is about 66 services, and it was written for an Indian audience. Pramp, Interviewing.io, Big Interview and Exponent each appear twice, Coursera twice and Glassdoor three times; AngelList is now Wellfound. A longer list is not a better list: pick one site per job (search, resume, interview, salary) and stop.

[Certain] "Free" is carrying a lot of weight in the tool cards. Sentry is source-available under the Functional Source License, not open source. Upscayl, Immich and VoiceStudio are AGPL-3.0. VoiceStudio's model weights and voices have separate terms. Immich is not a backup by itself: its README asks for a 3-2-1 plan.

[Likely] Whisper is not a drop-in replacement for Otter. It is a command-line transcriber: no meeting bot, no speaker labels, no live notes. The slide's own footnote admits the interface shown is illustrative.

[Certain] AnyPS5 was read and not installed. It is a PlayStation 5 compatibility layer (GPL-2.0 only) with an unclear legal position, and its trending rank says nothing about that.

[Guessing] The free-LLM provider list is probably still useful, but I could not verify it. The repository it credits (cheahjs/free-llm-api-resources) was unreachable from this session, so no rate limits are quoted. Free tiers change often; read each provider's page on the day you use it.

6. Prompts in plain text

Square brackets are your inputs. The MAPS, 5 P's, lookup, job-site and app-launch prompts were written by me from the graphics' steps; the sales-call steps follow the Lara Acosta bonus box closely. They are not the authors' own wording.

MAPS positioning coach
Act as my LinkedIn positioning coach. Use the MAPS framework.

Ask me one question at a time, in this order, and wait for my answer each time:
1. Market: who exactly would pay me tomorrow if I messaged them today, and what problem would they happily spend money fixing?
2. Achievements: which 3 results can I put a number on and prove?
3. Process: what 3 to 5 steps do I repeat every time to get my clients that result?
4. Stance: which popular advice in my field do I disagree with, and what result or story backs me up?

Then write my positioning line in this form:
"I help [market] get [result] through [process], after [achievement]."
Give 3 versions, then rewrite my current headline and the first two lines of my About section to match. Flag any claim I cannot evidence.

One post on the 5 P's
Write one LinkedIn post using the 5 P's, in this order:
Problem: name the problem I solve and explain it better than my audience can.
Promise: show the way out.
Proof: one real result with the story behind it.
Personality: why me.
Perspective: what my industry gets wrong.

Write so that someone who has never heard of me can follow it and someone who knows my field still learns something. Keep it under 1,300 characters, end with a soft call to action, and do not invent any result: use only the proof I paste below.

Mine a sales call for content
Here is a call transcript with names and company details removed and with the client's permission.

2. Turn each objection into a content idea.
3. For each idea, suggest which of these formats fits: celebrate a case study, lead magnet, before and after, two-way promo.
4. Leave a blank where my own experience solving it should go; do not make one up.

App launch check
Review my app project against the six launch checks and report only what is missing, with the evidence for each:
1. One complete flow works end to end on a real phone and the saved data is still there after closing the app.
2. Three testers can finish one task without help on the second build.
3. A second account cannot read or edit the first account's records.
4. A three-step funnel is tracked with real counts and the biggest drop is named.
5. A crash was reproduced, fixed, and the task repeated on the affected version and device.
6. Purchase, expiry and restore each grant or remove access correctly.
