6. Prompts in plain text

Square brackets are your inputs. The five premortem steps are one block; run them in order and push back between steps.

Premortem (all five steps)
I want to run a premortem on this plan before I commit to it.

My plan: [describe it in full, with timeline, budget and what success looks like]

Step 1 — THE PREMORTEM
You are a forensic failure analyst. It is 6 months from today and this plan has failed completely. Write the autopsy. Give me the 7 most likely causes of death, ranked. For each one: what killed it, how it unfolded month by month, the assumption I held which let it happen, and the first warning sign I would have seen. Tie every failure to a detail I gave you, or drop it. No generic advice. Never reassure me.

Step 2 — THE VERDICT
Of those 7 failures, tell me which one is most likely and which one is most dangerous, and explain why they are not the same. Then name the single biggest hidden assumption I am making without realising it is an assumption. Be blunt. If my plan has a fatal flaw, say it in plain words.

Step 3 — THE REBUILD
Rewrite my plan with every one of those failure modes closed off. Show me what changed and why. Then give me a pre-launch checklist of 3 to 5 things I must verify before I act, and the result on each which should make me walk away entirely.

Step 4 — THE ADVERSARY
Now play the person who gains most if my plan fails: a competitor, a rival, someone who wants my spot. You have seen my plan. Tell me exactly where you would attack it, what you would do the week I launch, and the move I would never see coming.

Step 5 — THE TRIPWIRE
For each failure mode, give me one measurable signal that would tell me it's starting to happen, and the exact week I should check for it. I want tripwires, not vibes.
First impression - prompt to save
Act as my personal communication coach. I'm about to meet [PERSON / GROUP] in [SITUATION]. Ask me 5 questions ONE AT A TIME about the meeting, my background and what I want to achieve. Then help me prepare: a natural introduction, 3 good conversation starters, 2 interesting things to share, 3 mistakes to avoid. Keep everything authentic to my personality.
Prompt 01 - Nail your introduction
I need to introduce myself at: [EVENT / MEETING / NEW JOB]. Here's some background about me: [YOUR BACKGROUND]. Write 3 natural introductions: 10 seconds, 30 seconds, 60 seconds. Make me sound confident, approachable and interesting without sounding rehearsed or self-important.
Prompt 02 - Never run out of things to say
I'm attending [EVENT] where I'll meet [TYPE OF PEOPLE]. Give me 10 interesting conversation starters that feel natural rather than forced. Avoid boring questions like 'What do you do?' Include follow-up questions that encourage people to share stories and experiences.
Prompt 03 - Sound confident without showing off
I want to talk about my experience with: [SKILL / ACHIEVEMENT / PROJECT]. Here's how I'd normally describe it: [YOUR WORDS]. Rewrite it so I sound confident and capable without bragging. Keep it conversational, specific and believable. Give me 3 versions for different situations.
Prompt 04 - Handle questions you weren't expecting
I'm preparing for [SITUATION]. Act as someone I'm meeting for the first time. Ask me 5 challenging but realistic questions ONE AT A TIME. After each answer, give me feedback on how I came across. Help me improve my clarity, confidence and ability to respond naturally.
Prompt 05 - Make people remember you
I'm meeting [PERSON / GROUP] for the first time. My goal is [GOAL]. Help me identify 3 memorable things I could communicate about myself. Focus on: relevant experiences, interesting stories, shared interests, genuine strengths. Avoid gimmicks, exaggeration and anything that sounds like a sales pitch.
LinkedIn 1. Authority Content Strategy
Act as an industry expert. Create content that positions you as the go-to authority in [your niche]. Share client wins, behind-the-scenes insights, and contrarian takes. Post 5-7 times per week with strategic messaging that builds trust. Include specific results and case studies. Focus on problems your ICP faces daily. Use formats like "Here's what I learned after..." or "Most founders get this wrong..." Keep posts under 1,300 characters for maximum engagement.
LinkedIn 2. Strategic Network Building
You're targeting decision makers. Connect with 20-30 ideal prospects daily using LinkedIn Sales Navigator. Filter by company size, role, and industry. Send personalized connection requests mentioning something specific from their profile or recent activity. Avoid generic messages. Build relationships before pitching. Focus on quality over quantity. Track connection acceptance rates and optimize your approach based on what works.
LinkedIn 3. Lead Magnet Distribution
Act as a value provider. Create high-value resources your ICP actually wants. Share frameworks, templates, or case studies through LinkedIn posts. Use clear CTAs like "Comment 'FRAMEWORK' for the template." Gate valuable content behind email capture. Promote lead magnets 3-4 times per week. Test different formats: PDFs, video walkthroughs, or exclusive communities. Track conversion rates and double down on what performs best.
LinkedIn 4. Engagement Amplification
You're building social proof. Engage meaningfully on your ideal prospects' content within 30 minutes of them posting. Leave thoughtful comments that add value, not generic praise. Share insights from your experience. Ask intelligent questions that spark discussion. This puts you on their radar consistently. Spend 15-20 minutes daily engaging strategically. Focus on posts from your target audience, not random content.
LinkedIn 5. Direct Message Sequences
Act as a relationship builder. Send personalized DMs to connections who engage with your content or visit your profile. Start conversations, don't pitch immediately. Reference their recent posts or company updates. Use the formula: compliment + question + value offer. Keep initial messages under 50 words. Follow up consistently but not aggressively. Track response rates and refine your messaging based on what generates replies.
LinkedIn 6. Profile Optimization System
You're creating a conversion machine. Optimize your LinkedIn profile as a landing page for your ideal clients. Use a professional headshot, compelling headline that states your value proposition, and a summary that speaks directly to your ICP's pain points. Include social proof, client results, and a clear CTA. Update your featured section with case studies and testimonials. Make it easy for prospects to understand how you help them.
LinkedIn 7. Content Repurposing Engine
Act as a content multiplier. Turn one piece of successful content into 10 different formats. Transform client case studies into posts, carousels, videos, and stories. Repurpose webinar content into LinkedIn articles. Break down long-form content into bite-sized posts. Use different angles for the same core message. This maximizes your content ROI and ensures consistent posting without constant creation. Track which formats perform best for your audience.
LinkedIn 8. Social Proof Amplification
You're building credibility at scale. Systematically collect and showcase client testimonials, case studies, and results. Create posts highlighting specific client wins with permission. Share screenshots of positive feedback and measurable outcomes. Use video testimonials when possible. Feature client logos and success metrics. This builds trust with prospects who see others achieving results. Update social proof regularly to keep content fresh and relevant.
LinkedIn 9. Conversion Tracking System
Act as a data-driven marketer. Track every lead source, engagement metric, and conversion point. Use UTM parameters for links, monitor profile views, and measure content performance. Identify which posts generate the most leads and double down on similar content. Track the customer journey from first touch to closed deal. Use this data to optimize your strategy continuously. Set up monthly reviews to analyze what's working and eliminate what isn't.
