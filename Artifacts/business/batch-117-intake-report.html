38 images in a zip plus three inline infographics processed with the seven-step intake. Result: three new skills, three references, three commands, three merges, three templates, three themes, four workflows.

1. Executive findings

  • [Certain] The McKinsey graphics are one third party's summaries, not McKinsey's own material. Their statistics and "real cases" (for example the Influence Model's "8 times more likely" and the Consumer Decision Journey's "20,000 consumers") are not verified here. I kept the frameworks and recomputed the worked arithmetic, which holds; I flagged the numbers as the cards' claims.
  • [Certain] Ten frameworks do not need ten skills. One picker skill (mckinsey-frameworks-10) with a reference file and one command covers them, and it points to the existing mckinsey-7s-model instead of duplicating it. Adding ten skills would have made routing worse.
  • [Certain] The six digital-product prompts are generic, and the real value is steps 5 and 6 (your voice, your proof). The graphic ends with an advertisement for allen.store; I recorded it as a promotion and built nothing on it.
  • [Likely] The Make-to-n8n method is sound as a mapping aid but the cards oversell it: credentials do not transfer, webhook URLs change, expressions differ, and some Make modules have no n8n node. The skill makes the mapping table a gate before any JSON.
  • [Certain] Steg Cloak is unverified (I could not locate the repo) and hides data in text with invisible characters, which is dual-use. It is recorded as unverified with a warning, not installed.
  • [Certain] Cobalt, Sunshine, RustDesk and CopyQ carry real risks (terms-of-service and copyright, open network ports, a remote-control surface, and a clipboard history that stores passwords). Each carries a care note in oss-alternatives.
  • [Certain] The eight "skills for 2027" video picks are unverified, add up to about 19 hours, and are no substitute for a project. They are recorded as pointers with the library skills that already cover each skill.

4. Where I disagree with the source material

  • I disagree that ten framework graphics each need a skill or that the statistics on them can be quoted as McKinsey findings. The risk in quoting them is repeating a figure no one can source. I built one picker and marked every number as the card's claim.
  • I disagree with "Let Claude do the migration" as a full answer. The risk is a workflow that imports cleanly and fails silently on credentials, expressions or error paths. I made the mapping table and a parallel run mandatory.
  • I disagree that a model drafts a product worth buying. The risk is a course that is interchangeable with every other one. The skill adds a five-buyer test before drafting and puts most effort into your own voice and proof.
