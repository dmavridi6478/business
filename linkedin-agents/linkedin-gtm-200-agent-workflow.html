1. Assessment before you build

Read this first. It changes what you deploy and what you expect from it.

“Copy, paste, run” is the weakest claim on the infographic.

CertainEach agent is only as good as the context it receives. Pasted into an empty chat, 200 agents return generic copy and invented specifics. The workflow below builds one GTM Context Pack first, and every prompt depends on it. Treat setup as a half-day job, not a paste.

Roughly half of this list is not LinkedIn work.

CertainCategory 05 (Retention & Expansion), most of 04 (Sales & Conversion) and category 10 (Pipeline Ops & Analytics) run on call notes, usage data, support tickets, survey results and your CRM. Claude does not see any of those unless you paste exports or connect tools. The LinkedIn-native core is categories 02, 06, 07, 08 and 09, plus the outreach half of 03. Build that first.

All 200 names are distinct, but many jobs overlap, and you need about a dozen.

LikelyFour Sales Navigator agents (Account Finder, Search Builder, List Builder, Lead List Organiser) cover one workflow. Lead magnets have four agents, comments have four, case studies three, referrals three, profile optimisation four. A founder or small team should start with the 12 marked Core and add an agent only when a stage in the weekly review is the biggest drop.

Several agents promise data LinkedIn does not hand over.

LikelyThe Competitor Follower Analyst, Hiring Spike Detector, Funding Announcement Responder and Profile Viewer Analyst assume bulk data about other people’s audiences or activity. LinkedIn does not provide a competitor’s follower list to outsiders. These prompts work only from what you can legitimately see and paste, and each one says so. They do not scrape, and they do not log in anywhere.

The Account Safety & Limits Monitor cannot know your real limits.

CertainIt has no access to your account, and LinkedIn does not publish exact limits. The agent compares your own activity log with a send budget you set, and it flags spikes, falling acceptance and warnings you report. Set the budget low and raise it slowly. Run this agent before every outreach session.

Tracking individuals’ job changes and promotions needs a documented basis.

LikelyUnder GDPR, systematically watching named people’s life events to trigger sales messages needs a recorded legitimate-interest assessment, a clear opt-out and data you minimise. This applies to the Job Change Tracker, New Hire Signal Detector, Promotion Trigger Agent and Warm Lead Ranker. If you sell into the EU, get advice from a data-protection professional before you scale these.

Four agents sit near legal or financial advice and are limited accordingly.

CertainThe Contract Review Assistant, Procurement Guidance Agent, Pricing Strategy Advisor and Revenue Forecast Agent recommend and flag. They do not give legal advice, and a forecast is only as good as the CRM data you supply. A person decides and a qualified professional handles contracts.

Small points about the source image.

CertainCategory 09’s list is numbered 17, 19, 19, 20 on the image; it contains 20 names, which I read in order. The “comment GTM for free access” mechanic is itself engagement bait, which LinkedIn discourages. I did not see the original prompts, so every prompt here is written from scratch to one standard.

6. Agent library: 200 prompts

Every prompt uses one template: role, context, mission, inputs, process, output format, rules. Choose a category, then copy a prompt, the whole category or a subagent file. Typing in the search box searches all 200.

7. Governance

  • Draft only. No agent posts, sends, connects, scrapes, logs in or writes to your CRM. You perform every action.
  • Send budget. One account-wide daily cap in the Context Pack. Start well below what you think is safe and raise it slowly. Every outbound agent counts against it.
  • Lawful basis. For prospects in the EU, record source, purpose, a legitimate-interest assessment and an opt-out route before outreach. Delete on request.
  • No pods and no bait. No engagement pods, no “comment to receive” mechanics, no automated reactions.
  • Fact gate. You check every number, quote and case study against a source before publishing. Named customers need written permission.
  • Advice limits. Contract, procurement, pricing and forecast agents recommend. A person decides, and a qualified professional handles legal questions.

Set targets from your own week-1 baseline. The measures that matter are connection acceptance rate, DM reply rate, call booking rate, show rate and close rate, then qualified conversations and meetings booked per week. Impressions and follower count are secondary.

The risk in this plan: 200 agents invite you to optimise every stage at once. Fix the single biggest drop in the funnel each week, and leave the rest alone.
