# Batch 104 prompt pack (plain text, copy and paste)

## 9 Claude commands (@the.wealth.lab)
1. Before you answer, ask me the most important questions you need to give me the best possible result.
2. Red-team this idea. Identify the weaknesses, risks, blind spots, and assumptions. Then suggest how to strengthen it.
3. Compare these options in a table. Include the pros, cons, risks, and your recommendation based on my goal.
4. Give me 3 strong, realistic examples so I can clearly see what a good result looks like.
5. Approach this like a top [FIELD] expert with 20 years of experience. Give me practical, strategic advice and explain your reasoning.
6. Explain this in simple, plain language, as if you were teaching a 12-year-old. Use one clear example.
7. Rewrite this to sound sharper, clearer, and more persuasive while keeping my original meaning and voice.
8. Keep going until the task is fully complete. Review your work, fix any gaps, and don't stop after the first pass.
9. Analyze this project or process and identify the biggest bottleneck. Explain why it's limiting progress and give me the simplest high-impact solution.

## 6 everyday prompts (@stackfloai)
1. Summarise this into the 5 things I actually need to know. Remove repetition and background information. End with any actions I need to take.
2. Write a reply to this email. Keep it professional but natural. Be concise and avoid corporate language. Ask me for any missing information first.
3. Turn these notes into a clear action plan. Separate: What needs doing / Who needs to do it / Priority / Next step.
4. I need to understand [TOPIC]. Give me the 20% of information that covers 80% of it. Explain it simply, then tell me what to research next.
5. Critique this draft before rewriting it. Tell me: What's unclear / What's unnecessary / What's missing / What could be stronger. Then rewrite it.
6. Before answering, ask me the questions you need to give me the best possible result.

## Spreadsheet to management brief (@earchoe)
A. Analyse this spreadsheet for the question: [BUSINESS QUESTION]. First describe the columns and identify missing, duplicated or suspicious values. Then calculate the metrics that answer the question. Show the formulas or logic used. Do not silently change the data.
B. Create a one-page management brief: headline finding -> supporting numbers -> trend or comparison -> possible explanations -> risks -> recommended next checks. Label correlation as correlation. Do not claim causation unless the data supports it.
C. (second pass) What did you assume? What information is missing that would materially improve the result? Attack your own answer: find weak claims, contradictions and edge cases. I will verify facts, figures, policies, prices and claims.
