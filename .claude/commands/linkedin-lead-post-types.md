## THE 5 POST TYPES

### 1. Storytelling Post
**What it gets you:** Emotional connection. Readers remember stories, not facts. This post makes people feel something and want to follow you.

**The structure:**
1. Name a problem (the hook — one line, stops the scroll)
2. Day-to-day feeling (what it was like living with that problem)
3. Frustration (the moment it became unbearable)
4. Lesson (what you discovered or decided)
5. Life after (the contrast — what changed)

**Rules:**
- Hook is everything — write 5 versions and pick the sharpest
- Be specific: real numbers, real dates, real feelings
- End with a question or invitation that invites replies
- No corporate language — write how you talk

**Prompt:**
```
I want to write a Storytelling Post on LinkedIn about: [YOUR STORY/EXPERIENCE].
My audience: [WHO READS YOUR CONTENT]
My offer or expertise: [WHAT YOU SELL OR DO]

Write the post using this structure:
1. Hook (1 line — names the problem, stops the scroll)
2. Day-to-day feeling (2–3 lines — what it felt like)
3. Frustration (1–2 lines — the breaking point)
4. Lesson (2–3 lines — the insight or decision)
5. Life after (1–2 lines — what changed)
6. Closing question or CTA (1 line)

Write 2 versions: one more vulnerable, one more professional. Keep each under 200 words.
```

### 2. Framework Share
**What it gets you:** Authority. When you give a usable process, readers save your post, share it, and think of you as an expert. This builds the "smart person to follow" reputation.

**The structure:**
1. Pick a process you use (something you do that others don't know how to)
2. Break it into clear steps (3–7 steps works best)
3. Stack frameworks (name the tools or concepts inside each step)
4. Hold nothing back (give the real thing — generosity builds trust)

**Rules:**
- The more useful, the more it gets shared
- Name each step clearly — people need to be able to screenshot it
- Avoid vague language like "be strategic" — give the actual tactic
- End with "Save this for when you need it" or a use-case reminder

**Prompt:**
```
I want to write a Framework Share post on LinkedIn.
My process/framework: [DESCRIBE THE PROCESS OR SYSTEM YOU USE]
My audience: [WHO NEEDS THIS]
My niche: [YOUR TOPIC/INDUSTRY]

Write the post:
1. Hook (1 line — names what the framework helps with)
2. Brief context (1–2 lines — why most people struggle with this)
3. The framework steps (numbered, each step named and explained in 1–2 lines)
4. Closing line (save this / when to use it)

Also give me 3 hook variations to test.
```

### 3. Contrarian Take
**What it gets you:** Debate, reach, and authority. Disagreeing with accepted wisdom triggers comments — and LinkedIn's algorithm loves comments. Done well, it positions you as someone who thinks independently.

**The structure:**
1. State your position (the hook — contradicts something widely believed)
2. Accepted wisdom (acknowledge what everyone else says)
3. Proof (your evidence, data, or experience that contradicts it)
4. Unspoken truth (the deeper reason why the accepted wisdom is wrong or incomplete)

**The rule you must follow:**
- AVOID controversy for its own sake. Contrarian ≠ inflammatory.
- Your take must be grounded in genuine experience or evidence
- You must be willing to defend it in the comments
- Pick battles that matter to your audience — not politics, not personal attacks

**Prompt:**
```
I want to write a Contrarian Take post on LinkedIn.
The accepted wisdom I'm challenging: [WHAT MOST PEOPLE BELIEVE IN YOUR INDUSTRY]
My actual position: [YOUR TAKE]
My evidence: [EXPERIENCE, DATA, OR EXAMPLES THAT SUPPORT YOUR VIEW]
My audience: [WHO READS YOUR CONTENT]

Write the post:
1. Hook (1 line — states the contrarian position sharply)
2. Acknowledge accepted wisdom (1–2 lines)
3. Proof (2–3 lines — specific, not vague)
4. Unspoken truth (2–3 lines — the deeper insight)
5. Closing (question or invitation to debate)

Check: Is this genuinely useful to my audience, or just edgy? Revise if it's the latter.
```

### 4. Case Study
**What it gets you:** Proof. A case study converts followers into leads because it shows — not tells — that you get results. It's the post type closest to a sales conversation.

**The structure:**
1. Starting numbers (where the client was before — be specific)
2. Process (what you actually did — enough detail to be credible)
3. Outcome with figures (the results — real numbers, not vague wins)
4. Tag the client (if they consent — adds credibility and reach)
5. Add a quote (their words > your words)

**Rules:**
- Always lead with the transformation, not the features
- "We grew revenue by 40% in 90 days" beats "We used our proprietary framework"
- Get client permission before posting
- End with a soft CTA: "DM me if you're facing something similar"

**Prompt:**
```
I want to write a Case Study post on LinkedIn.
Client situation before: [STARTING NUMBERS AND CONTEXT]
What we did: [THE PROCESS OR INTERVENTION — be specific]
Results: [THE OUTCOME IN REAL NUMBERS]
Client quote (if available): [QUOTE]
My offer: [WHAT I SELL OR DO]

Write the post:
1. Hook (1 line — leads with the transformation/result)
2. Starting situation (2–3 lines — where they were)
3. Process (3–4 lines — what we actually did)
4. Outcome (2–3 lines — specific numbers)
5. Client quote (if available)
6. Soft CTA (1 line — DM, comment, or link)

Keep it under 250 words. Make the numbers the star.
```

### 5. Direct Offer
**What it gets you:** Inbound leads. When done after building trust, a direct offer post converts followers who are already warm. The key: earn the right first, then ask plainly.

**The structure:**
1. Earn the right first (reference the value you've given: "I've shared X posts about Y…")
2. Announce explicitly (say clearly what you're offering — no buried CTAs)
3. One clear CTA (one action only — comment, DM, or link — never multiple)
4. Frequency: ~1 in every 4–5 posts

**Rules:**
- Never lead with the offer — always lead with the earned credibility
- Be direct, not pushy: "I'm opening 3 spots" > "BUY NOW"
- Only one CTA per post — multiple options reduce action
- Post this after a high-performing value post, not randomly

**Prompt:**
```
I want to write a Direct Offer post on LinkedIn.
My offer: [WHAT YOU'RE SELLING, OFFERING, OR OPENING UP]
Who it's for: [IDEAL CLIENT DESCRIPTION]
The value I've already given (recent posts or context): [REFERENCE YOUR RECENT CONTENT]
Availability/scarcity (if relevant): [SPOTS, DEADLINE, COHORT]
CTA: [THE ONE ACTION YOU WANT — DM, COMMENT, LINK]

Write the post:
1. Earn the right (2–3 lines — reference the value you've given)
2. Announce the offer (1–2 lines — direct and clear)
3. Who it's for (1–2 lines — qualifying language)
4. What they get (2–3 bullet points — specific outcomes)
5. One CTA (1 line — exactly one action)

Keep it under 150 words. No corporate language. Direct but not pushy.
```
