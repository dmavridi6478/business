## 1. HERO'S JOURNEY
**Use when:** Pitching yourself in an interview or telling your founder story.

**Structure (12 steps):**
1. Ordinary World — your life before the problem
2. Call to Adventure — the opportunity or challenge appears
3. Refuse the Call — initial hesitation or resistance
4. Meet the Mentor — who or what gave you the insight/tool
5. Cross the First Threshold — you commit and take the first step
6. Tests, Allies, Enemies — obstacles you faced along the way
7. Innermost Cave — the hardest moment, the deepest doubt
8. Ordeal — the crisis point, the real test
9. Reward — what you gained from surviving the ordeal
10. The Road Back — applying the lesson in the real world
11. Resurrection — the final challenge that proves the transformation
12. Return with the Elixir — you come back changed, with something to offer

## 2. THE GOLDEN CIRCLE
**Use when:** Explaining your company to someone who doesn't get why it matters.

**Structure:**
- **WHY** — your purpose: why you exist, what you believe
- **HOW** — your process: how you do it differently
- **WHAT** — exactly what you do, plus success stories

*Start with WHY. Most people start with WHAT and never get to the emotional core.*

---

## 3. THE MOUNTAIN STRUCTURE
**Use when:** Telling a story at dinner or giving a talk where there is a big payoff moment.

**Structure:**
1. **Background** — set the scene, who are you, what's the context
2. **Rising Action** — the consequences of the problem building up
3. **Climax** — when the consequence really hurts (the big moment)
4. **Falling Action** — the realization, the decision to change course
5. **Resolution** — the ultimate solution or offer

---

## 4. PIXAR'S STORY FRAMEWORK
**Use when:** Trying to make someone care before you ask for something.

**Structure:**
- Once upon a time… (the beginning — who the character is)
- And every day… (the familiar situation — life before the problem)
- Until one day… (the problem — something disrupts the norm)
- And because of this… (other solutions tried — what didn't work)
- And because of this… (the consequences — stakes get real)
- Until finally… (your offer or solution)
- And ever since that day… (the success story — results achieved)

---

## 5. THE 3-ACT PLAY
**Use when:** Writing a case study or explaining how you solved a client problem.

**Structure:**
- **Act 1 — Set Up:** Introduction → Plot Point (the inciting moment)
- **Act 2 — Conflict:** Rising Action → Stakes Get High → Plot Point → Crisis
- **Act 3 — Resolution:** Falling Action → Resolution (how it was solved)

*Classic structure. Works for any narrative with a clear before/after.*

---

## 6. WHAT, SO WHAT, NOW WHAT
**Use when:** Presenting to executives or making a case for budget.

**Structure:**
- **WHAT** — the background: what happened, what the data shows
- **SO WHAT** — the consequences: why it matters, what's at risk
- **NOW WHAT** — the solution: what you recommend doing next

*Fastest framework for busy decision-makers. Cut everything else.*

## QUICK PICKER

| Framework | Best for |
|-----------|---------|
| Hero's Journey | Personal story, founder story, interview pitch |
| Golden Circle | Company/product explanation, mission-led pitches |
| Mountain Structure | Talks, dinner stories, keynotes with a payoff |
| Pixar's Framework | Empathy-first asks, warm-up before a request |
| 3-Act Play | Case studies, client problem walkthroughs |
| What/So What/Now What | Exec presentations, budget requests |
