# The 5 Dimensions of Strategic Thinking
*Source: Cicely Simpson — Leadership Systems*

Strategic thinking is not a personality trait. It is a skill with 5 distinct parts. Most leaders use only 1 or 2 without realising it.

---

## The 5 Dimensions

**1. Long-Term Orientation**
Think in quarters and years, not days and weeks.

**2. Systems Thinking**
See the organisation as one connected system. It stops wins in one area from becoming losses in another.

**3. Pattern Recognition**
Connect dots across markets, industries, and functions.

**4. Anticipation**
See what is coming before it becomes urgent. It replaces fire drills with preparation.

**5. Strategic Questioning**
Ask questions that shift a team from tactical to strategic thinking. It builds a culture that solves problems before they escalate.

---

## The Daily 15-Minute Practice

Strategic thinking does not need a bigger block of time. It needs a repeatable moment in your day where you stop reacting and start looking further ahead.

**Before you check email, spend 15 minutes on these 3 questions:**

1. What patterns am I seeing?
2. What should I be anticipating?
3. What should I be asking my team today?

---

## Prompts — copy and paste

### Self-assessment prompt
```
Evaluate my strategic thinking across these 5 dimensions: Long-Term Orientation, Systems Thinking, Pattern Recognition, Anticipation, Strategic Questioning. For each, rate me 1–5 based on what I describe below, then tell me which dimension I should develop first and how. Context: [describe your role and recent decisions]
```

### Daily 15-minute session prompt
```
I have 15 minutes before I open email. Run me through the strategic thinking practice:
1. What patterns should I be noticing in [your industry/context]?
2. What should I be anticipating in the next 30–90 days?
3. What is the one question I should be asking my team this week?
Be direct. No filler.
```

### Systems thinking diagnostic prompt
```
I'm about to make this decision: [describe decision]. Map out the second and third-order effects across the business — where could a win in one area become a loss in another? Flag the 2–3 biggest systemic risks.
```

### Strategic questioning prompt (for team meetings)
```
Generate 5 questions I can ask in my next leadership meeting that shift the conversation from tactical to strategic. The current context is: [describe what the team is focused on]. Each question should make people stop and think ahead, not just report status.
```
