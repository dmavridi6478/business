# Operations Book in One Page

Source: Eric Partaker, "Operations Book in One Page" — 16 dense mini-
frameworks packed into a single poster infographic (download offered at
brief-link.com/operations). The source image is small enough that only
each framework's title, category structure, and headline labels are
reliably legible — sub-bullet detail inside several panels was too small
to transcribe accurately, so this file indexes what each framework is and
where to go deeper, rather than reproducing text that couldn't be verified.

## The 16, grouped

**Operational systems**
1. **5 Layers of Operational Excellence** — a circular maturity model: Standardization → Automation → Measurement → Continuous Improvement → Innovation
2. **The Operations Funnel** — Chaos → Standardization → Optimization → Automation → Innovation
3. **How to Build a Business That Runs Itself** — a strategy → goals → people/roles → delegation matrix → culture → feedback-loop sequence
4. **10 Principles of Kaizen** — continuous-improvement principles centered on "stop reacting, start solving" and small daily gains

**Goal-setting frameworks**
5. **6 Stellar Frameworks to Set Goals Like a CEO** — OKRs, EDGE Method, V2MOM, FAST Framework, SMARTER Goals, WOOP Model (side by side — pick whichever fits your planning cadence)
6. **OKRs vs. KPIs vs. CSFs** — what each is, how they guide you, common mistakes, best practices for all three, compared side by side. Already covered in more depth by this repo's `business-decision-frameworks` (OKRs) and `quarterly-okr-architect` (4DX/MASTER/DUMB/backward planning) — this panel is the fast comparison table those don't have.

**Metrics**
7. **Top 18 KPIs for CEOs** — a numbered list (Revenue Growth Rate, Gross Margin, Customer Acquisition Cost, Churn Rate, LTV:CAC, and 13 more) with what to measure and why. Different source and different exact list from this repo's existing `c-suite-30-kpis` command (Cicely Simpson, 30 KPIs) — use both; they don't fully overlap.
8. **OpEx / CapEx / RevEx / FinEx** — the 4 expenditure categories: what each is, why it matters, examples, best practices, common mistakes.

**People & leadership**
9. **How to Hire Top Talent (When You Can't Compete with Big-Company Pay)** — the 7-Method Scorecard, knowing what top talent wants, right person/right seat, building an offer beyond salary, hiring mistakes to avoid.
10. **C-Level Leaders** — one-line mandate for each: CEO (Chief Executive), COO (Operations), CFO (Financial), CMO (Marketing), CTO (Technology), CHRO (Human Resources), CIO (Information), CPO (Product).
11. **The Skill/Will Matrix** — plots people on Skill × Will (High/Low each) into 4 quadrants: High Potentials, Top Performers, Low Performers, Disengaged Experts — with the coaching approach for each.
12. **The Leadership Paradox** — Overtrusting vs. Micromanaging, and the "effective delegation" zone between them.

**Change & meeting cadence**
13. **7 Change Models Used by Top CEOs** — a named list (ADKAR, Kotter's 8-Step Process, Lewin's 3-Stage Model, McKinsey Influence Model, Prosci Change Triangle, Bridges Transition Model, and an 8th labeled "8CE's Change Delta Model" in the source) for leading organizational change.
14. **Master These 7 Meetings to Lead Like a Top CEO** — All-Hands, Weekly 1:1, Board/Investor Update, Leadership Team Meeting, Voice-of-Customer Session, Weekly Operating Review, Quarterly Planning Session.
15. **The Eisenhower Matrix (CEO-level)** — Urgent+Important → Do; Urgent+Not Important → Delegate; Not Urgent+Important → Decide; Not Urgent+Not Important → Delete — with 7 CEO-level examples for each quadrant.
16. **The CEO Checklist** — a category checklist: Strategic Direction, Team & Culture, Technology & Data, Leadership & Self-Mastery, Reinvestment, Scalable Operations, Risk & Compliance, Boards & Advisors, Customers & Retention, Brand & Market Presence, Exit & Long-Term Options.

## Plain-text prompts

**Run the CEO-level Eisenhower Matrix on a real task list:**
```
Sort this list of tasks into the CEO-level Eisenhower Matrix — Do
(urgent+important), Delegate (urgent, not important), Decide (not urgent,
important — needs a decision, not action yet), Delete (neither). For each
Delegate item, name who it should go to. Tasks: [PASTE LIST]
```

**Score a leadership team on the Skill/Will Matrix:**
```
Place each of these team members into the Skill/Will Matrix quadrant
(High Potential, Top Performer, Low Performer, Disengaged Expert) based
on this: [PASTE NOTES ON EACH PERSON]. For each, give the specific
coaching move for that quadrant — not generic management advice.
```

**Diagnose the Leadership Paradox:**
```
Based on this description of how I manage my team [PASTE DESCRIPTION],
am I overtrusting, micromanaging, or in the effective-delegation zone?
Give me the specific evidence for your read and one change to make.
```
