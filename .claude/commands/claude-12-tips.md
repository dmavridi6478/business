# 12 Ways to Use Claude Without Hitting Usage Limits
*Source: @your.aimentor on TikTok*

---

**TIP 01 — Edit Your Prompt. Don't Stack Messages.**
When Claude misses the mark, click the edit icon on your original message and fix it there. Every follow-up reloads the full conversation history and costs more tokens.
> Fix the source. Don't pile on.

---

**TIP 02 — Start a Fresh Chat Every 15 to 20 Messages.**
Claude re-reads your entire chat history on every single reply.
- Message 1 = ~200 tokens
- Message 30 = ~50,000 tokens

Ask Claude to summarize the session. Copy it. Open a new chat. Paste it in.
> Long chats are expensive chats.

---

**TIP 03 — Batch Your Questions Into One Message.**
Don't send three separate messages. Send one compound message:
> "Summarize this article, list the key points as bullets, then suggest 3 headlines."

One message. One token cost. Better answers because Claude sees the full picture.
> One turn instead of three. Always.

---

**TIP 04 — Upload Files to Projects, Not Every Chat.**
If you upload the same PDF or brief in every new chat, Claude counts those tokens every single time. Projects cache your files at up to 90% less token cost.
Upload once. Use it everywhere. Stop paying the same cost repeatedly.
> Upload once. Use it everywhere.

---

**TIP 05 — Store Your Custom Instructions in a Project.**
Without context, your first few messages are just re-explaining who you are and how you work. Go to Settings and store your role, tone, and preferences inside a Project. Those instructions get cached too — they run in the background without eating your limit.
> Set it once. It runs forever.

---

**TIP 06 — Turn Off Tools You Are Not Actively Using.**
Web search, Research mode, and connected apps add tokens to every response even when not requested. If you're writing or editing, toggle all tools off.
> Idle tools are hidden token drains.

---

**TIP 07 — Use Haiku 4.5 for the Small Stuff.**
Quick answers, brainstorming, formatting, and grammar fixes don't need Sonnet or Opus. Haiku 4.5 is fastest and lightest.
> Don't use a sledgehammer to crack a nut.

---

**TIP 08 — Match the Model to the Task.**
| Model | Use for |
|---|---|
| Haiku 4.5 | Quick tasks, outlines, simple edits, high-volume |
| Sonnet 4.6 | Writing, analysis, coding, content drafts (best for most work) |
| Opus 4.7 | Deep research, hard logic, long document review |

> Right tool. Right task. Every time.

---

**TIP 09 — Avoid Peak Hours When You Can.**
Peak hours: **5am–11am Pacific Time, weekdays**. The same task at 10am Tuesday hits your limit sooner than at 9pm. Shift heavy sessions to evenings or weekends.
> Off-peak = more headroom.

---

**TIP 10 — Spread Your Sessions Across the Day.**
Claude runs on a rolling 5-hour window that resets continuously — not at midnight. 2–3 focused sessions per day keeps you fresh all day.
> It resets as you go. Pace yourself.

---

**TIP 11 — Write Shorter, Sharper Prompts.**
Vague prompts get long, wandering answers that waste tokens. Specific prompts get precise answers on the first try.

| Vague | Sharp |
|---|---|
| "What do you think about this?" | "Write a 5-bullet summary in plain English under 200 words." |

> Precision costs less than vagueness.

---

**TIP 12 — Claude's Limit Is Not a Wall. It Is a Signal.**
- Edit, don't stack
- Reset every 15–20 messages
- Match the model to the task

> The limit is telling you something. Listen to it.
