---
description: Compare three options by upside, cost, reversibility, and risk
argument-hint: [three options and the decision context]
---

Compare the three options in "$ARGUMENTS" across four dimensions:

| Dimension | Option A | Option B | Option C |
|-----------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Upside** | best realistic outcome | best realistic outcome | best realistic outcome |
| **Cost** | full cost (money, time, attention) | full cost | full cost |
| **Reversibility** | how hard to undo if wrong | how hard to undo | how hard to undo |
| **Risk** | main downside and probability | main downside | main downside |

After the table:
- Name the option you would choose and the single most important reason
- Name the condition that would flip the choice (what would have to be true for a different option to win)
- Flag any assumption baked into the comparison that is worth checking before deciding

If fewer or more than three options are given, adapt accordingly. If "$ARGUMENTS" is empty, ask what options and context to compare before proceeding.

Note: `/decision-matrix` (also in this repo) compares two options with budget/timeline/strategic-value criteria. This command is the 3-option upside/cost/reversibility/risk framework.

Source: @theusefulshift "20 Claude Commands That Feel Illegal" — Command 09 (decision).
