## THE 7 FRAMEWORKS

### 1. Jobs To Be Done (JTBD)
People don't buy products. They hire them to get a job done. Focus on the problem, not the persona.

**Why it matters:** Shifts focus from who your customer is to what problem they need solved.

**How to apply:**
- Identify the core job your customer is hiring your product to do
- Build your positioning, messaging, and offers around that job
- Ask: "What were they struggling with before they found us? What were they using instead?"

**Prompt:**
```
My product is [PRODUCT]. Help me apply Jobs To Be Done:
1. What job is the customer hiring this product to do? (functional, emotional, social)
2. What is the "struggling moment" that triggers the hire?
3. Rewrite my value proposition around the job, not the features.
4. What competing "hires" am I up against? (alternatives the customer might use)
```

### 2. The 95-5 Rule
Only 5% of your market is ready to buy right now. The other 95% is future demand. Build brand today to win tomorrow.

**Why it matters:** Most of your audience isn't buying today. Brand marketing creates demand for tomorrow.

**How to apply:**
- Invest more in reaching and educating future buyers, not just chasing ready ones
- Measure brand metrics (awareness, recall, association) alongside conversion metrics
- Create content for the 95% who aren't ready yet — so you're top of mind when they are

**Prompt:**
```
My business is [BUSINESS]. Apply the 95-5 Rule:
1. What does my 5% (ready-to-buy) audience look like? What content converts them?
2. What does my 95% (future demand) audience look like? What content builds demand?
3. Give me a content strategy that serves both without confusing either.
4. What brand metrics should I track alongside revenue?
```

### 3. Peak-End Rule
People remember the peak (most intense moment) and the end of an experience. Design those moments with intention.

**Why it matters:** Those two moments shape how people remember your brand and decide what to do next.

**How to apply:**
- Map your customer journey and identify the peak moment (most emotionally intense)
- Design the peak to be powerful, memorable, and on-brand
- Make the ending satisfying and clear — the last thing they experience shapes everything

**Prompt:**
```
My customer journey for [PRODUCT/SERVICE] looks like: [DESCRIBE].
Apply the Peak-End Rule:
1. Where is the current peak moment? Is it positive or negative?
2. Where is the current end point?
3. How do I redesign the peak to create a "wow" moment?
4. How do I redesign the ending to leave them satisfied and clear on next steps?
```

### 4. Anchoring
The first number or idea people see becomes the reference point for everything after.

**Why it matters:** First impressions set expectations and influence every comparison.

**How to apply:**
- Lead with your highest value or price first to set a strong anchor
- In pricing, show the most expensive option first — everything else looks reasonable by comparison
- In content, lead with a big claim or bold number before qualifying it

**Prompt:**
```
I'm pricing/positioning [PRODUCT/OFFER].
Apply Anchoring:
1. What should my anchor be? (highest price, biggest result, strongest claim)
2. How do I present it first in my sales page / pitch / email?
3. What comparisons do I want customers to make after seeing the anchor?
4. Where am I accidentally anchoring low right now?
```

### 5. Information Gap Theory
Curiosity is triggered when people sense there's a gap in what they know. That's why hooks make content go viral.

**Why it matters:** Curiosity drives attention, clicks, and shares.

**How to apply:**
- Use curiosity-driven hooks that create a gap and promise the answer
- Open loops early in content; close them at the end
- Tease the insight before delivering it

**Prompt:**
```
I'm creating content about [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE].
Apply Information Gap Theory:
1. What does my audience think they know about this? What are they wrong about?
2. Write 3 hook variations that create an information gap (curiosity-driven, not clickbait).
3. How do I structure the content to maintain the open loop until the payoff?
4. What's the "promised answer" that makes clicking irresistible?
```

### 6. Growth Loops vs Funnels
Funnels leak. Growth loops compound over time and turn customers into your next channel.

**Why it matters:** Loops create compounding growth. Funnels always lose leads.

**How to apply:**
- Design products, offers, and experiences that encourage sharing, inviting, and referrals
- Identify where customers naturally want to tell others (peak moment, transformation, status signal)
- Build the loop into the product itself, not as an afterthought

**Prompt:**
```
My business is [BUSINESS]. I currently use a funnel approach.
Help me design a growth loop:
1. Where in the customer journey do users naturally want to share or invite others?
2. What incentive (intrinsic or extrinsic) would make them act on that impulse?
3. Design a growth loop: input → action → output → re-input.
4. What's the viral coefficient this loop could achieve, and what would unlock it?
```

### 7. Memory Structure
Buyers remember situational cues, feelings, and mental shortcuts more than product features or feature lists.

**Why it matters:** Memory drives recall in the moment of decision.

**How to apply:**
- Build strong mental associations with specific situations and triggers ("when X, think us")
- Use consistent cues: color, sound, phrase, image, or character that becomes associated with you
- Tie your brand to the moment of need, not just the product category

**Prompt:**
```
My brand is [BRAND]. Apply Memory Structure:
1. What is the specific "buying moment" — the situation where customers should think of us?
2. What mental shortcut or category cue do we want to own?
3. Design a memory trigger: what consistent element (visual, phrase, sound, emotion) anchors recall?
4. Where in our current marketing are we competing on features instead of building memory structures?
```

## THE BIG PICTURE

Marketing isn't just about getting attention. It's about understanding how people **think**, **remember**, and **decide** — then building strategies that respect human psychology.

Goal: Understand people → Design better experiences → Build demand → Drive growth
