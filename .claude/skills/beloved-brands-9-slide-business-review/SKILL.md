# 9-slide business review (Beloved Brands graphic)

Source: one graphic showing nine thumbnail slides for a fictional brand ("Gray's Cookies"). The thumbnails were partly too small to read, so the structure is exact and the example text is partial. The numbers on the scorecard thumbnail were not legible and are not reproduced.

## The nine slides
| # | Slide | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ScoreCard | A table of brand health, market health, mindshare and physical availability metrics: this year, last year, plan, competitor, comment |
| 2 | SWOT Summary | Strengths and drivers, weaknesses and inhibitors, opportunities, threats (two or three bullets each) |
| 3 | Review Summary | One theme from each section, plus one "brand challenge" statement |
| 4 | MarketPlace | Three findings, then "Marketplace Review conclusion" |
| 5 | Consumers | Three findings, then "Consumer Review conclusion" |
| 6 | Competitors | Three findings, then "Competitor Review conclusion" |
| 7 | Channels | Three findings, then "Channel Review conclusion" |
| 8 | Brand | Three findings, then "Brand Review conclusion" |
| 9 | Financials | Three findings, then "Financial Review conclusion" |

## The pattern that makes it work
- Every section slide has **three numbered findings, an arrow, one bold conclusion line**. The review summary repeats the conclusions in order.
- The example's chain: market shrinking but "good for you" segment growing, consumers like the concept, competitors will enter the segment, channels have distribution gaps, brand needs more awareness, financials show growth not matching investment. One brand challenge sums it up.

## Use it
Run `/business-review-9 <your brand and data>`. Related: `competitive-landscape`, `kpi-scorecard`, `business-intelligence-report`.

## Cautions
- **[Certain]** The example is invented; do not treat its percentages as benchmarks.
- **[Likely]** A three-bullet limit forces focus but drops nuance; keep a backup page of evidence per slide.
