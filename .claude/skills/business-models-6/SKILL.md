# 6 key business models - pick, then run

Source: a six-panel infographic. The panels give one-paragraph definitions; the method below is added so each can be run, and is marked as such.

## Pick by the question you actually have

| Question | Model | Output |
|---|---|---|
| How much should we invest in today's business vs new bets? | McKinsey 3 Horizons | Horizon map with an allocation decision |
| Where in the digital customer journey are we weak? | Smart Insights (Plan, Reach, Act, Convert, Engage) | Stage scorecard + KPI per stage |
| How attractive is this industry and who holds the power? | Porter's Five Forces | Force-by-force rating + implication |
| How big is the opportunity we can realistically win? | TAM - SAM - SOM (+ earlyvangelists) | Three sizes, method shown |
| Is our strategy balanced across company, customer, competitor? | Ohmae's 3 Cs | Gap list per C + the overlap statement |
| Which business units or products deserve money? | GE-McKinsey nine-box | Unit placement + invest/selective/harvest call |

If two models fit, run the cheaper one first. Never run all six on one question.

## The six, as drawn in the source

**McKinsey 3 Horizons.** H1 core business - maximise profit and stability from current products. H2 emerging opportunities - nurture what could become future income. H3 future ideas - genuinely new businesses. Axes: value over time. Method: list every initiative, tag H1/H2/H3, show spend share per horizon, flag an empty H2 (the usual failure). A 70/20/10 split is often quoted; it is a rule of thumb and is not in the source.

**Smart Insights framework.** Plan (strategy, SMART goals) -> Reach (build awareness) -> Act (get interaction) -> Convert (sales) -> Engage (long-term relationship, advocacy). Method: one KPI and one current number per stage; the lowest conversion between stages is the bottleneck.

**Porter's Five Forces.** Supplier power (price influence), buyer power (pricing and quality), competitive rivalry (intensity), threat of substitutes (ease of switching), threat of new entrants (barriers). Method: rate each Low/Med/High with one piece of evidence; the strategy implication is the force rated High.

**TAM - SAM - SOM.** TAM total demand; SAM the segment your product can serve; SOM the portion you can realistically capture. Source adds the innermost ring, EVG = earlyvangelists, the most likely first customers. Method: top-down and bottom-up, both shown; if they differ by more than 3x, say which is wrong and why.

**Ohmae's 3 Cs.** Company, Customer, Competitors; strategy sits where the three overlap. Method: one question per C (what do we do uniquely well; what does the customer value and pay for; what do rivals do better), then write the single sentence where all three agree.

**GE-McKinsey nine-box.** X = competitive strength of the business unit (low/med/high); Y = industry attractiveness. Top-left cells: invest/grow; diagonal: selectivity/earnings; bottom-right: harvest/divest. Benefits listed in the source: portfolio analysis, resource allocation, strategic insight, decision making, market positioning. Method: score each unit on 5-8 weighted factors per axis, plot, then decide.

## Rules

- Inputs must be real figures or labelled assumptions; a framework filled with guesses is decoration.
- State the model's blind spot in one line (Porter ignores complements; nine-box scores are subjective; TAM is easy to inflate).
- Pairs with: `market-sizing-analysis`, `competitive-landscape`, `gtm-strategy`, `mckinsey-7s-model`, `business-decision-frameworks`.
