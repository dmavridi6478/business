# LinkedIn algorithm 2026 - claims, graded

Source: one infographic, "How the New LinkedIn Algorithm Works 2026 - a behavior-driven distribution system: relevance + dwell time + conversation depth", footer www.smarterwithai.news. It cites no source and the layout is AI-generated. Transcribed 15 October 2026; grading uses a web check the same day.

**Evidence available:** a LinkedIn Engineering article from October 2024 on dwell-aware ranking is cited by practitioners (I saw it only second-hand and did not read it). Everything about 2026 weights, multipliers and format rankings found online is third-party analysis; one vendor puts comments at "5 to 15x" likes, and another source says its own documentation does not support multipliers. LinkedIn publishes no signal ranking.

## The infographic, graded

| Section | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| State of LinkedIn | Reach is selective, relevance over volume; quality over raw impressions; value over promotion; consistency + quality beat frequency | Plausible, unsourced |
| State of LinkedIn | Native multi-slide documents boost dwell time | Plausible (dwell is a published signal; the document effect is practitioner data) |
| Science of comments | Early comments boost initial distribution; long comments raise dwell; threads extend lifespan; replies signal participation | Plausible, unquantified. The 0-60 min / 1-24 h / 2-7 day windows are not sourced |
| Science of comments | Memory aid C.O.R.E. = Conversation depth, Ongoing replies, Relevance, Early activity | A mnemonic, not a finding |
| Format table | Carousels > infographics > video > polls > text with visuals > text-only (bars) | Unsourced; bars are illustrative |
| Impact matrix | 1 Reposts, 2 Meaningful comments, 3 Dwell time, 4 Saves, 5 Likes | Unsourced and internally odd: reposts are ranked first yet labelled "secondary reach amplification" while comments and dwell are called "critical" |
| Distribution flow | Test audience -> early signals (dwell, comments, saves) -> expanded reach -> resurfacing | Standard tiered-distribution story; consistent with how feeds generally work, not a LinkedIn statement |
| Checklist | Post in your audience's active window; strong opening line for "See more"; concise but substantial; spacing; pair insight with visuals; end with a specific question | Sound craft advice that costs nothing to follow |
| Do / Don't | Ask "What would you do?"; avoid "Thoughts?" | Sound, specific-question advice |
| Creator patterns | Consistent not excessive; skimmable; document posts; comment shortly after posting; reply within the first hour; follow-up posts | Plausible; "first hour" is folklore with logic behind it |

Study flash prompts on the graphic: meaningful engagement beats impressions; carousel-style documents and infographics raise dwell time; the first-hour action is active comment engagement and quick response.

## What to do with it

Follow the checklist items (free, harmless). Do **not** restructure a content plan around the format ranking or signal order. Run the test below and let your own numbers decide.

### 6-week test (one variable at a time)

1. Weeks 1-2: baseline. Post the way you do now; record impressions, reactions, comments, saves, profile views per post (the `linkedin-analytics-reader` skill or Taplio analytics).
2. Weeks 3-4: change only the format (document vs text with visual), same topics and times.
3. Weeks 5-6: change only your reply behaviour (answer every comment within 60 minutes vs your usual).
4. Compare per-post medians, not best posts. Fewer than ~8 posts per arm is noise; say so.
5. Keep what moves *leads* (see strategy 9 in `linkedin-9-lead-gen-strategies`), not what moves impressions.
