# 6 prompts to build a digital product

Source: an infographic by Richard Yu (Batch 117), "Choose your favorite LLM and start building." The six prompts are quoted verbatim; replace the brackets. The graphic closes with a promotion for allen.store (a hosting and checkout service); that is an advertisement, not part of the method, and nothing here depends on it.

| # | Step | Prompt (verbatim) | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Define the transformation | "Act as a digital product strategist. My buyer is [who]. They want to go from [before] to [after]. Write this transformation as one clear sentence." | Clarity on who it is for and the result, before you write anything |
| 02 | Generate the outline | "Build a 5-module digital product teaching [result] to [avatar]. For each module give a title, 3 to 5 lessons, and the key takeaway." | A module-by-module structure |
| 03 | Cut and reorder | "Here's my outline. Remove anything that doesn't directly help the buyer reach [result], and reorder the rest into the simplest logical path." | Only what moves the buyer toward the result |
| 04 | Draft each module | "Write Module [X]: [title]. Make it a clear step-by-step lesson with one worked example. Keep it simple enough for a beginner to follow." | One module at a time, so content stays focused |
| 05 | Rewrite in your voice | "Rewrite this in a direct, personal voice. Keep the structure, but leave space for me to add my own story and examples. Flag where my experience would strengthen it." | Room for your stories, numbers and mistakes, which is what adds value |
| 06 | Add proof and examples | "For each module, suggest where a real result, screenshot, or before-and-after example would make the lesson more convincing." | Every lesson grounded in a tangible result |

## How to run it well (added advice)

- **Do step 01 on paper first.** If you cannot say the buyer, the before and the after without the model, the product is not ready; no prompt fixes that.
- **Validate before step 04.** After step 03, test the transformation sentence with five people who match the buyer (a call or a message). Writing six modules for a product nobody asked for is the common failure.
- **Steps 05 and 06 are the product.** A model-written lesson with no stories, numbers or screenshots of your own is interchangeable with every other course. Budget more time for them than for step 04.
- **One module per prompt, in a fresh message,** and paste the approved outline each time so the model keeps the thread.
- **Check facts and claims** the draft makes; never ship income or result claims you cannot document.
- **Copyright and disclosure.** Do not paste other people's course material into prompts; check the rules of the platform where you sell about AI-assisted content.
