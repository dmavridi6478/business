# The strategy behind a $10k LinkedIn content system

Source: one infographic by Lara Acosta ("Follow Lara Acosta for more frameworks"). Transcribed 15 October 2026. The "$10k" figure and the "1-5%" Diehards share are the author's; nothing here verifies them.

## 1. Find your niche with SMPV
- **S - Skill:** your unfair advantage. Not your job title or the outcome you sell, but the specific expertise you have because you have been on the inside of a problem most people have not.
- **M - Market:** who specifically needs that skill. The fastest market to win is the one closest to your own experience, a problem you have watched up close.
- **P - Problem:** the real pain your market is stuck on, the one it usually misdiagnoses. You know the real problem; they only see the symptoms. That gap is your authority.
- **V - Value:** turn it into an offer across tiers: free content that builds trust by pulling back the curtain, and a paid service that solves the problem directly.

## 2. Write for all three audience tiers
- **Diehards (1-5%):** know your industry and you, nodding along before you finish the sentence.
- **Drive-bys (some):** do not know you well; scroll past and click "see more" only if something grabs them right now.
- **Drop-ins (most):** never heard of you; landed by algorithm accident, with zero context and zero trust.
Write so a drop-in can follow a post and a diehard still learns something.

## 3. Build every post on the 5 P's (writing posts that sell without sounding like a pitch)
| P | Do |
|---|---|
| **Problem** | Name the problem you solve; explain it better than your audience can |
| **Promise** | Show the way out; describe the transformation |
| **Proof** | Share results; back them up with stories and experience |
| **Personality** | Show why you; let your tone and story stand out |
| **Perspective** | Share what your industry gets wrong; own the insight only you can offer |
The graphic's sample post follows this order: a result in the opening line, the offer's mechanism in numbered steps, why it works, how it is delivered, then a soft call to action.

## 4. Four formats to copy
1. **Invite the crowd to celebrate:** market the case study and see who raises their hand for the solution; you find more leads with a similar problem.
2. **Package it as a lead magnet:** a simple way to surface more leads with the same problem; the graphic's example is a "niche-finder cheat sheet" (Skill, Market, Problem, Value boxes). *(Source copy error: formats 1 and 2 reuse the same sentence, "Market the case study and see who raises their hand for the solution".)*
3. **The classic before and after:** a glow-up gives readers hope that change is possible; pair it with a picture of the transformation.
4. **Two-way promo:** a behind-the-scenes look at a client's process and challenges, closing with their recommendation: storytelling plus process plus results in one post.

## Bonus: mine your sales calls for content
Take a real sales, onboarding or coaching call; ask AI to identify the top 5-10 objections raised; turn those objections into content ideas; anchor each to your own experience solving it; turn each into the four formats above.

## Use it safely
- **Client material:** a recording or transcript contains personal data; get consent, strip names, and use only what you are allowed to share before pasting a call into any AI tool.
- **Proof posts need permission** and real numbers; never invent a result for the "Proof" P.
- **A lead magnet must be delivered** (see engagement-bait note in `linkedin-9-lead-gen-strategies`).
