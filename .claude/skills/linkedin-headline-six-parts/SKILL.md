# The six-part LinkedIn headline

Source: one infographic by Nader Alnajjar (LEVER), "Optimise this key part of your LinkedIn profile ahead of 2027". The six parts, the layout and the rules quoted below are the sheet's; the template, checks and cautions are my additions. Nothing was tested against LinkedIn search.

## The layout the sheet shows

`[Who you are] | I help [who you help] [get the outcome they want] [through your method] | [Your proof] | [Your next step]`

| # | Part | The sheet's rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who you are | Two or three words of credibility, like "3x Founder" or "Ex-[company people know]". Leave job titles out unless the company name does the work for you |
| 2 | Who you help | Name one audience. Use the words they would type into LinkedIn search, because that is how your headline gets found |
| 3 | The outcome they want | Use the words they would type into search. (The sheet repeats "Name one audience" here, which looks like a copy error) |
| 4 | How you do it | Describe your method or offer in a few words. This is what makes you different from everyone else promising the same outcome |
| 5 | Your proof | A number or a name people recognise. It builds trust before they have read a single post |
| 6 | Your next step | Point to your newsletter, a free resource or your Featured section. "This is where the opportunities start" |

## The four examples, as the sheet annotates them

| Person | Part the sheet highlights | The sheet's reading |
|---|---|---|
| Kinza Azmat | 1 Who you are | "The Exit Gal" is a name people remember, and "3x CEO, 2x Exits" proves she has earned it |
| Lara Acosta | proof | "Forbes 30u30" and "Helped 5,000+ people" build trust before a post is read |
| Mark O'Donnell | who you help | "For exited founders & seasoned operators" tells the right people straight away that it is for them |
| Josh Sanders | next step | "Follow for posts on Personal Branding, Content Creation & AI" tells you what you get if you follow |

**Mistake on the sheet.** The numbered badges on three examples do not match the formula's numbers: the formula has proof at 5, who you help at 2 and next step at 6, but the examples show proof as 4, who you help as 6 and next step as 5. Go by the part names, not the badge numbers. These are real people's public headlines: use them as patterns, copy nobody's claims.

## Fill-in template (my addition)

Write each part, then join with the pipes.

1. Who you are (2-3 words): ______
2. One audience, in their search words: ______
3. Outcome, in their search words: ______
4. Method in 3-5 words: ______
5. One number or recognised name you can prove: ______
6. Next step (newsletter, free resource, Featured): ______

Joined: `[1] | I help [2] [3] [4] | [5] | [6]`

## Checks before you publish (my additions)

- **Proof is true and checkable.** Do not invent "3x exits" or "5,000+ people"; each number must be backed by something you can show.
- **Length.** The full formula is long. LinkedIn caps headlines (about 220 characters at the time of writing; confirm the current limit) and lists show only the first words, so put the audience and outcome early and drop part 4 or 6 first if you must cut.
- **One audience, one outcome.** If you cannot choose, run `positioning-triangle` first.
- **Words, not guesses.** Check the audience's search words in their own posts and job ads rather than assuming.
- **Measure.** The sheet gives no result data. Record profile views, search appearances and inbound messages for four weeks before and after, and change one part at a time.
- **Next step must exist.** Point only to a Featured item, newsletter or resource that is live.
