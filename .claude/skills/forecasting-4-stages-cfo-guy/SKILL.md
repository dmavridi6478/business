# 4 stages of forecasting, beginner to advanced (Your CFO Guy)

Source: one infographic (copyright Josh Aharonoff 2024, yourcfoguy.com). The sample figures on it (for example ARR $3.67m) are illustrative.

| Level | Build | What goes in |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Create a revenue build | Map the business model: inputs lead to outputs | Segment revenue into existing, pipeline and new customers; show ARR movement (new, expansion, contraction, churn) |
| 2 Attach a profit and loss | Introduce costs and a dynamic headcount build (who is on the team, who you will hire) | Revenue, cost of goods sold, gross profit, operating expenses, net operating income, other income or expense, net income; forecast expenses (headcount, professional fees, sales and marketing, travel and entertainment, office supplies, other G&A) |
| 3 Include a balance sheet and cash flows | A 3-statement model with dynamic cash calculated from the P&L and balance sheet | Forecast accounts payable, accounts receivable, deferred revenue, inventory, debt; segment cash flows into operating, investing, financing |
| 4 Include historical data and dashboards | Import existing data so you see where you have been and where you are going; refresh monthly | Match the chart of accounts from accounting software; at least 12 months of history (P&L, balance sheet, headcount, sales); summaries (P&L, balance sheet, cash flows, headcount, revenue); dashboards (budget vs actuals, period-over-period) |

## Why the order matters
Accrual accounting hides cash: profit and loss will not equal cash flow once you grow, which is why Level 3 matters (the graphic's own point). Most early-stage companies can stop at Level 1 or 2.

## Checklist
1. Revenue drivers written as inputs (price, volume, churn), not typed totals.
2. One assumptions sheet; no hard-coded numbers in formulas.
3. P&L ties to the balance sheet and cash flow (cash check equals zero).
4. Actuals imported monthly and a variance column (budget vs actuals).
5. Scenarios (base, low, high) stored separately.
