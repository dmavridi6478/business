# 6 learning prompts (@ai_slacker)

Replace [topic]. Texts are exactly as on the slides.

1. **Learn anything in 20 hours** - "I need to learn [topic] quickly. Create a focused 20-hour learning plan based on the 20% of concepts that deliver 80% of the results. Break it into 10 sessions of 2 hours each, include the best resources, and add a 15-minute review at the end of every session."
2. **Create a one-page cheat sheet** - "Summarize the most important concepts of [topic] on a single page using bullet points, simple diagrams, and practical examples so I can review everything in under 5 minutes."
3. **Quiz me until I break** - "I just studied [topic]. Test me with 10 increasingly difficult questions. After every answer, grade my response, explain mistakes clearly, and help me improve weak areas."
4. **Build a learning ladder** - "Break [topic] into 5 difficulty levels and show me a step-by-step path from beginner to advanced. Include clear milestones, core skills, and what I should master at each stage."
5. **Find the best learning resources** - "List the top 5 books, videos, courses, creators, or websites for learning [topic] quickly. Explain why each resource is valuable and what makes it worth spending time on."
6. **Use the Feynman technique** - "Explain [topic] in the simplest possible way. Then ask me to explain it back to you, identify gaps in my understanding, reteach the weak points, and repeat until I can explain the topic clearly on my own."

## Order I would use
4 (map the topic) then 5 (choose resources, then check they exist) then 1 (schedule) then 2 and 3 after each session, 6 before you call it learned.

## Critique
"100x faster" is marketing: the gain is from active recall (3, 6) and a plan (1, 4), not from reading Claude's summaries. Weakest prompt is 5: models invent books, courses and URLs, so verify every name. The 80/20 split is a heuristic, not a measured fact. For a topic you will be tested on, use the official syllabus as the source.
