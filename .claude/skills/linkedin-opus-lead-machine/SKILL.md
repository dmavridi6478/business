# LinkedIn lead machine in 15 prompts (SME AI, Issue 03)

Source: a 12-page PDF from smeaiconsultancy.com. The file is image-only (no text layer), so I read it page by page from rendered images. The prompts and the weekly routine are the guide's; the "Checks" and "Risks" sections are my additions. Nothing was run on a real profile.

## The method, by phase

| Phase | Prompt | What it asks (paraphrased; keep the guide's wording when you use it) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Audit | 01 Brutal audit | Paste the full profile; read it as the ideal client on a first visit; why scroll past, what is confusing or missing; diagnose only, no rewrite |
| 1 | 02 Ideal client mapping | Job title, company size, the problem, the words they type into search, the fear behind it, the outcome they buy. This becomes the brief for every rewrite |
| 2 Rewrite | 03 Headline | Under 220 characters, outcome first, name the audience, 3 versions (direct, curiosity, result-forward), no buzzwords |
| 2 | 04 About | Open with the client's problem in their words, show one specific result, method in 2 sentences, one CTA, max 260 words, first person |
| 2 | 05 Experience | Per role: one context sentence plus 3 result bullets [action verb] + [what] + [measurable outcome]; no "responsible for" |
| 2 | 06 Featured | Three items: a free resource, a case study or proof, a booking link; title and 160-character description for each |
| 2 | 07 Keywords | 8 to 12 search terms, where each appears (headline, about, experience, skills), without stuffing |
| 3 Content | 08 Pillars | Four pillars, each with topic, audience pain, best format, 3 post ideas this week |
| 3 | 09 Hook system | Five repeatable hook structures with template, why it works, one example |
| 3 | 10 Outlier analysis | Paste 5 high-performing posts; hook, trigger, format, why rewarded; 3 respun ideas |
| 4 Connections | 11 Segmentation | Sort the list into A (message this week), B (nurture with content), C (not now), with reasons for A |
| 4 | 12 Outreach | Per A-list person: under 60 words, reference something specific, no pitch, restart a conversation |
| 4 | 13 Follow-up sequence | Message 1 opens, message 2 after 5 days adds value, message 3 after 7 more days a low-friction offer |
| 4 | 14 Response handling | Five reply templates (busy, tell me more, not now, wrong person, how much), under 80 words |
| 5 Voice | 15 Brand voice | Five words, what I never say, default format, a consistent opinion, the feeling I want; output a one-page brief to paste at the start of every session |

## The weekly routine (90 minutes)

Monday 30 min: five posts, one per pillar, mixed formats, under 200 words, strongest hook first. Wednesday 30 min: ten A-list outreach messages, follow up last week's, log in a simple table (name, date, status, next action). Friday 30 min: five comments on outlier posts, three sentences each, adding a real perspective. Guide's claim: run it 8 weeks and inbound will look different.

## Checks (my additions)

- **Model and price claims are unverified.** The guide credits "Opus 4.8" with unique reasoning and quotes an API price and plan availability. I could not confirm any of it; use whichever strong model you have and turn on extended thinking if it is offered. "£0 cost" ignores your time and the subscription.
- **Evidence:** no case, number of clients or outcome is shown. "Ghostwriters charge £5k to £15k" is a sales line. The guide is a lead magnet for an AI readiness assessment and an audit tool.
- **Prompt 05** says to use "relative improvement language" if you have no numbers. Do not invent results: state only what you can back.
- **Prompt 10 and the "steal what works" page** are fine as pattern analysis; copying a post's wording is not. Keep the pattern, change everything else, as the guide itself says.

## Risks it leaves out

- **Your connections' personal data.** Pasting names, titles and companies of other people into an AI service is processing their personal data. Check your tool's data terms and your obligations (for example GDPR), keep the list minimal, and prefer pasting only the fields you need.
- **Outreach etiquette and platform rules.** LinkedIn limits automation and unsolicited bulk messaging; send each message yourself, keep volume low, and honour a "no".
- **AI-written messages that read as templates.** Edit every message so a real detail you verified is in it.
- **Profile text may be public.** Do not paste confidential client details into the audit.
