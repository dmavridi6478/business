# 10 things not to say in a job interview

| # | Avoid | Why it hurts | Say instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I'm open to anything." | Seems unfocused and unsure of what you want | "I'm particularly interested in this role because it aligns with my expertise in [specific skill or industry]." |
| 2 | "I don't know much about your company." | Signals lack of preparation and curiosity | "I was impressed by [specific company initiative], and I'd love to learn more about how this role contributes." |
| 3 | "I don't have any weaknesses." | Seems arrogant or lacking self-awareness | "One area I've been developing is [skill]. I've taken steps like [specific action] to improve, and it's made a big difference." |
| 4 | "How soon can I get promoted?" | Impatient rather than focused on adding value first | "What does growth typically look like in this role, and how does success get measured?" |
| 5 | "I'm a perfectionist." | Overused, insincere, tells nothing about your work style | "I hold myself to high standards, but I've learned to balance quality with efficiency by setting clear priorities." |
| 6 | "I left my last job because I couldn't stand my boss." | Speaking negatively about a past employer raises red flags | "I was looking for a role that offers [specific opportunity, e.g. leadership growth, strategic impact], which is why I'm excited about this position." |
| 7 | "I prefer to work alone." | Collaboration is key in most roles; you seem difficult to work with | "I'm comfortable working independently but also enjoy collaborating with teams to drive results." |
| 8 | "What's the salary?" (as the first question) | Bringing up pay too soon looks like your primary concern | "I'd love to learn more about the role and expectations. Could you share how the company approaches compensation and career growth?" |
| 9 | "Time management isn't my strong suit." | A weakness with no solution hurts credibility | "I've been refining my time management by using [specific tool or strategy], which has helped me stay ahead of deadlines." |
| 10 | "I don't have any questions." | Not asking signals lack of interest | "What are the biggest challenges for someone stepping into this position?" |

## Where I disagree with the infographic (use judgment)
- The "say instead" lines are templates. Reciting them verbatim reads as rehearsed; **fill every bracket with a true, specific detail** or do not use the line. A fabricated "initiative you admired" is worse than an honest "I haven't followed the company closely; here is what I learned preparing."
- #8 is context-dependent: in some markets (including Greece and much of the EU) salary expectations are raised early by the recruiter; follow their lead.
- #3 and #9 invite a "weakness + fix" formula that interviewers also recognise. Pick a real weakness with real evidence of improvement.

## Procedure
When the user pastes an answer, find which of the 10 it resembles, explain the specific risk in one sentence, and rewrite it with the user's real facts (ask for them; do not invent).
