# Where product marketing ends: who owns what

Source: an infographic by Svitlana Shuliak (Batch 121; branded Jason AI by Reply). It is written from a product-marketing point of view and gives metrics only for PMM; the metrics for the other four functions in the last column are suggestions added here, not from the graphic. Org charts differ by company: use this to settle ownership, not to imply every company has five teams.

| Function | The question it answers | What it is (graphic) | Day-to-day work (graphic) | Typical metrics (added, not on the graphic) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brand marketing | Why remember | Shapes how the market sees and remembers the company, long before anyone needs to buy | Campaigns and visual identity; PR, events, sponsorships; guard tone and brand guidelines | Awareness, branded search, share of voice |
| Product marketing | Why buy | Owns positioning and messaging; turns features into reasons to buy; arms sales with the story; tracks market reception | Positioning docs and battlecards; competitive research; brief sales and support on launches; test messaging | Win rate, feature adoption, battlecard usage, pipeline influenced (these four are on the graphic) |
| Demand generation | Why now | Turns attention into pipeline with campaigns and offers that bring in qualified leads | Paid and organic campaigns; webinars and lead magnets; funnel tests and attribution | Qualified leads, pipeline created, cost per opportunity |
| Lifecycle marketing | Why stay | Sends the right message at every stage, from first login to renewal | Onboarding and nurture emails; segments and behaviour triggers; A/B tests on every flow | Activation, retention, expansion, churn |
| Customer marketing | Why tell others | Turns happy customers into advocates, case studies and expansion revenue | Case studies and reviews; referral and advocacy programmes; community and customer events | Advocates, references used in deals, referral revenue |

PMM's skills on the graphic: storytelling, research, alignment. The graphic's line is "Feeds all four. Replaces none."

## Using it
- **Settle handoffs in writing.** For each launch, list who owns positioning (PMM), the campaign (demand gen), the in-app and email sequence (lifecycle), the proof (customer marketing) and the brand rules (brand). Overlaps are where launches slip.
- **Share one message house.** PMM owns it; the other four use it. See `pmm-positioning:message-house` and `product-marketing-context`.
- **Check the graphic against your company.** A small company may have one person doing three of these; split by task, not by title.
