# The Positioning Triangle (T, U, D)

Source: one infographic by Nader Alnajjar (LEVER). The three corners and the examples below come from it; the checks in the last section are my additions.

## The three corners

| Corner | What it is | Weak | Strong | Try this | Rule of thumb on the slide |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T - Target audience | The exact people you help, and the problem they would pay to fix | "Business owners" | "Founders who want LinkedIn bringing in leads" | Who would reply if you messaged them tomorrow? | The narrower the audience, the easier every post gets to write |
| U - Unique value proposition | What they actually get from you, in outcomes they recognise | "I help with personal branding" | "I turn a founder's LinkedIn into a steady source of inbound calls" | Finish this sentence: after working with me, they can ____ | If it describes your service, rewrite it as what they walk away with |
| D - Differentiation | The reason they pick you over someone making the same promise | | | Name the advice everyone in your space repeats. What do you do instead? | Pick one and commit. Four differentiators is the same as none |

## Four kinds of differentiator (pick one)

1. **A method:** a process you run that others do not.
2. **A view:** something you will defend that your industry disagrees with.
3. **A proof point:** work you have done that nobody else can claim.
4. **A focus:** one niche, done properly, while others spread wide.

## The positioning line

Formula: **I help [target audience] get [value] by [differentiator].**
Example on the slide: "I help founders turn LinkedIn into a system that brings in clients, using the same process that built one of the platform's biggest founder brands."
Test on the slide: read it out loud. It should never sound like a CV.

## Before and after (from the slide)

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "Marketing consultant" | "I help SaaS founders turn LinkedIn into booked sales calls" |
| "Business coach" | "I help agency owners scale past their first hire without working weekends" |
| "Recruiter" | "I help early-stage startups hire their first sales team in under a month" |
| "Freelance designer" | "I design landing pages that turn founder content into booked calls" |

## Where to use it

| # | Place | What goes there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Headline | Who you help and what they get |
| 2 | About | Open with the line, then prove it |
| 3 | Banner | Your offer or free resource in one line |
| 4 | Posts | Your audience and your view, in the hooks and the closing line |

The slide's reason: a positioning line only works where people check before they follow, message or buy. Use the same wording in all four, because repetition is how people start recognising you.

## Checks I would add

- **Self-identification:** could a stranger in your audience tell, in five seconds, that the line is for them?
- **Outcome test:** does the U corner name a result a client could measure, not an activity?
- **Proof test:** can you point to one piece of evidence for the D corner? If not, choose a different kind of differentiator.
- **Claims:** "in under a month" and "booked calls" are promises; use only numbers you can back.
