# How to answer "Tell me about yourself"

Source thesis: most candidates lose this question in the first 30 seconds - they start from their first job and go chronologically, ramble, and never connect their story to the role. It is not small talk; it is the opening statement.

## Structure (three parts)

| Part | Do | Bad (source) | Good (source) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Where you have been | Relevant foundation, not your full history | "I started in 2010 at a small agency..." | "I spent five years in B2B SaaS, leading product launches across three product lines." |
| 2. Where you are, right now | Current role and what you have built | "Currently I'm a marketing manager doing general things..." | "Now I lead a team of four, focused on driving pipeline through content." |
| 3. Where you are going | Why this role, this company, right now | "I'm looking for a new challenge..." | "Your expansion into enterprise is exactly where I want to grow next." |

Example contrast in the source: the bad version is chronological and vague ("graduated in 2013... a bit of social media... general marketing stuff"); the good version states a role, a result (inbound pipeline +140% at a mid-market CRM platform) and links the move upmarket to the target company.

## Delivery (three rules)

4. **Keep it to 90 seconds** - past two minutes you have lost them; practise with a timer.
5. **Practise out loud, not in your head** - record, play back, ask someone for honest feedback.
6. **Tailor to every role** - read the job description, swap in the examples that fit; generic answers signal low interest.

## Build procedure

1. Ask for: target role and company, 3 achievements with a number each, current role in one line, the reason for this move. If `docs/about-me.md` is filled in, read it first; never copy from it without checking with the user.
2. Draft in the three parts, 150-200 words (about 90 seconds spoken).
3. Check: every claim is true and provable; one number appears; the last sentence names the company or role; no chronological list.
4. Offer a 30-second version for networking and a one-line version for introductions.
5. Give three follow-up questions the interviewer is likely to ask next.

## Rules

- Use only facts the user provides; never invent an achievement, figure or employer.
- Keep the user's own vocabulary; do not make the answer generic.
- The "+140%" and team size in the source are examples; they must not appear in the user's answer unless true.
