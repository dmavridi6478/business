# GTM strategy to sales play, and the three layers of GTM

Sources (Batch 129): an unsigned infographic, "GTM Strategy (where and how to win) to Sales Play (turn strategy into action)", and a graphic by Lucia Kim, "3 layers of GTM: how brands turn strategy into pipeline, revenue and long-term growth". Both are generic frameworks without data. Related existing skills: `gtm-strategy`, `gtm-funnel-2026`, `gtm-six-levels-strategy`, `gtm-plays-12`, `abm-gtm-layers`.

## Part 1: from strategy to a sales play
**GTM strategy ("where and how to win")** covers market analysis, segmentation and prioritisation, positioning and value proposition, and markets and go-to-market model. **The sales play ("turn strategy into action")** means a clear focus (who, what, where, how), an action plan with concrete initiatives, aligned product, sales and marketing, and executing, measuring and adjusting.

| # | Question | What it decides | Evidence to bring |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who? | Ideal customer profile: who are we really targeting? | Won and lost deals, retention by segment |
| 2 | What? | Value proposition: why should customers buy from us? | Buyer words from calls, win-loss reasons |
| 3 | Where? | Which territories and customer segments we prioritise | Market size per segment, reach and cost to serve |
| 4 | How? | Direct or indirect sales; what sales organisation and marketing activities we need | Sales cycle, deal size, channel economics |
| 5 | Not what? | What we are deliberately **not** going to pursue | A written stop list with an owner |

Action loop: **Action plan** (concrete initiatives, owners, timelines) then **Execute** (in the field) then **Measure and adjust** (refine until it works). The graphic calls it "a living sales play".

How to use it: answer 1 to 5 in one page; list three initiatives with owner and date; review monthly. Question 5 is the one teams skip. Without a written "not what", every request becomes a priority.

## Part 2: the three layers of GTM in 2026
| Layer | What it does (summary) |
|---|---|
| GTM foundation | Defines market, ICP, positioning and messaging; a repeatable go-to-market plan across product, marketing and sales; the base for consistent demand |
| Demand activation | Drives awareness and interest through content, campaigns, SEO/AEO/GEO and paid; builds and nurtures pipeline across touchpoints; converts the right demand into qualified opportunities |
| Revenue growth loops | Turns customers into expansion, retention and advocacy; flywheels through feedback, product usage and lifecycle marketing; compounds revenue and market presence over time |

Audit by layer: **Foundation** (can five people state the ICP and positioning the same way?), **Demand** (which channel produces qualified opportunities, at what cost?), **Loops** (what share of new revenue comes from existing customers, and what feeds referrals?). A weak outer layer cannot be fixed by working harder on the inner ones.

## Limits
- Both graphics are generic; neither supplies numbers, benchmarks or a method for choosing between options. Use your own data.
- "Rings" imply order, but the layers run at once; revisit the foundation when loops show who really buys.
- Five questions are necessary, not sufficient. Add pricing, packaging and the cost of acquiring and serving each segment.
- "Aligned product, sales and marketing" is a result, not an action: name who resolves conflicts.
