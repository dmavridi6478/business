# 20 unique AI GTM tools: pick by job, not by logo

Source: a LinkedIn infographic by Roma Kovalenko, sponsored by Reply, which owns **Jason AI** (#11). Treat the list as a sponsored shortlist: one entry is the sponsor's own product, and the card gives no prices, customers, results or dates.

**Verification status:** names and one-line jobs are copied from the card. Vendor sites, pricing, funding, security posture and whether each product still exists were **not** checked in this session. Check each before any trial.

Overlap rule: every row already has a verb in the stack. A new tool earns a place only if it does that verb **measurably better** on your data.

## Method (30 minutes)

1. Name the leak in one sentence: where does pipeline drop (target, reach, reply, meeting, close, adopt)? Use `/gtm-weekly-loop` if you have no numbers.
2. Take only the rows in that stage. Shortlist at most **two**.
3. Write the success metric and the kill date **before** the trial (for example "reply rate +30% over 4 weeks, else cancel").
4. Check the vendor: data residency, what it stores about your prospects, how it handles opt-outs, whether it writes to your CRM unreviewed.
5. Trial on a copy or a small segment. Autonomous senders (AI SDR, voice agents, auto-dialers) stay behind a human approval step; calling and cold-messaging rules differ by country.

## Risks the card does not show

- **Tool sprawl:** 20 tools are 20 data stores and 20 sets of credentials. Integration and clean-up cost often exceeds licence cost.
- **Sponsored list:** survivorship and selection bias. A list built by a vendor's partner is not a market scan.
- **Autonomy risk:** AI SDRs and voice agents act in your name. Consent, recording and anti-spam laws apply to you, not to the vendor.
