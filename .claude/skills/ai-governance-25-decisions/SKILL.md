# 25 AI governance decisions leaders cannot leave unanswered

Source: an infographic by Tiffany Masson, Psy.D. (falkovia.com), "25 AI Governance Decisions Leaders Cannot Leave Unanswered: what to decide before AI becomes difficult to interrupt" (Batch 129). Item names and the leader-decision questions are as printed; the short definitions are my condensed paraphrase. "Human Authority Line" and "Governance Traceability Map" are marked with a trademark symbol on the graphic: attribute them to the author and do not present them as generic terms. Compare `ai-governance-15-concepts` (a different graphic), `ai-governance-layers`, `data-to-ai-governance-10`, `governance-15` (command).

| # | Item | Meaning in brief | Leader decision (as printed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Visibility** | | | |
| 1 | AI Inventory | A living record of every model, agent, vendor feature and AI-enabled workflow | Can you see where AI already influences real work and real decisions? |
| 2 | Embedded Vendor AI | AI that arrives through software updates or third-party tools, not a formal purchase | Which approved vendors can introduce or change AI without a new review? |
| 3 | Shadow AI | AI used outside the approved path because the path is unclear, slow or unsuitable | Where are people routing around the system, and what is that behavior telling you? |
| 4 | Decision Touchpoints | Moments where AI informs, recommends, prioritizes or completes an action | Which decisions change meaningfully because AI is present? |
| 5 | Risk Concentration | The point where one model, vendor, workflow or dataset carries outsized influence | How far could one error travel before a person notices and intervenes? |
| **2. Ownership** | | | |
| 6 | Executive Owner | The leader accountable for the organizational outcome | Who answers when the system causes a material issue for people, operations or reputation? |
| 7 | System Owner | The named person for approved purpose, controls, performance and continued use | Does every deployed AI system have one accountable name attached to it? |
| 8 | Decision Owner | The person who keeps final judgment when AI contributes | Who owns the final judgment when the tool influences the answer? |
| 9 | Human Domain Owner | The clinical, academic, operational or professional expert on whether the system fits the real work | Who can say the system is technically functional but professionally wrong? |
| 10 | Vendor Accountability | The documented split of responsibility between organization and vendor | What remains the vendor's responsibility, and what risk has quietly moved to you? |
| **3. Human authority** | | | |
| 11 | Human Authority Line™ | The documented point where automated action ends and human judgment, approval or intervention must begin | What may AI do alone, and what must remain a human decision? |
| 12 | Pause Authority | The named authority to stop or suspend a live AI system when risk appears | Who can stop the system immediately without waiting for a meeting? |
| 13 | Override Safety | Protection for a person who questions, corrects or stops an AI-supported decision in good faith | Can someone challenge the system without being labeled resistant or punished for slowing progress? |
| 14 | Escalation Trigger | A predefined signal that requires human review, leadership attention or a pause | What tells the organization that normal oversight is no longer enough? |
| 15 | Reversal Path | The documented way to undo, correct or contain an AI-assisted action after it has been taken | Can the action be reversed, and who has the authority to do it? |
| **4. Evidence** | | | |
| 16 | Decision Log | A record of what the system recommended, what the person decided, and why | Could you reconstruct the decision six months from now? |
| 17 | Approved Use Record | A clear statement of what the AI was approved to do, where and what remains outside its authority | Is the live use still the same use leadership originally approved? |
| 18 | Override Record | A record of when people reject, correct or set aside the system's output, and what happened | Are overrides treated as evidence about the system rather than resistance to it? |
| 19 | Incident and Near-Miss Register | A record of failures, close calls, unexpected behavior and weak signals | Are you learning only from incidents, or also from close calls and early warning signs? |
| 20 | Governance Traceability Map™ | A connected view of each AI-assisted decision, its owner, controls and evidence | Can you trace an outcome back to the system, person, approval and reasoning that produced it? |
| **5. Continuous governance** | | | |
| 21 | Performance Review | A recurring test of whether the system performs as approved in the environment where it is used | Is the system still producing the outcome it was approved to produce? |
| 22 | Material-Change Review | A renewed review when model, vendor, data, workflow, permissions or intended use changes | What type of change automatically sends the system back through governance? |
| 23 | Drift Monitoring | Ongoing observation for changes in performance, behavior, user patterns or operating conditions | What evidence would show that the system is moving away from its approved state? |
| 24 | Governance Reassessment | A scheduled review of whether ownership, authority, controls and evidence remain adequate | Has the governance changed as quickly as the system has changed? |
| 25 | Retirement Decision | The documented criteria and authority for removing a system that is no longer safe, useful, supportable or aligned | Who can decide the system should no longer operate, and what happens next? |

## Scorecard
For each item score 0 (cannot answer), 1 (answer exists but is undocumented or owned by nobody), 2 (named owner, documented, reviewed in the last 12 months). Report by group; any group under 50% is the agenda for the next meeting. Sum under 20 of 50 means governance is mostly assumed.

## Limits
- A checklist shows what to ask, not how well the answer works. Test items 12, 13 and 15 with a drill, not a policy.
- Rules differ by sector and region: EU AI Act obligations (for example human oversight and record-keeping for high-risk systems), sector regulators and contracts decide what you must do. Take legal advice; this skill is not legal advice.
- The graphic is a consultant's framing without data or a maturity model; weight items by your own risks.
- Item 2 and 10 need your vendor contracts, not just a register.
