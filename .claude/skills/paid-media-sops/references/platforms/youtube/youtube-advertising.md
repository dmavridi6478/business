# YouTube Advertising — Two Tools for Two Different Jobs

## How We Think About YouTube for Ecommerce Clients

YouTube is not one channel. It is two fundamentally different advertising approaches that happen to share a platform. Understanding which tool to use, when, and what to expect from each is the difference between YouTube being a growth lever and YouTube being a budget drain.

**Last reviewed: March 2026.**

---

## The Two YouTube Approaches

| | Non-Skippable Placement-Targeted | Demand Gen (Conversion YouTube) |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | 15-second non-skippable ads hand-placed on specific YouTube channels and videos | AI-optimized conversion campaigns running across YouTube, Discover, Gmail, Maps |
| **Targeting** | Manual — you select the channels, videos, or topics | Audience signals — you provide audiences, the algorithm finds more |
| **Format** | 15-second non-skippable in-stream | Skippable in-stream, Shorts, in-feed, plus image ads on Discover/Gmail |
| **Creative style** | Polished brand/product video — every second counts | UGC-style, direct response, Meta-like creative — hook-driven |
| **Optimization goal** | Brand awareness + consideration; impressions/reach/frequency | Conversions, conversion value, or clicks |
| **Expected in-platform ROAS** | Little to none — this is upper funnel | Measurable — similar to Meta DR campaigns |
| **Incrementality profile** | High — you are reaching people who were NOT searching for you | Moderate — algorithm may capture some existing demand |
| **Difficulty for media buyer** | High — requires research, channel selection, ongoing curation | Moderate — more similar to managing Meta campaigns |
| **Feels like** | Buying a TV commercial on a specific show | Running a Meta conversion campaign on Google's inventory |

**Our position:** Both approaches have a place. For most ecommerce clients, we prefer to start with non-skippable placement-targeted campaigns when the goal is genuine brand building and incremental reach. We layer in Demand Gen when the client has strong direct-response creative (UGC, testimonials) and wants to scale conversion volume beyond what Search and Shopping can deliver. They are complementary, not competing.

## Approach 1: Non-Skippable Placement-Targeted (The TV Buy)

### Philosophy

This is the most labor-intensive YouTube strategy and the one that shows the least in-platform ROAS. It is also, in our experience, the most incremental and the most valuable for long-term brand building.

**Why we prefer this approach for brand investment:**

1. **You control exactly where the ad shows.** By hand-selecting channels and videos, you ensure the ad is seen in a relevant, brand-safe context. The algorithm does not decide placement — you do.

2. **Non-skippable means 100% view rate.** Every impression is a completed view. There is no "skipped after 2 seconds" — the viewer watches the full 15-second message. This is the closest thing to a guaranteed impression in digital advertising.

3. **It reaches people who are not in-market.** Unlike Search or Shopping (which capture existing intent), this approach puts your brand in front of people during content consumption. They are not looking for you. This is demand *creation*, not demand *capture*.

4. **It is highly incremental.** Because you are reaching people outside of active purchase intent, these impressions are far less likely to cannibalize organic or existing paid conversions. A holdout test will typically show strong incremental lift from non-skippable campaigns.

### The "No ROAS" Problem

Non-skippable placement-targeted campaigns will almost never show direct ROAS in your Google Ads dashboard. This is expected and does not mean the campaign is not working.

**Why there is no visible ROAS:**

- The ad is 15 seconds with no click-through option (non-skippable in-stream does not encourage clicks — it encourages watching)
- The viewer sees the ad, registers the brand, and may convert days or weeks later through a different channel (organic search, direct visit, branded search, Meta retargeting)
- Google's attribution model credits the last-click channel, not the YouTube impression that planted the seed

**How to measure the true impact:**

| Measurement Method | What It Shows | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| **Branded search lift** | Did branded search volume increase after launching YouTube? | Moderate — directional but affected by other factors |
| **Direct traffic lift** | Did direct-to-site traffic increase? | Moderate — same caveat |
| **YouTube → audience → conversion tracking** | Build an audience of YouTube ad viewers, track their downstream conversion rate across other campaigns | High — see "Closing the Loop" section below |
| **Geo holdout test** | Run YouTube in test markets, suppress in control markets, measure revenue difference | Highest — causal measurement. The gold standard. |
| **Post-purchase surveys** | "How did you hear about us?" with YouTube as an option | Low-moderate — self-reported, but directional |
| **Google Brand Lift study** | Google's built-in survey measuring ad recall, consideration, purchase intent | Moderate — useful for large spend ($10K+) |

**How to frame this with clients:**

> "This campaign is not designed to show up in the ROAS column of your Google Ads report. It is designed to put your brand in front of [X] thousand targeted viewers per week who have never heard of you. The return shows up in your branded search volume, your direct traffic, and your overall revenue trend — not in a click-through conversion on the YouTube campaign itself. We measure its impact through [holdout test / branded search lift / audience tracking], not through platform attribution."

Set this expectation before launch. If you wait until the first report to explain why ROAS is zero, you have already lost the conversation.

### Channel and Video Selection Process

This is where the work is. The quality of your placements determines the quality of your impressions.

**Step 1: Define the viewer profile**

Before selecting channels, write down who you are trying to reach:
- What content does your target customer watch on YouTube?
- What adjacent interests do they have?
- What creators do they follow?
- What problems are they researching on YouTube?

**Example (skincare brand):**
- Skincare routine videos and "get ready with me" content
- Dermatologist channels (Dr. Dray, Doctorly)
- Beauty review channels
- Wellness and self-care content
- Fitness and health channels (adjacent — people who care about their skin often care about fitness)

**Step 2: Research channels**

Use these methods to find placement candidates:

1. **YouTube search:** Search for terms your customers would search. Note the channels that appear consistently.
2. **Competitor analysis:** Watch competitor ads (use the Google Ads Transparency Center to see where competitors are running). Note which channels they target.
3. **Audience research:** Ask the client's existing customers what YouTube channels they watch. Survey data is gold.
4. **YouTube analytics (if the client has a channel):** Look at the "Other channels your audience watches" report.
5. **Social listening:** Check Reddit, Twitter, and forums in the client's niche for YouTube channel recommendations.

**Step 3: Evaluate each channel**

For each candidate channel, check:

| Criteria | What to Look For | Red Flags |
|---|---|---|
| **Subscriber count** | 50K+ preferred for meaningful reach | Under 10K may not deliver enough impressions |
| **View counts on recent videos** | Consistent views (not one viral hit) | Views declining over time |
| **Audience demographics** | Match your target customer | Primarily children, wrong geography, wrong language |
| **Content quality** | Professional, brand-safe, topically relevant | Controversial content, clickbait, misleading titles |
| **Comment section** | Engaged, relevant discussion | Spam, toxic comments, bot activity |
| **Upload frequency** | Regular uploads (weekly or more) | Dead channels with no recent uploads |

**Step 4: Build and organize your placement list**

Organize channels into tiers:

- **Tier 1 (Core):** Directly relevant channels your customer definitely watches. 10-20 channels.
- **Tier 2 (Adjacent):** Related interest channels where your customer likely spends time. 20-40 channels.
- **Tier 3 (Broad):** Large lifestyle/entertainment channels that reach your demographic broadly. 10-20 channels.

Start with Tier 1. Expand to Tier 2 after the first month if you need more reach. Use Tier 3 sparingly.

**Step 5: Ongoing curation**

- Review placement performance monthly (which channels drove the most impressions, at what CPM)
- Remove channels that are no longer active or have shifted content
- Add new channels as you discover them
- Monitor brand safety — channels can change content direction over time

### Non-Skippable Creative Guidelines

Every second matters. You have exactly 15 seconds to deliver a complete message.

**Structure:**

```
Seconds 1-3: Hook — Grab attention. Show the product or state the problem.
Seconds 4-10: Message — Deliver the core value proposition. Show the product in use.
Seconds 11-13: Proof — Social proof, results, or credibility signal.
Seconds 14-15: CTA + Brand — Brand name, URL, clear next step.
```

**Rules:**
- No logo intros. You do not have time. Start with the product or the problem.
- Show the product within the first 3 seconds. This is not a mystery — tell them what this is.
- Text overlays are mandatory. Most viewers have sound off.
- One message per ad. Do not try to communicate three benefits in 15 seconds. Pick the strongest one.
- The CTA should be visual (on screen) and specific ("Visit [brand].com" or "Search [brand] on Google").
- Produce 3-5 variations with different hooks/angles. Rotate to prevent fatigue.

### Budget Allocation for Non-Skippable

**Recommended:** 10-20% of total ad budget for established brands looking to invest in awareness. For new brands building initial awareness, this can be higher (20-30%) in the first 3-6 months.

**CPM expectations:** $8-$20 for placement-targeted non-skippable, depending on channel competitiveness and audience size. More niche channels = higher CPMs but more relevant impressions.

**Minimum viable test:** $3,000-$5,000 per month for 8-12 weeks. Anything less will not generate enough impressions to measure impact.

## Approach 2: Demand Gen / Conversion YouTube (The Meta-Style Buy)

### Philosophy

Demand Gen (which absorbed Video Action Campaigns in July 2025) is Google's answer to Meta-style direct response advertising. It runs conversion-optimized video and image ads across YouTube, Discover, Gmail, and Maps.

**This feels like running Meta ads on Google's inventory.** The creative style is similar (UGC, talking head, product demo), the optimization is similar (conversions, ROAS), and the audience approach is similar (signals and algorithmic expansion).

### How It Differs From Non-Skippable

| Dimension | Non-Skippable Placement | Demand Gen |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Awareness, brand building, incremental reach | Direct response conversions |
| **Audience** | You choose placements (channels/videos) | You provide audience signals, algorithm expands |
| **Creative** | 15-second polished brand video | UGC-style, hook-driven, direct response |
| **Measurement** | Brand lift, search lift, holdout tests | In-platform CPA, ROAS, view-through conversions |
| **What it replaces** | Traditional TV/brand video spend | Meta or paid social conversion spend |

### Why Targeting Matters More on YouTube Than on Meta

This is a critical operational difference between Demand Gen and Meta campaigns:

**On Meta, broad targeting works.** Meta's algorithm (Andromeda + GEM) is extraordinarily good at finding the right person from a broad pool because Meta has deep behavioral data from the social graph — likes, comments, follows, messaging, content consumption, purchase behavior.

**On YouTube/Google, targeting still matters significantly.** Google's behavioral signals are different — they come from search history, YouTube watch history, website visits, and app usage. These signals are strong for intent but weaker for the kind of lifestyle/identity profiling that Meta does.

The result: **Demand Gen campaigns with well-defined audience signals consistently outperform broad-targeted Demand Gen campaigns.** This is the opposite of the trend on Meta, where broad often beats narrow.

**Recommended audience signals for Demand Gen (in order of strength):**

1. **Customer Match list** — Your actual buyer list. Strongest signal.
2. **Website remarketing** — Site visitors, cart abandoners, product page viewers.
3. **YouTube engagement audiences** — People who watched your YouTube ads (see "Closing the Loop" below).
4. **Custom Intent segments** — Built from search terms your customers use on Google.
5. **In-market audiences** — Google's pre-built audiences of people actively shopping in your category.
6. **Competitor custom segments** — Built from competitor URLs or competitor search terms.

**Avoid:** Generic interest audiences ("fitness enthusiasts," "beauty shoppers") without layering more specific signals on top. These are too broad for YouTube to optimize effectively.

### Demand Gen Creative Guidelines

Demand Gen creative should feel like Meta creative — not like a TV commercial.

**What works:**
- UGC-style talking head testimonials (30-60 seconds)
- Product demonstration with voiceover
- Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA structure
- "Reaction" style content (trying the product for the first time)
- Founder story / behind-the-brand content

**What does not work:**
- Overly polished brand videos with cinematic pacing (too slow for feed environments)
- Logo-heavy corporate content
- Videos without a clear CTA
- Content that assumes the viewer knows who you are (they probably do not)

**Format requirements:**
- Provide both landscape (16:9) and vertical (9:16) versions. Vertical is critical for Shorts and mobile Discover.
- Include both video and image assets. Google reports that multi-format campaigns drive 20% more conversions at the same CPA.
- Minimum 3 video variations and 3 image variations per ad group.

### Demand Gen Bidding and Budget

**Bidding strategy:**
- Start with Maximize Conversions to build data
- Move to Target CPA once you have 30-50+ conversions
- Use Target ROAS for ecommerce with varied product values

**Budget:**
- Minimum $100/day (or 10x your target CPA, whichever is higher)
- Allow 2-4 weeks for the learning phase before evaluating
- Do not judge Demand Gen on last-click ROAS alone — use data-driven attribution or view-through conversions

**Realistic expectations:**
- CPA will typically be 20-50% higher than Meta for similar products (Google's conversion optimization is strong but Meta's social graph gives it an edge for cold prospecting)
- View-through conversions will be a significant portion of attributed conversions — treat these with the same skepticism as Meta 1dv (see `08-attribution-windows.md`)
- Demand Gen is best used as a complement to Search and Shopping, not a replacement

## Closing the Loop: YouTube Audiences → Other Campaigns

This is one of the most powerful and underutilized YouTube strategies. It turns upper-funnel YouTube spend into a direct input for your conversion campaigns.

### How It Works

1. **Build a custom audience** of people who watched your YouTube ads (video viewers audience in Google Ads → Audience Manager)
2. **Segment by engagement level:**
   - Watched 25% of a video
   - Watched 50% of a video
   - Watched 75% or 100% of a video (highest intent)
   - Watched any video from your channel
3. **Feed these audiences into other campaign types:**
   - **PMax:** Add YouTube video viewers as an audience signal in your asset groups. PMax uses this to find similar users and to prioritize your product ads to people who already saw your brand video.
   - **Search:** Add YouTube viewer audiences as observation audiences. Bid higher on people who saw your YouTube ad and then search for relevant terms.
   - **Display Remarketing:** Retarget YouTube viewers with product-specific display ads.
   - **Demand Gen:** Use YouTube viewer audiences as a seed for lookalike expansion.

### Why This Matters

Without this loop, YouTube spend exists in isolation — money goes out, brand impressions happen, but there is no measurable downstream connection to conversions. With this loop:

- You can track what percentage of YouTube viewers later appear in your conversion campaigns
- PMax becomes smarter because it has a richer audience signal
- Search campaigns can bid more aggressively on users who were primed by YouTube
- You create a measurable bridge between brand spend and conversion performance

### Setup Steps

1. Navigate to: Tools → Audience Manager → Create Audience
2. Select "YouTube users"
3. Define the criteria (viewed any video, viewed specific video, viewed as ad, etc.)
4. Set the membership duration (30-90 days recommended)
5. Name it clearly (e.g., "YT Ad Viewers - 50%+ Watch - 60 Days")
6. Apply this audience as a signal in PMax asset groups and as an observation audience in Search campaigns

## Budget Split Between the Two Approaches

This depends on the client's maturity and goals:

### New Brand / Building Awareness
| Approach | Budget Share | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Non-Skippable Placement | 60-70% of YouTube budget | Build awareness and recognition |
| Demand Gen | 30-40% of YouTube budget | Test conversion creative, build data |

### Established Brand / Scaling Conversions
| Approach | Budget Share | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Non-Skippable Placement | 30-40% of YouTube budget | Maintain reach, expand to new audiences |
| Demand Gen | 60-70% of YouTube budget | Drive measurable conversions alongside Search/Shopping |

### Always-On YouTube Budget as % of Total Ad Spend
- **Starting point:** 10-15% of total ad spend allocated to YouTube
- **Scaling point:** 15-25% once you have measured incremental impact via holdout or brand lift
- **Maximum (awareness-heavy brands):** 25-35% — beyond this, ensure Search and Shopping are not under-funded

## How This Connects to the SOP Framework

| SOP Concept | YouTube Connection |
|---|---|
| **CPM** (02) | Primary cost metric for non-skippable. Placement selection directly determines CPM. |
| **CTR** (02) | Less relevant for non-skippable (no click-through). Critical for Demand Gen — equivalent to Meta CTR. |
| **CVR** (02) | Demand Gen CVR should be benchmarked against Meta, not against Search. Non-skippable CVR is essentially zero (and that is fine). |
| **Creative Metrics** (05) | Video view rate, view-through rate, and completion rate are the YouTube equivalents of hook rate and hold rate. |
| **Attribution** (08) | YouTube attribution is overwhelmingly view-through. Evaluate with extreme skepticism. Use holdout tests. |
| **Incrementality** (09) | Non-skippable placement-targeted is among the most incremental campaign types available. Demand Gen is moderate. Both should be validated with holdout studies. |
| **Contribution Margin** (10) | YouTube brand spend should be evaluated on its contribution to overall business CM, not on in-platform ROAS. Frame it as a portfolio investment, not a standalone P&L line. |
