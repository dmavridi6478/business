# 6 key business models for leaders (Kevin Box graphic)

Source: one graphic; each panel names its own source site (for example researchgate, smartinsights, edrawmind, productmindset, justfreeslide). Panel text paraphrased.

| Model | What the panel says | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| McKinsey's 3 Horizons | H1 core business (maintain and defend), H2 emerging opportunities (nurture), H3 future ideas (create genuinely new business), plotted as value over time | Where should growth effort go across today, next and later? |
| Smart Insights framework | Plan, Reach, Act, Convert, Engage as a funnel, with SMART goals and KPIs for content, media and experience | How do digital activities map to a customer journey? |
| Porter's Five Forces | Supplier power, buyer power, competitive rivalry, substitute threat, new-entrant threat | How attractive and defensible is this market? |
| TAM, SAM, SOM | Total addressable market, serviceable available market, serviceable obtainable market, plus "earlyvangelists" (your most likely early customers) | How big is the opportunity I can actually win? |
| Ohmae's 3Cs | Company, Customer, Competitor; strategy sits where the three overlap | Where is my advantage versus customer needs and rivals? |
| GE-McKinsey nine-box | Industry attractiveness (high, medium, low) against competitive strength; cells say invest and grow, selectivity and earnings, harvest and divest | Which business units get investment? Benefits listed: portfolio analysis, resource allocation, strategic insight, decision making, market positioning |

## Cautions
- **[Certain]** None of these gives an answer by itself; each needs your own data. A TAM built top-down from a headline market size is the most common error.
- **[Likely]** "Smart Insights framework" is the RACE planning model; "earlyvangelists" is Steve Blank's term. Credit the originals when reusing.
- **[Likely]** Five Forces and the nine-box are static; revisit them yearly.
