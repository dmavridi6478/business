# Freelance AI tools and five traffic sites

Source: two carousels (watermarked @dagier_official, and a "save for later" paper-texture set). What each slide says is quoted or paraphrased; the critique and the plan are my additions. None of the tools were opened or tested here.

## A. AI tools to land a first client (@dagier_official)

| Tool | Slide line | Use it for | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recraft | Generate illustrations, icons and marketing graphics; "create design packages for brands that need fresh content" | A first set of graphics for a client | Check the licence and commercial-use terms for generated assets; image tools differ |
| Hedra | Turn images and scripts into talking AI videos; promo clips for businesses | Short promo clips | Get written consent before using a real person's face or voice; label AI video where a platform or law requires it |
| Lovable | Landing pages and websites for small businesses: "describe what the client needs, generate the website and customise it" | A first landing-page draft | Generated sites need a security and content check before launch (see `vibe-app-five-holes`) |

The closing slides: "You can use AI websites to significantly undercut the graphic designers and wed designers to land a client... Save this for your own build."

**Critique.** [Likely] Competing on a lower price is the weakest position for a new freelancer: you attract price-driven clients, you cannot raise rates, and the tools are available to the client too. A better first-client path is a narrow niche and a measurable outcome (use `linkedin-positioning-triangle` and `gtm-first-100-customers`), with AI as your speed advantage rather than your price tag. The slides give no evidence that anyone landed a client this way.

## B. Five free sites for traffic (the "10x'd my website traffic" set)

| # | Site | Slide claim | Real use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Google Trends (`trends.google.com`) | Shows what people search for right now, so you post before it peaks | Topic timing; compare terms and regions |
| 2 | SERPtag (`serptag.com`) | Finds keywords you can rank for on Google and in AI answers | Keyword ideas; listed in `oss-tools-part3-checked` |
| 3 | Pinterest (`pinterest.com`) | Every pin sends clicks for months, not hours | A search-like channel that suits visual products |
| 4 | Bing Webmaster Tools (`bing.com/webmasters`) | Gets you indexed on Bing, which powers ChatGPT search too | Submit your sitemap; the ChatGPT link is the slide's claim and is not verified here |
| 5 | Google Analytics (`analytics.google.com`) | Shows which posts bring visitors so you double down | Measure which content earns visits; needs the tracking tag and, in the EU, consent handling |

**Critique.** The title "10x'd my website traffic" has no baseline, timeframe or evidence. The five are a sensible free stack (timing, keywords, a channel, indexing, measurement), not a growth guarantee.

## One-week order (my reading)

1. Day 1: install Analytics and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit the sitemap, set a baseline.
2. Day 2: Google Trends and SERPtag to pick five topics.
3. Days 3 to 5: publish one piece per topic; pin each to a Pinterest board if the content is visual.
4. Day 7: read Analytics and double down on the one piece that earned visits.
