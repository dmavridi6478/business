# Salary negotiation kit

Source: a nine-panel poster collage by Reno Perry, "A Salary Negotiation Book in One Page" (Batch 129). The image was low resolution; scripts are quoted where legible and paraphrased otherwise. The 14-phrases panel ("Never say this / Instead say this") is already the `/salary` command and is not repeated. The poster's numbers (for example "73% of employers expect candidates to negotiate", "55% of candidates accept the first offer", dollar costs per mistake and example totals) are **unsourced**; do not quote them.

## 1. Set three numbers before any conversation
**Rock bottom** (walk-away; never say it), **Target** (market rate you would happily accept), **Stretch** (top of market). Give a range only if you would genuinely accept its bottom. Say total compensation, not just base.

## 2. Preparation checklist (12 steps, as printed)
Research the job market; get clear on your value; list key accomplishments; know the full compensation package; set a target number; have a salary range ready; prepare for common objections; practise what you will say; pick the right moment; keep it professional; plan your follow-up; confirm the offer in writing.

## 3. Six key points
Know your worth; pick the right time (after you have the offer details, not before); speak with confidence; think about everything (non-cash too); ask for more; be ready to say no.

## 4. Answering "what's your expected salary?"
| Situation | Do | Don't say / say (paraphrased from the poster) |
|---|---|---|
| They ask before an offer exists | Flip the question; learn their range first | Don't: "I'm flexible, open to anything." Say: ask for the comp band for the role and what the full scope is |
| You must go first | Give a range anchored near the top of what the market supports, in total comp | Don't: "I just need around X." Say: "I'm targeting total comp in the X to Y range, flexible on structure" |
| They say "that's above our band" | Don't drop base; explore equity, sign-on, review timing | Don't: "OK, what can you do?" Say: "What's flexible on equity, sign-on or review timing?" |
| They ask your current salary | Current pay is not the right starting point | Say: current comp isn't the right starting point; give your target for this role |
Salary-history questions are restricted in many US states; elsewhere the rules differ (see cautions).

## 5. Fifteen things to negotiate besides base
Company equity; signing bonus; more vacation days; work-from-home flexibility; professional development budget; performance bonuses; better job title; flexible work hours; premium health coverage; extended start time; home office setup; parking or commute coverage; full relocation package; severance protection; accelerated review cycles. The poster's dollar ranges are illustrative.

## 6. Six mistakes (and the instead-lines)
1. Accepting the verbal offer on the spot: "Can you send the offer letter and equity documents so I can review the full terms?" 2. Negotiating only the base: "Before we close on base, I want to walk through signing bonus, equity and vacation." 3. Showing all your excitement on the offer call: "I appreciate you walking me through it. I'd like a few days to review and come back with questions." 4. Sharing the exact number of a competing offer: say you have a competitive offer and would like to see their strongest package. 5. Treating an exploding deadline as fixed: ask for a week to make a fully informed decision. 6. Skipping "what else can you offer?": "Given you can't move on base, what else might you be able to offer?"

## 7. The low-offer cheat sheet
(1) Set three numbers. (2) **Check the level first**: level drives band, bonus target, equity and scope; if you were told one level and offered a lower one, address it before numbers. (3) **Negotiate the package**: base, annual bonus, signing bonus, equity, each with what it is, why it helps and a line to say. (4) **Close the deal**: define the package you would accept before negotiating, use a talk track ("if you can get to X in salary and Y in signing bonus, I'm ready to accept once those changes are in the written offer"), negotiate the package, not one item at a time, and use your leverage after they have decided they want you.

## 8. Twelve numbers to know
Market: market range (low to high), total comp benchmark, internal pay band ceiling. Accept: rock bottom, target, stretch. Components: base salary, sign-on bonus, annual bonus target. Equity: equity grant value, vesting schedule, refresh grant timing. Ask for the equity value in dollars in writing, not a share count; find out whether the bonus target is guaranteed and what it actually paid in recent years.

## 9. Before you sign: 12 things to check
Manager pattern; comp benchmarks; equity terms; exit clauses; team health; growth trajectory; references; vesting schedule; sign-on (clawback terms); board and investors; company runway; peer reviews. Each has four checks, for example: ask how long the last direct reports stayed, read severance terms, confirm the cliff and vesting in writing, confirm months of cash runway.

## 10. Compare offers by components, not salary
For each of salary, bonus, signing bonus, RSUs and stock options: what it is, how to calculate it, what to check, and the trap. Traps in short: a low base padded with bonus; a bonus target that rarely pays out; a clawback on signing bonus; RSUs priced on a private-company valuation you cannot sell; options with an exercise window and a tax bill. Build a four-year total-comp table per offer (base, expected bonus, equity at a conservative price, signing bonus, benefits) with your own assumptions and compare totals and risk.

## Cautions
- **Jurisdiction.** The poster is US-flavoured. [Likely] In the EU the Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970) restricts asking candidates about pay history and requires pay ranges to be available to applicants, with national transposition due in mid-2026; check your country's rules. Contracts, notice periods and equity tax treatment differ widely: take local advice before signing.
- **Do not lie** about competing offers or current pay; verifiable claims can void an offer.
- **Equity is not cash.** Value private equity at a steep discount unless there is a liquidity route.
- **Read the written offer** and the equity plan, including clawbacks, non-competes and notice periods.
- **Scripts are starting points.** Adapt to the culture and the person; a rigid script can sound hostile.
