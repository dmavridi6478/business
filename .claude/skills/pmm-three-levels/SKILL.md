# Three levels of a product marketing manager

Source: an infographic by Svitlana Shuliak (Jason AI by Reply), "Junior vs Senior vs Staff: 3 levels of a Product Marketing Manager" (Batch 128). The content is the author's view of the role. Compare `product-marketing/references/marketing-functions-who-owns-what.md` and `pmm-periodic-table.md` (earlier graphics by the same author).

| Factor | Junior | Senior | Staff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mission | Ship the launch well, on time, on message | Own a GTM motion end to end | Shape positioning strategy across the whole portfolio |
| Metrics | Content output, launch checklist completion | Adoption, win rate, sales enablement usage | Portfolio-level revenue influence, market share |
| Skills | Clear copywriting, basic market research, launch project management | Positioning and messaging frameworks, buyer and win-loss analysis | Executive communication, pricing and packaging strategy |
| Decisions | "Am I messaging this right?" | "Are we solving the right buyer problem?" | "Are we even in the right market?" |
| Work style | Execution-focused, guided | Independent, cross-functional | Influence-driven across product, sales, exec |
| Blind spot | Polishing copy without validating the message | Chasing sales requests without a point of view | Losing touch with what reps actually say on calls |
| Main stack | Canva, Docs, Slack | Crayon, Amplitude, Gong | Gong, Tableau, Klue |
| Superpower | Turning specs into clear copy fast | Turning a vague market into a sharp narrative | Making the whole org agree on one story |
| #1 LLM tool | ChatGPT | Claude | Claude |
| Daily phrase | "Does this sound right?" | "Is this what buyers actually care about?" | "Is this even the right market to be in?" |
| Task tracker | Asana | Asana | Asana |

## What changes at each step
- **Junior to senior:** from executing a brief to owning the problem. Evidence: a launch where you chose the message, a win-loss readout that changed a plan, enablement that reps use.
- **Senior to staff:** from one motion to the portfolio. Evidence: a positioning or pricing decision that held across products, an exec narrative the org repeated, a market call you made before it was obvious.
- **Each blind spot is the next test.** Junior: validate the message with five buyers. Senior: write your point of view before taking the sales request. Staff: sit in on ten sales calls a quarter.

## Self-assessment (score 0 to 2 per line, then read where you sit)
1. I choose the message, not just polish it. 2. I can name the buyer problem and show evidence. 3. I own a metric beyond output (adoption, win rate). 4. I run win-loss or call reviews and act on them. 5. I influence product and sales without authority. 6. I can set or challenge pricing and packaging. 7. I can tell the exec story in three minutes. 8. I question which market we should be in. Scores 0 to 5 junior-like, 6 to 11 senior-like, 12 to 16 staff-like. A rough guide, not a verdict.

## Limits of the graphic
- **Titles vary by company.** "Senior" at a start-up can be "staff" elsewhere; use the scope (one launch, one motion, the portfolio) rather than the label.
- **The stack row is a picture, not a standard.** Tools are examples; the graphic is published by a vendor-adjacent account, and "#1 LLM tool" is an opinion. Pick tools by need and data terms.
- **Metrics belong to the company.** Output counts and launch checklists can mislead; agree outcomes with your manager.
- **Levels are not tool collections.** Buying Gong does not make anyone staff.
