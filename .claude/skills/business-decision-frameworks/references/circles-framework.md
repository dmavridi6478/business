# CIRCLES Framework

*Authored directly for this repo — the parent skill's routing table pointed here, but no source content shipped with the install. This fills that gap.*

## What it is (and isn't)

CIRCLES is a PM-interview answer-structuring mnemonic (associated with Lewis C. Lin's *Decode and Conquer*) — **not an industry-standard strategic methodology.** Use it to structure a clear, complete answer to an open-ended product design/strategy question under time pressure (interview or otherwise), not as a substitute for real discovery, research, or a strategic framework when time isn't constrained.

## The seven steps

| Letter | Step | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **C** | Comprehend the situation | Restate the problem in your own words; ask clarifying questions about scope, constraints, and the specific user/context before proposing anything |
| **I** | Identify the customer | Name who specifically you're designing for — not "users" broadly. Different customer segments often want conflicting things |
| **R** | Report customer needs | State the underlying need/job-to-be-done, not just a feature request — the "why," not just the "what" |
| **C** | Cut, through prioritization | Narrow to the 2-3 needs worth solving now, explicitly setting aside the rest — don't try to solve everything in one answer |
| **L** | List solutions | Brainstorm multiple candidate solutions per prioritized need before picking one — breadth before depth |
| **E** | Evaluate trade-offs | Weigh each candidate solution's pros/cons/costs/risks against the others — show the reasoning, not just the conclusion |
| **S** | Summarize recommendation | State a clear recommendation with the reasoning that led there — end with a decision, not an open list of options |

## How to use it

1. Walk the steps in order — the framework's value is largely in the sequence itself (comprehend before identifying, identify before listing solutions) since skipping ahead to solutions is the most common failure mode it's designed to prevent.
2. Narrate the step you're on out loud (in an interview) or as a visible section header (in a written answer) — this is a structuring tool, and making the structure visible is most of the point.
3. Don't over-invest time proportionally — Comprehend and Cut deserve real time; a long List-Solutions section with ten shallow ideas is weaker than three well-evaluated ones.
4. Treat "Evaluate trade-offs" as the differentiator step — most weak answers jump from a list of solutions straight to a recommendation without showing the trade-off reasoning that justifies picking one over the others.

## When not to use it

For a real strategic decision with time to do actual discovery, research, and validation, don't stop at CIRCLES — it's a structure for organizing a *response*, not a substitute for the underlying research a real decision requires. Use `product-strategy-session` or `saas-idea-validator` (if applicable to the situation) for that instead.
