# LEADERSHIP Cheatsheet

*Authored directly for this repo — the parent skill's routing table pointed here, but no source content shipped with the install. This fills that gap.*

## Caveat (per the parent skill — read this before using the acronym below)

This is the weakest of the five frameworks in this reference library: a mnemonic word-list with one line per letter, no underlying research model, no citations to a real leadership theory. **Use it only as a talking-points checklist** — for a team offsite discussion prompt, a self-reflection journaling exercise, or quick culture-language vocabulary. **Never use it as the basis of a formal leadership development plan or competency framework.** If the user needs the latter, push back and suggest a real model instead:

- **Situational Leadership** (Hersey & Blanchard) — adapting leadership style to a team member's competence/commitment level on a given task.
- **Kouzes & Posner's Five Practices of Exemplary Leadership** — model the way, inspire a shared vision, challenge the process, enable others to act, encourage the heart. Backed by decades of published research (*The Leadership Challenge*).
- **Zenger-Folkman competency model** — a data-derived set of leadership competencies clustered around character, personal capability, focus on results, interpersonal skills, and leading change.

## The mnemonic (talking-points use only)

| Letter | Stands for | One-line prompt |
|---|---|---|
| **L** | Listen | Do you create space for others to speak before you decide? |
| **E** | Empathize | Do you understand what a decision costs the people affected by it, not just its business logic? |
| **A** | Adapt | Do you flex your approach to the person/situation, or apply one style regardless of context? |
| **D** | Decide | Do you make timely calls with incomplete information, or stall waiting for certainty that won't come? |
| **E** | Empower | Do you delegate real authority, or only tasks with no decision-making attached? |
| **R** | Recognize | Do you name specific contributions publicly, or let good work go unacknowledged? |
| **S** | Support | Do people trust you'll back them when a reasonable decision doesn't work out? |
| **H** | Hold accountable | Do you address underperformance directly and promptly, or let it slide? |
| **I** | Inspire | Do people leave conversations with you more energized about the work, or less? |
| **P** | Persevere | Do you model steadiness through setbacks, or visibly transmit your own stress downward? |

## How to use it (within its limits)

- **Team offsite / retro prompt**: pick 2-3 letters, ask each person to self-rate 1-5 and share one example — fast, low-stakes, generates real conversation without pretending to be a formal assessment.
- **Self-reflection**: journal against each letter monthly to notice drift, not to produce a score to report anywhere formal.
- **Culture language**: borrow the vocabulary ("that was a good moment of Empowering the team") without treating the full acronym as a validated model.

Do not use it for: performance reviews, promotion criteria, 360-feedback instrument design, or any deliverable that will be treated as authoritative by HR or leadership — for those, use one of the three real models named above instead.
