# 5 simple presentation frameworks

Source: an infographic by Michael of Sollo (Batch 121), "How to give a great presentation". Attributions in brackets are standard attributions, not on the graphic.

| # | Framework | Best for (card) | Structure (card) | Limits (added) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The 10-20-30 rule (Guy Kawasaki) | Keeping a presentation focused and digestible | 10 slides maximum; 20 minutes maximum; 30-point font minimum | Written for investor pitches; a technical review or training may need more. Cut by message, not to hit the number |
| 2 | The Golden Circle (Simon Sinek) | Opening with purpose and inspiring action | Why: the belief or cause. How: your unique approach. What: the priorities or actions | Do not open with an empty "why"; it must be true and specific. Weak for status updates |
| 3 | The Rule of Three | Making your message memorable | Three main points, three supporting examples per point, three takeaways in the close | Three is a rhetorical habit, not a law; do not pad or drop a real fourth point |
| 4 | The STARR method | Backing up claims with proof; ideal for sharing real progress | Situation (context or challenge); Task (goal or problem); Action (what was done and why); Result (what changed); Reflection (what you would repeat or refine) | Results must be measured and sourced; STARR is a STAR interview technique plus reflection |
| 5 | What? So what? Now what? | Ending with clarity and a clear next step | What: share the facts plainly. So what: why it matters (impact, risk, opportunity). Now what: what decision is needed and who owns the next step | Commonly traced to reflective-practice models; the next-step owner must be a named person and a date |

## How to choose
- **Pitch or sales opener:** 2 (Golden Circle) to open, 3 (Rule of Three) for the body, 5 to close.
- **Board or project update:** 5 (What? So what? Now what?), with 4 (STARR) for each major result.
- **Investor or short pitch:** 1 (10-20-30) plus 3.
- **Case study or progress report:** 4 (STARR).
- Combine at most two frameworks; a deck that uses all five reads as a template.

## Checks before you present
1. One message per slide and one sentence stating the point of the whole talk.
2. Every number has a named source on the slide or in the notes.
3. The close names a decision or action, an owner and a date.
4. Time it aloud; cut until it fits with a margin for questions.
5. Accessibility: font size, contrast, no colour-only meaning.
