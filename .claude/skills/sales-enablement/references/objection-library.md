# Objection Library

*Authored directly for this repo — the original `sales-enablement` skill's routing table pointed here, but no source content shipped with the install. This fills that gap.*

Full library of common B2B objections by category, following the main skill's 5-field response framework (objection statement → why they say it → response approach → proof point → follow-up question). Fill in `[proof point]` and product-specific details before use — this library provides the reasoning structure, not fabricated metrics.

## Price

| Objection | Why they say it | Response approach | Follow-up question |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | Comparing sticker price, not total value or cost of inaction | Reframe to cost-of-problem vs. cost-of-solution; anchor on payback period, not monthly fee | "What would solving this be worth to you over the next 12 months?" |
| "No budget this quarter" | Genuine budget cycle constraint, or a soft no | Ask whether it's a timing issue or a priority issue — they require different responses | "If budget weren't the constraint, is this still the top priority to solve?" |
| "Competitor is cheaper" | Comparing list price without adjusting for scope/support/implementation differences | Compare total cost of ownership, not sticker price; name what's included that theirs isn't | "What's included in their price — implementation, support, and at what SLA?" |

## Timing

| Objection | Why they say it | Response approach | Follow-up question |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Not the right time" | Competing priority, or the pain isn't urgent enough yet in their mind | Quantify the cost of waiting explicitly — what does another quarter of the status quo cost them | "What would need to be true for this to become the right time?" |
| "Maybe next quarter" | Often a polite deferral rather than a real commitment | Get specific: what changes next quarter that doesn't hold true today | "What's driving the shift to next quarter specifically?" |
| "Too busy to implement" | Real bandwidth concern, especially from technical buyers | Address implementation burden directly — show the actual time cost and who does the work | "If implementation took [X] and we handled [Y], would timing still be the blocker?" |

## Competition

| Objection | Why they say it | Response approach | Follow-up question |
|---|---|---|---|
| "We already use X" | Switching cost concern, or genuinely satisfied | Don't disparage the incumbent — ask what's not working, if anything, before pitching a switch | "What's working well with X, and what would you change if you could?" |
| "What makes you different?" | Genuine evaluation question, or testing if you have a real answer | Lead with the one differentiator most relevant to their stated pain, not a full feature comparison | "Which of these differences matters most for what you're trying to solve?" |

## Authority

| Objection | Why they say it | Response approach | Follow-up question |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I need to check with my boss" | Genuine lack of authority, or a way to end the call without committing | Offer to join that conversation directly rather than relying on secondhand relay | "Would it help if I joined that conversation to answer questions directly?" |
| "The committee decides" | Real multi-stakeholder process | Map the committee: who's on it, what each member optimizes for, who has veto power | "Who's on the committee, and what does each of them care about most?" |

## Status quo

| Objection | Why they say it | Response approach | Follow-up question |
|---|---|---|---|
| "What we have works fine" | Genuine satisfaction, or unexamined pain that hasn't surfaced yet | Probe for the gap between "works" and "works well" — ask about workarounds, not the tool itself | "Walk me through what happens when [specific edge case] comes up." |
| "Not broken, don't fix it" | Risk aversion — change itself is the perceived cost, not the current tool | Reframe risk: the risk of standing still vs. the risk of change, with a low-friction path (pilot, phased rollout) | "What would a low-risk way to test this look like for you?" |

## Technical

| Objection | Why they say it | Response approach | Follow-up question |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Does it integrate with X?" | Real integration dependency, often a deal-breaker if unanswered | Answer directly and specifically — if it doesn't integrate, say so and offer the workaround, don't dodge | "How critical is that integration to day-one usage vs. something we could phase in?" |
| "Security concerns" | Real compliance/security review requirement, especially at larger companies | Route to your security documentation/certifications proactively rather than answering from memory | "What does your security review process typically require — can I get ahead of it?" |
| "Can it scale?" | Concern about growing past the product's current fit | Answer with specifics (current largest customer scale, architecture notes) rather than a general reassurance | "What scale are you planning for in the next 12-18 months?" |

## Formats

- **Quick-reference table** (for live calls): strip to Objection | One-line response | Proof point — must fit on one screen without scrolling.
- **Detailed doc** (for prep/training): full table above plus role-play scenarios — pair reps to practice each category live before a call, not just read the table.

## Keeping this current

Objections drift as the market, competitors, and product change. Review this library quarterly against actual call recordings/notes — objections reps report hearing that aren't in the table are the highest-priority additions.
