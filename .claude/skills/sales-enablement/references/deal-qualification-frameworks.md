# Deal Qualification Frameworks: MEDDPICC & BANT

*Authored directly for this repo — the original `sales-enablement` skill's routing table pointed here, but no source content shipped with the install. This fills that gap.*

## MEDDPICC (full breakdown)

An extension of the older MEDDIC framework, adding Competition and a second I (Identify pain vs. Implicate pain, depending on the source). Use it for complex, multi-stakeholder B2B deals where a wrong qualification call is expensive.

| Letter | Stands for | What to establish |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | Metrics | The quantifiable business outcome the buyer is chasing — a number they'll be measured on, not a vague goal |
| **E** | Economic buyer | The person who controls budget and can say yes without further approval — not the champion, not the end user |
| **D** | Decision criteria | The formal/informal criteria the buying committee will score vendors against |
| **D** | Decision process | The actual sequence of steps/approvals between now and signature — legal review, security review, procurement, etc. |
| **P** | Paper process | The contracting mechanics: who signs, what paperwork is required, how long procurement/legal typically takes at this company |
| **I** | Identify pain | The specific, named business pain driving urgency — not a generic category problem |
| **C** | Champion | An internal advocate with influence who will sell on your behalf when you're not in the room |
| **C** | Competition | Who else is being evaluated, including "do nothing" as a competitor |

### Scorecard approach

Score each letter 0-2 per deal:
- **0** — Unknown or unconfirmed
- **1** — Believed true but not verified by the buyer directly
- **2** — Confirmed directly by the buyer or the economic buyer

A deal scoring mostly 0s and 1s late in the sales cycle is a red flag regardless of how good the relationship feels — qualification gaps don't resolve themselves as a deal moves forward; they resolve when someone asks the question directly.

**Total score interpretation** (16 points max):
| Score | Read |
|---|---|
| 12+ | Well-qualified — proceed with confidence, focus energy on paper process/legal |
| 7-11 | Gaps remain — identify which letters are weak and go get them before investing more time |
| ≤6 | High risk — this deal may not be real; verify Economic Buyer and Metrics before investing further |

### When to use full MEDDPICC vs. lighter BANT

Use MEDDPICC when: the deal is complex, multi-stakeholder, enterprise-scale, or the sales cycle is long enough that qualification gaps compound.

## BANT (lighter framework)

Simpler, faster qualification for shorter cycles or lower-complexity deals.

| Letter | Stands for | What to establish |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | Budget | Do they have (or can get) budget for this category of spend |
| **A** | Authority | Is this person able to decide, or influence the decision meaningfully |
| **N** | Need | Is there a real, acknowledged problem this solves |
| **T** | Timeline | Is there a real forcing function or date driving urgency |

### When to use BANT instead

Use BANT when: the deal is self-serve or inside-sales-led, the sales cycle is short (days to a few weeks), or there's a single decision-maker rather than a buying committee. BANT is faster to run but misses the process/paper/competition dimensions that matter once a deal involves procurement or legal — don't force BANT onto a deal that's clearly become MEDDPICC-shaped as it grows.

## Choosing between them

| Signal | Framework |
|---|---|
| Single decision-maker, short cycle, low deal size | BANT |
| Buying committee, formal procurement/legal, longer cycle, higher deal size | MEDDPICC |
| Started as BANT but a committee/procurement process emerged | Upgrade to MEDDPICC mid-cycle rather than forcing the original framework |
