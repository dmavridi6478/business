# 5 Questions Executives Should Ask Their AI About Marketing

Source: infographic by David Manela, "Stop Reading Dashboards, Start Asking Better Questions." Use this when reviewing a marketing dashboard, drafting a CEO/board report, or challenging a KPI set that only reports blended averages.

## 1. Cohort Revenue, Not Blended Revenue
- **Ask:** How are recent cohorts performing compared to older ones?
- **What it is:** Revenue performance of recent customer cohorts vs older ones.
- **Why it matters:** This month's blended number lies. Cohort decay tells the truth — a business can show flat/growing blended revenue while new-cohort quality is quietly collapsing.
- **The question to put in front of the exec:** "Is the business growing on new customers, or living off old ones?"

## 2. Marginal CAC, Not Blended CAC
- **Ask:** What's the cost of acquiring our *next* customer, not the average?
- **What it is:** The cost of the next customer, not the average across all of them.
- **Why it matters:** Blended CAC hides where the team is scaling waste — the marginal customer is almost always more expensive than the average one, and only the marginal figure tells you if you're still profitable to keep scaling.
- **The question to put in front of the exec:** "Is marginal CAC climbing faster than LTV?"

## 3. Payback Period, By Channel
- **Ask:** How long until each channel pays back?
- **What it is:** How long until each channel's dollar comes back.
- **Why it matters:** Boards think in cash returns, not ratios — a channel with a great ROAS but an 18-month payback can still break the company's cash runway.
- **The question to put in front of the exec:** "Is payback stretching across the channels that fund the business?"

## 4. Contribution Margin Trend
- **Ask:** What's left of marketing revenue after variable costs?
- **What it is:** What's left of marketing revenue after variable costs.
- **Why it matters:** This is the real P&L story underneath the revenue line — top-line growth funded by shrinking contribution margin is not growth, it's deferred bad news.
- **The question to put in front of the exec:** "Are these dollars funding growth or getting eaten by costs?"

## 5. Channel Concentration
- **Ask:** What percentage of acquisition comes from our top one or two channels?
- **What it is:** The percentage of acquisition coming from the top one or two channels. Watch for anything over 60%.
- **Why it matters:** This is the silent killer in every board deck — concentration risk doesn't show up until the top channel breaks (algorithm change, platform policy shift, cost spike).
- **The question to put in front of the exec:** "What breaks if the top channel goes away tomorrow?"

## Using this as a report-QA tool

When asked to review, structure, or challenge a marketing report before it goes to a CEO or board, run these five questions against the report as a checklist. If the report only shows blended revenue, blended CAC, and channel-agnostic ROAS, say explicitly that it fails the marginal/cohort test — don't just add the missing metrics silently. The value of this framework is naming the failure mode, not just supplying better charts.
