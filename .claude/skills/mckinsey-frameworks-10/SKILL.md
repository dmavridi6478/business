# Ten McKinsey frameworks

Source: Natan Mohart's ten one-page graphics. They are a third party's summaries of McKinsey frameworks, not McKinsey's own publications. Treat the quoted statistics and company cases as the graphics' claims. The arithmetic in the worked examples was recomputed and is right (GE-McKinsey scores 3.40 and 3.05; cost-curve price $50 to $36 is -28%; market cap $20B x 1.5 = $6B x 5 = $30B).

Details, questions and red flags for each: `references/frameworks.md`. The 7S model already has its own skill, `mckinsey-7s-model`.

## Pick the framework by the problem

| Problem you have | Use | One-line output |
|---|---|---|
| A change programme isn't changing behaviour | Influence Model | Four statements scored 1-5; fill the weakest lever |
| Marketing spend feels misplaced | Consumer Decision Journey | The stage where you lose most vs your top rival |
| Everything is short-term, nothing is being built | Three Horizons | Budget and leadership hours per horizon |
| You need to find a different way to win | Business System | The one step where you can make a choice no rival makes |
| Strategy is clear but nothing executes | 7S | Score matrix and red links |
| The future is uncertain and you must choose bets | Portfolio of Initiatives | No-regrets, options and big bets, plotted |
| Who could buy you, or whom could you buy | Strategic Control Map | Peer plot with zone and move |
| Predict price, price wars or who exits | Industry Cost Curve | Price per demand scenario, exits first |
| Where to invest, hold or exit across businesses | GE-McKinsey Matrix | Weighted scores and a zone per unit |
| Why an industry is (un)profitable and what happens after a shock | SCP | Structure, conduct, performance and the feedback loop |

## Method for any of them
1. State the decision the framework must inform, in one sentence. If there is none, stop: a framework without a decision is a poster.
2. Gather numbers, not adjectives. Where the card says "estimate", say what the number's source is.
3. Agree the scoring or weights before anyone scores (GE-McKinsey, 7S, Influence survey).
4. Run the framework's own 4-5 steps (in the reference) and write the "ask your team" answers.
5. Name what would make the output wrong (each framework lists where it misleads).
6. End with one action, an owner and a review date.

## Cautions
- These are lenses. A neat matrix can hide bad inputs; weights that are subjective produce scores that are subjective.
- Several frameworks (Control Map, Cost Curve) need data you may not have. Say so rather than invent it.
- Do not present a framework's output as McKinsey advice.
