# LinkedIn client acquisition in one page (16 cards, animated)

Source: an animated GIF of a 16-card sheet ending "Comment ASSET to get the guide". The statistics inside the mock-ups (acceptance rate, views, leads per week, +214% inbound DMs) change between frames, so they are **illustrative, not evidence**. Nothing was commented.

| # | Card | The idea as drawn |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | The 5-layer client acquisition stack | Profile, content, outreach, DMs, calls: build from the bottom |
| 02 | Turn your profile into a landing page | Headline, banner and featured section that sell before you speak |
| 03 | Define your ideal client in one line | One buyer, one pain, one outcome; ignore the rest |
| 04 | Connection requests that get accepted | About 20 a day to your ICP, personal note, no pitch |
| 05 | Posts that pull buyers, not just likes | Write about the buyer's problems; inbound follows relevance |
| 06 | The 4-message DM framework | Context, value, question, call; never pitch in message one |
| 07 | The lead magnet funnel | One genuinely useful PDF turns silent readers into named leads |
| 08 | Comment-to-DM: the keyword play | Ask for a keyword, deliver in DMs, start a conversation |
| 09 | Sales Navigator searches that find buyers | Filter by role, size, recent activity; keep the 5% that fit |
| 10 | Book the call inside the DM | Offer two times, not a link; confirm in the same thread |
| 11 | Your first 30 days, week by week | Profile, content, outreach, calls, one layer per week |
| 12 | The weekly acquisition routine | About 45 minutes a day: write, comment, send, follow up, review |
| 13 | The 6 numbers that predict revenue | Reach, accept, reply, call, proposal, won; fix the leakiest step first |
| 14 | Social proof that closes deals | Named results and screenshots beat claims |
| 15 | From proposal to signed client | Recap, one offer, one price, signed within 48 hours |
| 16 | The client acquisition checklist | Profile sells the outcome, ICP in one line, 3 posts a week, 20 DMs a day, 2 calls booked |

## Cautions
- **[Certain]** Automated connection requests and DMs, and comment-to-DM bots, can breach LinkedIn's terms and risk account restriction; keep volume human and personal.
- **[Likely]** "20 requests a day" and "20 DMs a day" are the author's rule of thumb, not a platform limit; weekly invitation limits apply.
