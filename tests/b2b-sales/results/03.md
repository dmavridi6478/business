- Of the 5 hot test leads, 3 converted. Those 3 are 3 of the 9 test conversions [calc: 3/9], so the hot band captured one third of conversions and 6 of 9 happened below the hot threshold.
- The order hot > warm > cold holds in the test set, which is the direction we want. But the hot band has only 5 leads, so 3 of 5 could easily be luck.
- Weak points, stated plainly: only a third of test conversions were caught by the hot threshold; the test sample is 24 leads; the test leads are the newest and may not have finished the 120-day window (see Data check).
- This does not show predictive accuracy. It shows the rules are not contradicted by the held-out leads.

## Monitoring plan

- Re-run every quarter with new leads, keeping the same time-ordered split.
- Compare the hot band's conversion with all other leads, as counts. Flag drift if the hot band's rate is no longer above the rest.
- Record the date of every behaviour (pricing page visit, demo request) so decay and "behaviour came before the decision" can be tested.
- Wait until each lead has had the full 120 days before labelling it a non-conversion.
- Re-check the ignored values (Finance Manager, Owner, cold chain, 1000+ band) as they reach 10 leads.

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

- [assumption] `days_since_first_touch` is the age of the lead at extract, used to order leads in time.
- [assumption] Points multiplier of 5.
- [assumption] Decay half-life of 30 days.
- Unknown: the extract date, what `days_since_first_touch` means for converted leads, the dates of behaviours, lead source cost, sales capacity.
- The 120-day window may be unfinished for many leads, so some zeros may be unlabelled future conversions.
- Several attributes were tested at once on 56 leads, so some lifts will look good by chance.
- Most likely to be wrong: the hot threshold and the weights for 3PL, inbound and referral, because each rests on 3 to 5 conversions.
