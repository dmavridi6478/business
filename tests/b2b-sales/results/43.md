## Patterns

Reported with counts, small subgroups flagged.

1. By deal value, the losses split cleanly. All 3 of the $30,000 losses were capability or attestation losses (L2, L5: SAP connector; L7: SOC 2). The 6 $18,000 losses were size, price, or no decision (L1, L6 too small; L3, L9 price; L4, L8 no decision). Counts: 3 of 3 and 6 of 6 [calc]. Signal only: n = 3 and 6.
2. The three capability or attestation losses ran long: 140, 92 and 130 days open, an average of 120.7 days [calc: (140 + 92 + 130) / 3]. The five $30,000 wins averaged 102.8 days [calc: (95 + 120 + 88 + 101 + 110) / 5]. The measures differ (days_open against cycle_days) and n is 3 against 5, so this is only a hint that these deals consumed time before failing.
3. Both "no decision" losses were open longer than any $18,000 win: 75 and 80 days against a longest $18,000 win of 62 days [calc: max of 62, 48, 55, 41, 57, 39, 44]. Their notes point to internal causes: reorganisation, and no budget owner. n = 2.
4. "Too small": 2 of 9 losses, both with an Owner as buyer, 90 and 70 employees, lost within 30 and 25 days. No won deal has fewer than 210 employees or an Owner as buyer [calc: 0 of 12]. n = 2: a signal, not a rule.
5. Price: 2 of 9 losses, both $18,000 deals with a Finance Manager as buyer. The Finance Manager group also has 3 wins (W7, W10, W12), so Finance Manager deals were 3 won and 2 lost [calc: 3 of 5 won]. L3's note says the cheaper tool lacked an exception queue, so price was not the only difference.

Reasons and attributes that appear in both wins and losses and do not discriminate:
- Region: Greece in 6 of 12 wins and 4 of 9 losses [calc: counts].
- Buyer role: Finance Director (4 won, 2 lost), Finance Manager (3 won, 2 lost), CFO (2 won, 2 lost), Head of Operations (3 won, 1 lost). Only Owner is lost-only (0 won, 2 lost).

- Deal value: $30,000 deals 5 won and 3 lost (62.5% [calc: 5/8]); $18,000 deals 7 won and 6 lost (53.8% [calc: 7/13]).

Win notes and loss notes are different kinds of record (a situation against a stated reason), so reason categories cannot be compared between the two groups.

## Changes to test

At most three.

1. Ask about ERP and security attestation requirements in the first call of every $30,000-tier deal. Measure: share of $30,000 deals with ERP type and attestation requirement recorded by day 30 (baseline not recorded in the files); and days open at loss for capability losses (baseline 120.7 days, 3 deals [calc]).
2. In $18,000-tier deals, confirm the budget owner and decision process by a set point in the cycle. Measure: "no decision" outcomes among $18,000 deals (baseline 2 of 13 decided $18,000 deals [calc: 2/13]) and their days open (baseline 75 and 80).
3. Record, for every decided deal, the buyer's own stated reason with its source (interview or email) and the seller's belief separately. Measure: share of decided deals with both recorded (baseline 0 of 21 [calc]).

## Limits

- Sample: 12 won and 9 lost; subgroups of 1 to 6 deals.
- Notes: author unknown, so buyer-stated reasons cannot be separated from seller inference. No buyer interviews were supplied, so nothing is known about who was interviewed or declined.
- Seller beliefs: not recorded.
- Different fields: wins have cycle_days and source; losses have days_open and loss_reason. Win notes record the situation; loss notes record a reason.
- Completeness: not stated whether these are all decided deals; win rates depend on that.
- No dates, so no trend over time.
- Bias: the loss notes may be written after the fact by the seller, which would favour reasons the seller can name (price, product) over buyer reasons not discussed.
- Individual buyers are not named; roles only.

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

Assumptions:
- My coding of notes into categories and situations (flagged where a reader could differ) [assumption].
- My reading of which notes sound like buyer statements [assumption].

Unknowns that change the conclusion: who wrote each note; whether the files are complete; what drove the wins from the buyer's side.

Most likely to make this wrong: the $30,000 versus $18,000 split in pattern 1. It rests on 3 and 6 deals, and a single reclassification (for example L7 as product gap or trust) does not change it, but one more large deal lost for a different reason would.
