## Learning goals

Draft, internal call preparation. Persona: a new Finance Director at a road freight company. Call length: 30 minutes. The three unknowns you named are G1 to G3. G4 and G5 are added so the sequence runs from current state to problem, outcome and decision.

- **G1. How invoices are matched today** (your unknown 1): steps, tools, people, and what happens to an invoice that does not match.
- **G2. How many invoices and how many mismatches** (your unknown 2): monthly volume, the share that does not match, and where the buyer's figure comes from.
- **G3. Impact:** where the invoice work shows up for the buyer (close, carrier disputes, audits or elsewhere). The persona file lists slow month-end close, carrier billing disputes and audit pressure as pains reported in public reviews and past deals. For this buyer these are hypotheses, not facts. Do not name them in a question; listen for them.
- **G4. Desired outcome:** what a good result looks like in the buyer's words and how they would recognise it.
- **G5. Who else decides** (your unknown 3): who else has a say, how a decision like this gets made, and what the buyer still has to find out, since they are new in the role.

## Question sequence

Opening, 2 minutes `[assumption]`: thank them, confirm the 30 minutes, ask what they would like from the call. Then seven main questions, about 3.5 minutes each `[calc: 7 x 3.5 = 24.5]`, then the closing question and wrap-up, 3 minutes `[assumption]`. Total `[calc: 2 + 24.5 + 3 = 29.5]` minutes. This follows the 3 to 4 minutes per main question rule `[assumption]`.

| # | Goal | Main question (open, single) | Purpose |
|---|------|------------------------------|---------|
| 1 | G1 | "How are carrier invoices handled in your team from the moment they arrive?" | Gets the current process in the buyer's own sequence, without naming a method. |
| 2 | G2 | "What does a typical month look like in terms of carrier invoice volume?" | Gets the volume and its range. |
| 3 | G1 | "What happens when an invoice does not match the shipment record?" | Gets the exception path, its owners and its tools. |
| 4 | G2 | "What share of a typical month's invoices end up not matching, as best you can tell?" | Gets the mismatch rate and how firm it is. |
| 5 | G3 | "Where, if anywhere, does this invoice work show up in your month-end?" | Lets the buyer name the impact, or say there is none. |
| 6 | G4 | "What would a good result look like for you?" | Gets the outcome and success measure in the buyer's words. |
| 7 | G5 | "Who else would have a say in a decision like this?" | Gets the other decision-makers. |

Order follows the method: context and current state (1 to 4), problem and impact (5), desired outcome (6), decision process (7).

## Probes

One probe per main question. Use it only after the buyer has answered.

| # | Probe |
|---|-------|
| 1 | "What do you use to check an invoice against its shipment?" |
| 2 | "How does that change across the year?" |
| 3 | "Can you take me through the last one?" |
| 4 | "Where does that figure come from?" |
| 5 | "Can you give me an example from the last close?" |
| 6 | "How would you know it had happened?" |
| 7 | "What would you need to find out to know who should be involved?" |

Listen for, but do not prompt: how long the close takes, carrier disputes, audit work, and who has been asked already. These come from the persona hypotheses in G3.

Not to do:
- Do not pitch or describe any product capability on this call.
- Do not fill a silence; wait after each answer and repeat their last key words if more is needed.
- Do not use leading wording such as "Don't you find..." or "You'd agree...".
- Do not stack two questions in one turn.
- Do not ask what is public: the company's size, location, or fleet.
- Do not treat this as a fixed script; if the buyer opens with one of these topics, reorder.

## What to cut first

Cut in this order if the call runs over. Never cut questions 1, 2, 4 and 7, because they answer your three stated unknowns.

1. All probes except those on questions 1, 4 and 7.
2. Question 5 (impact). Cover it in a follow-up.
3. Question 6 (outcome). Ask it by email or on the next call.
4. Question 3 (exception path), only if question 1's probe has already covered it.

If you cut 5 or 6, say so to the buyer and propose when you will pick it up.

## Closing question

"What haven't I asked about that you think I should know?"

It covers anything outside the seven main questions. Take notes, do not respond to it with a pitch.

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

Assumptions:
- `[assumption]` One main question per 3 to 4 minutes, taken from the skill method; 3.5 minutes used.
- `[assumption]` 2 minutes for the opening and 3 minutes for the closing question and wrap-up.
- The persona's pains (slow month-end close, carrier billing disputes, audit pressure) are treated as hypotheses about this buyer, not as known facts.
- Tallyleaf's product category (invoice matching and freight audit) is known from the company file, but no product detail is used in the questions.

Unknowns that change the output:
- How new the buyer is. A very new Finance Director may not know the volume or mismatch figures. Questions 2 and 4 then need a fallback: "Who would know?"
- Whether the buyer has other people joining the call. Only one attendee was assumed.

Most likely to be wrong: that the buyer can give invoice volume and mismatch rate from memory. If they cannot, expect to leave with G2 still open and a named person to ask.
