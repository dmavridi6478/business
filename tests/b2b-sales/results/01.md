## Sample and confidence

- Won deals: 12 (W1 to W12). Lost deals: 9 (L1 to L9).
- 12 won deals meets the minimum of 10, so this is a profile and not only a hypothesis. The lost sample is small: no loss reason appears in more than 2 of the 9 lost deals, so every exclusion below is provisional.
- Not supplied: churn, retention or expansion data. "Best third" is therefore profiled on sales-cycle length only. That is a speed measure, not an outcome-quality measure.
- The lost file may not hold every lost opportunity, so I do not compute a win rate for the whole pipeline.

## Discriminating attributes

**Common to most won deals**
- 200 to 1,000 employees: 11 of 12 [calc: 11/12].
- Located in Greece, Cyprus, Bulgaria, Romania or Italy: 12 of 12 [calc: 12/12].
- Buyer holds a finance title (Finance Director, Finance Manager or CFO): 9 of 12 [calc: 9/12].

**Common to won and lost alike (does not discriminate)**
- Industry mix. Road freight is 4 of 12 won and 3 of 9 lost. Sea freight is 3 of 12 won and 2 of 9 lost.
- Finance-titled buyer: 9 of 12 won and 6 of 9 lost [calc: 6/9].
- Greece as region: 6 of 12 won and 4 of 9 lost.
- 200 to 1,000 employees is also common in lost deals: 5 of 9 [calc: 5/9]. Being in the size band does not by itself win a deal.
- Deal value mix (18,000 versus 30,000).

**Rare in won, present in lost (small counts, provisional)**
- Under 200 employees: 0 of 12 won, 2 of 9 lost. Both lost deals were closed as "too small". Two deals is below the three-deal threshold for a rule.
- Owner as buyer: 0 of 12 won, 2 of 9 lost. These are the same two deals (L1, L6), so this is not independent evidence.
- Germany: 0 of 12 won, 1 of 9 lost (L2). One deal only. It is an observation, not a pattern.
- Air freight: 0 of 12 won, 1 of 9 lost (the same deal, L2).

**Best third by short cycle (4 of 12 deals; no retention data)**
- The four shortest cycles are W10 (39 days), W7 (41), W12 (44) and W3 (48).
- All four are inbound, all worth 18,000, all 210 to 380 employees, and all have a Finance Director or Finance Manager as buyer [calc: 4/4 on each].
- The four longest cycles are W4 (120), W11 (110), W8 (101) and W2 (95). All four are outbound and worth 30,000 [calc: 4/4]. Their buyers are CFO (W4, W8) or Head of Operations (W11, W2).
- Short cycle is not proof of a better customer.

## ICP statement

Built only from the attribute table.

- **Firmographic fit:** a freight or logistics company (road, sea, 3PL, courier or cold chain all appear in the won deals) with 200 to 1,000 employees [calc: 11/12 of won deals] in Greece, Cyprus, Bulgaria, Romania or Italy [calc: 12/12 of won deals].
- **Situation:** carrier invoice errors or disputes, manual invoice matching that strains a small finance team, or outside scrutiny of freight costs (see Triggers).
- **Buyer roles:** a finance title (Finance Director, Finance Manager or CFO) in 9 of 12 won deals; Head of Operations in 3 of 12.
- **Two observed sub-profiles (provisional):**
  - 210 to 520 employees, Finance Director or Finance Manager buyer, deal value 18,000 [calc: 7/7 deals].
  - 640 to 1,200 employees, CFO or Head of Operations buyer, deal value 30,000 [calc: 5/5 deals].
- **Disqualifiers:** see Exclusions.

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

- [assumption] Deal values are in USD, as the column name says. W2's note mentions "EUR 40k"; I did not convert it and it is not used in any total.
- [assumption] The lost file is all lost deals in the same period as the won file. If it is a sample, the comparison of won and lost is biased.
- [assumption] The three trigger themes are my grouping of free-text notes. A different grouping could change the counts.
- Unknown: ERP system of the won customers (matters for the SAP exclusion), lead source of lost deals, any churn or retention data.
- Most likely to be wrong: the exclusions. Each rests on 2 deals, so one more deal in either direction would change it.
