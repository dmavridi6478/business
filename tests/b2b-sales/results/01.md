## ICP statement

Using only attributes in the table above (working profile from 12 wins and 9 losses):

- Firmographic fit: freight and logistics companies (road freight, sea freight, 3PL, courier or cold chain) with 250 to 1,000 employees (10 of 12 wins), located in Greece, Cyprus, Bulgaria, Romania or Italy (12 of 12 wins).
- Buyer roles: Finance Director, Finance Manager or CFO (9 of 12); Head of Operations (3 of 12, all in sea freight).
- Situation: carrier invoice control problems of the kinds listed under Triggers: charge errors or disputes (4), audit or cost-visibility demand (3), manual matching load (2), system or staff change (2).
- Deal shape: $18,000 (7 of 12) or $30,000 (5 of 12). All five $30,000 wins were sea freight or 3PL and ran longer.
- Disqualifiers: see Exclusions.

## Exclusions

- Under 250 employees: 3 lost, 1 won (W12 at 210). Two of the losses are noted "too small" (L1 "Four invoices a week; spreadsheet is fine.", L6 "Handles invoices in the accounting package."), at 90 and 70 employees. Rests on 3 lost deals.
- Needs a native SAP connector: L2 "Needed a native SAP connector." and L5 "Their ERP is SAP; wanted a native connector." (2 deals). company.md lists no native SAP connector. Rests on 2 deals plus the product fact.
- Requires a SOC 2 report: L7 "Required SOC 2 report; we had none." (1 deal). company.md says no SOC 2 report is mentioned anywhere. One deal: flag early in qualification, not a rule.
- Not profile exclusions, but qualification checks: price (L3, L9: 2 of 9) and no decision / no budget owner (L4, L8: 2 of 9).
- Not excluded despite one loss each: air freight, Germany (L2 only).

## Validation test

Before outcomes are known, tag the next 20 opportunities as in profile (250 to 1,000 employees, five countries, Finance-function or Head of Operations buyer) or out of profile. When they close, compare won share in vs out. With 20 opportunities a difference of a few deals is weak evidence; repeat on the next batch before changing targeting. Record ERP and lead source on lost deals so those attributes can be tested.

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

- [assumption] The two files contain every closed deal in the period; if not, won shares are biased.
- [assumption] Trigger groups are my classification of free-text notes; a different grouping changes the counts.
- Unknowns that change the conclusion: ERP of won customers (SAP exclusion untestable); lead source of lost deals; retention and expansion (so "best customers" is not tested); whether the 250 boundary or a lower one is right (one win at 210).
- Most likely wrong: the 250-employee boundary and the "owner buyer" exclusion, because both rest on 3 or fewer lost deals, and 4 lost deals still sit inside the 250 to 1,000 band.
