- Of 6 accounts rated red, 3 churned: 50% [calc: 3/6].

- The 6 accounts not rated red had 2 churners: 33% [calc: 2/6]. Red at 50% against 33% outside red is a weak separation on counts this small. The overall test churn rate is 42% [calc: 5/12].
- Both green accounts stayed, but 2 accounts is too few to rely on.

Signal by signal in the test set, shown for information only because every group is under 10 accounts:
- Tickets 6 or more: 3 of 6 churned. Tickets 5 or fewer: 2 of 6. The training gap (48 points) shrank to 17 points [calc: 50 minus 33].
- Utilisation below 0.60: 5 of 7 churned. 0.60 or above: 0 of 5. This signal held up.
- Sponsor not active: 2 of 3 churned. Sponsor active: 3 of 9.

The tickets signal weakened and utilisation strengthened, which is the kind of change expected from small groups. It is not a reason to re-weight on 12 accounts.

## Limits

- Small data: 40 accounts, test set of 12 with 5 churn events. The test neither confirms nor rejects the model. Treat it as a first version.
- The split is by assumed account order, not by dates.
- Signals may be measured after the decision to leave (a ticket spike or a drop in use can follow the churn decision). The gap between signal and outcome is not supplied.
- Churn is not defined in the file: no period, and no distinction between cancel and non-renewal.
- Contract value is not supplied, so a red band cannot be ranked by revenue at risk.
- Product changes over time are not captured.
- Association is not cause. Fixing a signal (for example raising seat use) may not prevent churn.
- Training rates are not accuracy. Only the test counts above speak to performance.

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

**Assumptions**
- Account numbers C1 to C40 follow time order [assumption].
- The 20-point rule for keeping a signal [assumption].
- The utilisation cut at the training median, 0.60 [assumption], and the ticket cut at 6 [assumption].
- The 5 and 10 working-day review windows [assumption].

**Unknowns that change the conclusion**
- True dates, to make the split genuinely by time.
- The meaning and timing of "churned" and when each signal was measured.
- Contract values.

**Most likely to make this wrong:** the time ordering. If account numbers do not follow sign-up order, the test set is not a later sample and the 50% and 60% figures say little. The second risk is the very small test set: a change of one account moves a rate by 8 to 17 points.
