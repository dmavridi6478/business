## The claim

"The total addressable market for freight-audit software in Greece and the EU is 5 billion dollars, so winning even 1 percent gives us 50 million dollars in revenue." Source: slide 7 of the internal planning deck, no citation given.

Built top-down: a market figure with no stated method, then a percentage of it. The claim's geography is Greece and the EU; the segment you sell to (below) is five countries only.

## Segment definition

Logistics companies with 200 to 2,000 employees in Greece, Cyprus, Bulgaria, Romania and Italy (from the inputs).

Account count: not known. The only source is a trade association member list of 1,150 companies in the five countries (list dated 2025-12) that does not say how many are in the size band. A member list may also miss non-members. So the in-band count is a range:

- Low: 10% of the list = 115 accounts [calc: 1,150 x 0.10] [assumption: 10%].
- Base: 30% = 345 accounts [calc: 1,150 x 0.30] [assumption: 30%].
- High: 60% = 690 accounts [calc: 1,150 x 0.60] [assumption: 60%].
- Hard ceiling from the list: all 1,150 in band (100%).

## Bottom-up build

Accounts x annual price per account. Prices from the inputs: Starter $18,000, Growth $30,000. Base price is the midpoint $24,000 [calc: (18,000 + 30,000) / 2] [assumption: an even Starter/Growth mix].

- Low TAM: 115 x $18,000 = $2,070,000 [calc: 115 x 18,000].
- Base TAM: 345 x $24,000 = $8,280,000 [calc: 345 x 24,000].
- High TAM: 690 x $30,000 = $20,700,000 [calc: 690 x 30,000].
- Ceiling: 1,150 x $30,000 = $34,500,000 [calc: 1,150 x 30,000].

Adoption (share of accounts that would ever buy), for the obtainable figure: low 5%, base 15%, high 30% [assumption: all three].

## Gap versus claim

- Claim TAM $5,000,000,000 vs bottom-up base $8,280,000: the claim is 604 times the base [calc: 5,000,000,000 / 8,280,000 = 603.9]; 242 times the high case [calc: 5,000,000,000 / 20,700,000 = 241.5]; 145 times even the ceiling [calc: 5,000,000,000 / 34,500,000 = 144.9].
- Claimed revenue $50,000,000 exceeds the entire ceiling TAM of $34,500,000 by a factor of 1.45 [calc: 50,000,000 / 34,500,000 = 1.449]. Even if every listed company bought Growth, 1% of anything cannot be $50 million in this segment.
- Caveat: the claim covers "Greece and the EU", wider than the five countries. The inputs hold no account count outside the five countries, so I cannot test the wider market.

## Capacity check

- Seller capacity: 3 sellers x $600,000 quota = $1,800,000 a year [calc: 3 x 600,000].
- Versus claimed revenue of $50,000,000: the claim is 27.8 times capacity [calc: 50,000,000 / 1,800,000 = 27.78].
- Versus base obtainable $1,242,000: capacity is 1.45 times that figure [calc: 1,800,000 / 1,242,000 = 1.449]. Versus base TAM: capacity equals 21.7% of it [calc: 1,800,000 / 8,280,000]. Quota capacity per year is therefore already large compared with the bottom-up segment.
- One-year view only; multi-year renewals were not modelled.

## Verdict

Unsupported as stated, and overstated where it can be tested: for the segment you actually sell to, the bottom-up base is about 1/600 of the claim. The single most influential input is the number of companies in the 200-2,000 employee band across the five countries (the 115 to 690 range is a sixfold spread [calc: 690 / 115 = 6.0]); but the verdict on the $50 million does not depend on it, because even the whole list at Growth price gives $34.5 million. Only a larger geographic scope than the five countries could rescue the claim, and nothing in the inputs supports that.
