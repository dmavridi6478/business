## The claim

> "The total addressable market for freight-audit software in Greece and the EU is 5 billion dollars, so winning even 1 percent gives us 50 million dollars in revenue."

Source: slide 7 of the internal planning deck, no citation given.

How it was built: top-down. A market figure with no cited source, multiplied by a percentage (5,000,000,000 x 1% = 50,000,000 [calc]). There is no count of customers and no price behind it. "Winning even 1 percent" is not a method, so I do not test it further and rebuild the number bottom-up instead.

## Segment definition

A customer is a logistics company with 200 to 2,000 employees located in Greece, Cyprus, Bulgaria, Romania or Italy.

Count of what qualifies:
- Counted source: a trade association member list of 1,150 companies in the five countries (list dated 2025-12). It does not say how many are in the 200 to 2,000 employee band.
- The share in the band is unknown, so I use a range [assumption]: low 20%, base 30%, high 40% of the list.
  - Low: 230 accounts [calc: 1,150 x 0.20]
  - Base: 345 accounts [calc: 1,150 x 0.30]
  - High: 460 accounts [calc: 1,150 x 0.40]
- Even if every company on the list qualified, the count would be 1,150, and that is a member list, so companies that are not members are not in it. The true population could be larger. The size of that undercount is not known.

## Bottom-up build

Revenue per account per year, from your prices: Starter 18,000 dollars and Growth 30,000 dollars.
- Low: 18,000 (all Starter).
- High: 30,000 (all Growth).
- Base: 24,000 [calc: (18,000 + 30,000) / 2] [assumption: an even mix of the two plans].

Bottom-up market if every qualifying account bought (annual, per year):
- Low: 230 x 18,000 = 4,140,000 dollars [calc: 230 x 18,000]
- Base: 345 x 24,000 = 8,280,000 dollars [calc: 345 x 24,000]
- High: 460 x 30,000 = 13,800,000 dollars [calc: 460 x 30,000]

Adoption: the share of qualifying accounts that would buy dedicated freight-audit software (rather than a spreadsheet, an ERP module or a competitor). Not in the inputs. Range [assumption]: low 20%, base 40%, high 60%.

## Gap versus claim

- Claim divided by bottom-up base: 5,000,000,000 / 8,280,000 = about 604 [calc]. Against the high case: about 362 [calc: 5,000,000,000 / 13,800,000]. Against the low case: about 1,208 [calc: 5,000,000,000 / 4,140,000].
- The claim's "1 percent" revenue of 50,000,000 is about 3.6 times the high bottom-up market with every account buying [calc: 50,000,000 / 13,800,000], and about 15 times the base revenue from adopting accounts [calc: 50,000,000 / 3,312,000].
- To reach 5,000,000,000 at the Growth price of 30,000, the segment would need 166,667 accounts [calc: 5,000,000,000 / 30,000], which is about 145 times the only counted list of 1,150 [calc: 166,667 / 1,150].
- Scope differs: the claim covers "Greece and the EU" and does not mention company size, while the segment here is five countries and 200 to 2,000 employees. A wider scope could explain part of the gap, but the inputs hold no counts for the wider scope, so I cannot say how much. The comparison that matters for your sellers is the five-country segment.

## Capacity check

- Seller capacity: 3 sellers x 600,000 dollars quota = 1,800,000 dollars a year [calc: 3 x 600,000] [assumption: quota is new annual contract value].
- Years of full quota needed to take the base revenue from adopting accounts (3,312,000): 1.8 years [calc: 3,312,000 / 1,800,000]. For the low case (828,000): 0.5 years [calc: 828,000 / 1,800,000]. For the high case (8,280,000): 4.6 years [calc: 8,280,000 / 1,800,000].
- The claimed 50,000,000 would take 27.8 years of full quota [calc: 50,000,000 / 1,800,000].
- Meaning: at the base case, three sellers would run out of adopting accounts in under two years if they hit quota. The segment looks too small for this headcount and quota, which is a planning problem even if the TAM claim were ignored. This ignores renewals, churn and expansion, which the inputs do not cover.

## Verdict

**Overstated**, and also unsupported as written because it has no citation.

The single input that would change the verdict most is the number of qualifying companies. The claim needs 166,667 accounts at the Growth price [calc: 5,000,000,000 / 30,000]; the only counted list has 1,150. Counting the list by employee band, and finding out how many companies are not on it, would settle the account count. Adoption has the widest range of the three inputs (20% to 60%), but even 100% adoption in the high case gives 13,800,000, far below the claim.

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

- [assumption] Share of the member list in the 200 to 2,000 band: 20%, 30%, 40%.
- [assumption] Even mix of Starter and Growth for the base price: 24,000.
- [assumption] Adoption of 20%, 40%, 60%.
- [assumption] Quota is new annual contract value, not total revenue.
- Unknown: the size band of each company on the member list, the number of companies not on it, the account counts for the rest of the EU, implementation fees, any churn or expansion.
- The member list is dated 2025-12 and may be out of date.
- Most likely to be wrong: the account count. The 1,150-member list is neither a guaranteed ceiling (non-members are missing) nor a count of the size band; only a count by employee band will fix it.
