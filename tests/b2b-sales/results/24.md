## Question audit

The eight questions below are quoted exactly as the seller asked them. The classification is based on the wording alone: no transcript, answers, call goal or persona were supplied.

| # | Question (verbatim) | Type | Why |
|---|---------------------|------|-----|
| 1 | "Don't you think your close process is too slow?" | Leading | Contains the answer ("too slow") and invites agreement. |
| 2 | "What tools do you use today and how many people work on invoices and what is your budget?" | Stacked | Three questions: tools, people, budget. |
| 3 | "Is accuracy important to you?" | Closed | Yes or no. Nearly everyone says yes, so it teaches the seller nothing. |
| 4 | "Why haven't you fixed this before?" | Assumptive | Assumes there is a problem to fix and that the buyer failed to fix it. The "why" reads as blame. |
| 5 | "How does the matching work today?" | Open | Invites a description in the buyer's words. |
| 6 | "You'd agree that manual work is expensive, right?" | Statement disguised as a question | A statement with a tag asking for agreement. |
| 7 | "Who decides on a purchase like this?" | Open | Asks for names and roles; cannot be answered yes or no. |
| 8 | "What happens when an invoice does not match?" | Open | Invites a sequence of events. |

## Rewrites and probes

Questions 5, 7 and 8 are already open, single and neutral. Keep them as they are. The probes below are for the rewrites.

**1.** Original: "Don't you think your close process is too slow?"
- Rewrite: "How would you describe the month-end close at the moment?"
- It now invites: the buyer's own view of the close, including whether it is a problem at all.
- Probe: "What happens in the last few days of it?"

**2.** Original: "What tools do you use today and how many people work on invoices and what is your budget?" Split into three single questions, asked at different points in the call.
- 2a. Rewrite: "What do you use for invoices today?" It now invites: a list of tools in the buyer's words. Probe: "What does each one do?"
- 2b. Rewrite: "Who is involved in handling invoices?" It now invites: roles and names rather than a headcount. Probe: "How much of their time does it take?"
- 2c. Rewrite: "How is a purchase like this usually funded here?" It now invites: the funding route and who owns it. Probe: "Who holds that budget?" Ask this one late in the call, after the problem and its impact are clear.

**3.** Original: "Is accuracy important to you?"
- Rewrite: "What matters most to you in how invoices are processed?"
- It now invites: the buyer's own priorities, with accuracy appearing only if it is one.
- Probe: "Can you give me an example of when that mattered?"

**4.** Original: "Why haven't you fixed this before?"
- Rewrite: "What, if anything, has been tried so far?"
- It now invites: past attempts, without blame. "What" replaces the accusatory "why".
- Probe: "What happened with that?"

**6.** Original: "You'd agree that manual work is expensive, right?"
- Rewrite: "What effect, if any, does the manual work have on the team?"
- It now invites: the buyer's own account of the cost, in time or other terms, which can be quantified later.
- Probe: "How would you put a number on that?"

## Habits to practise

1. Pause after the answer. Count to three before you speak; buyers often add the important part after a silence.
2. Repeat key words. Echo the last two or three words of the answer as a question ("...not documented?") to draw out more.
3. Ask for an example. After any general statement, ask "Can you give me an example?"

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

Assumptions:
- Classification is from the wording only. No answers, tone or context were available.
- No call goal or persona was given, so the rewrites are generic and not tuned to a buyer.
- Question 7 is classed as open because it asks for names and roles, not yes or no. It could be heard as assuming a purchase will happen.
- The rewrite of question 2c is a suggested place in the call, not a requirement; budget questions can come later.
- No numbers other than counts of the questions are used.

Most likely to be wrong: a question I classed as open may have been used with a leading tone in the call. Wording does not show tone.
