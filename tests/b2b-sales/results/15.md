## Weakest stage

**Deliverability and list quality (sent to delivered).** It has the largest drop against the user's own August period: 74.0% delivered against 94.0% [calc: 1,036/1,400; 846/900], with 364 bounces, 26.0% of sent [calc: 364/1,400], against 6.0% in August [calc: 54/900]. The list behind it is the oldest, dated 2024-03, and was purchased.

Later stages fell too, but they are measured on fewer delivered emails and cannot be judged until the delivery problem is separated out. Copy is not ruled in or out yet; per the method, it is examined after list quality and deliverability.

Compliance flag, for review by the user's counsel and not stated as settled law: the contacts come from a purchased list, and 9 people asked to unsubscribe. Confirm that all 9 are on the do-not-contact list now, and that the lawful basis for contacting a purchased list in Greece (EU) is reviewed before any further send.

## Hypotheses and evidence needed

| # | Hypothesis | Evidence that would confirm | Evidence that would reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | List decay: many addresses on the 2024-03 list no longer exist | Bounce reason codes show mostly "address not found" (hard bounces); a re-verification of a sample finds a similar share invalid | Bounces are mostly temporary (full mailbox, server busy); re-verified sample is mostly valid |
| H2 | Sender reputation damaged by the 26.0% bounce rate, so some "delivered" mail lands in spam | Seed-inbox placement test shows spam placement; spam-complaint count; sender-authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) status; blocklist check | Placement test shows inbox; authentication passes; no complaints or listings |
| H3 | Targeting: purchased list does not match the persona (wrong titles, sizes, countries) | Share of list rows that are Finance Directors at road freight firms of 300 to 600 employees is low on a checked sample | Sample matches the persona |
| H4 | Message and offer: the step 1 sample does not state what Tallyleaf does ("save money on freight" is not invoice matching) and asks for a meeting at once | Content of the 21 replies and 4 positive replies; reasons given for the 9 unsubscribes | Replies are about timing or the wrong person, not the offer |
| H5 | The two periods differ in more than the list (list source, volume, message), so the comparison is confounded | August message, August list source and send settings differ from September's | They match |
| H6 | The 21 "replies (any)" include automatic replies, bounces or unsubscribes, so reply counts overstate interest | Classification of the 21 replies | All 21 are human replies |

Outstanding inputs needed to test these: bounce reason codes, spam-complaint count, authentication status, the August message and list source, and the text of the replies.

## One test

**Variable changed: the contact list only.** Send the unchanged step 1 message, from the same mailbox and domain, at the same daily volume and time of day, to a batch of newly sourced contacts that match the persona and whose addresses were verified before sending. Do not change anything else.

- Measure: delivered rate, compared with September's 74.0% [calc: 1,036/1,400] and August's 94.0% [calc: 846/900].
- Batch size: 100 contacts `[assumption: chosen as a round number above the minimum]`. Minimum to see a difference in delivered rate between 74% and 94% at the conventional 5% significance and 80% power is about 49 per group [calc: (1.96 + 0.84)^2 x (0.74 x 0.26 + 0.94 x 0.06) / (0.74 - 0.94)^2 = 7.84 x 0.2488 / 0.04 = 48.8]; the 1.96 and 0.84 are the standard statistical constants, not an industry benchmark.
- Caution: reply, positive-reply and meeting rates cannot be judged on this batch. Seeing the gap between September's 2.03% and August's 3.66% reply rates [calc: 21/1,036; 31/846] would need about 1,600 delivered emails per group [calc: 7.84 x (0.0203 x 0.9797 + 0.0366 x 0.9634) / (0.0366 - 0.0203)^2 = 1,613]. With 100 contacts, treat reply counts as anecdotal.
- A single batch compared with earlier months is not a controlled comparison; reputation and timing can differ. Read the result as a hypothesis check, not proof.
- If delivered rate stays near 74% on the verified batch, H1 is weakened and H2 (reputation) becomes the next thing to test.

## What not to change

During the test, do not change: the message text, subject line, sender name and domain, the persona, the offer, the daily send volume, the send time, the number of steps, or the follow-up timing. Do not rewrite the copy at the same time as changing the list, or the result cannot be attributed.

Honouring the 9 unsubscribe requests and suppressing the 364 bounced addresses is a compliance and hygiene step, not part of the test, and should happen regardless.

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

Tagged assumption:

- `[assumption]` A test batch of 100 contacts.

Other assumptions and unknowns:

- "Delivered" is the sender system's count of accepted mail, which may include mail filtered to spam.
- I treated "Replies (any)" as separate from the 9 unsubscribe requests; if unsubscribes are inside the 21 replies, the human reply count is lower.
- The August list source, message and send settings are unknown, so the period-to-period comparison cannot isolate a cause.
- Only step 1 data was supplied; later steps and follow-ups were not diagnosed.
- Counts are small (4 positive replies, 2 meetings), so differences between periods are anecdotal.

The one thing most likely to make this diagnosis wrong: that the cause is not list age alone. A sender-reputation or authentication problem would also lower delivery and would not be fixed by a fresh list, which is why H2 is listed with its own evidence.
