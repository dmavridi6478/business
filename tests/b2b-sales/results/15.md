## Weakest stage

Deliverability and list quality: the largest drop is between sent and delivered. Delivery fell 20 percentage points `[calc: 94.0 - 74.0]`, and bounces rose from 6.0% to 26.0%.

The later stages also fell, but they are downstream of that and the counts are small (4 positive replies against 9, 2 meetings against 5). Those differences could be chance.

This points at the list before the message. I cannot yet say that copy is not also weak.

## Hypotheses and evidence needed

| Hypothesis | Evidence that would confirm | Evidence that would reject |
|---|---|---|
| H1: the list has aged and many addresses are dead | Bounce reasons are mostly permanent "address does not exist"; bounce rate rises with contact age in the file | Bounces are mostly temporary or blocks, or are spread evenly regardless of age |
| H2: the purchased list is poor quality | A sample of addresses fails verification at a similar rate; the source cannot say how addresses were collected | Verified sample is mostly valid |
| H3: sending reputation or authentication problem, perhaps from the 55.6% jump in volume | Failed SPF, DKIM or DMARC checks; spam-folder placement; spam-complaint counts (not supplied); bounces mentioning blocking | Authentication is clean and bounce messages are address errors |
| H4: the message is weak | Replies and positives fall even on a verified list; reply text shows confusion about the offer | Rates recover on a verified list with the same message |

Message note, for later and not as a cause: the step 1 sample ("Hi {first_name}, I help companies like yours save money on freight. Can we talk this week?") names no specific problem, no outcome and no opt-out line, and carries only a first name as sender identity. I cannot tell whether a footer with sender identity and opt-out follows. August's message was not supplied, so I cannot compare.

Also read the 21 replies and sort them (positive, neutral, negative, unsubscribe). That is free and informs H4.

## One test

Variable: address verification status, nothing else.

- Group A: 100 random contacts from the remaining 2024-03 list, unverified `[assumption]`.
- Group B: 100 contacts of the same persona whose addresses have been checked or freshly sourced `[assumption]`.
- Same step 1 message, same sending domain, same day and time.
- Measure: bounce rate per group.

Sample size: to see 26% against 6% bounces with 5% significance and 80% power `[assumption]`, I need about 49 per group `[calc: (1.96 + 0.84)^2 x (0.26x0.74 + 0.06x0.94) / (0.26 - 0.06)^2 = 7.84 x 0.2488 / 0.04]`, so 100 per group leaves margin. Seeing the reply-rate gap (2.0% against 3.7%) would need about 1,500 delivered per group `[calc: 7.84 x (0.02x0.98 + 0.037x0.963) / (0.037 - 0.02)^2]`, more than one month's sending, which is why bounce rate is the test measure.

Small samples mislead: a result from 100 per group is a direction, not proof.

## What not to change

While the test runs, keep fixed: the message copy and subject line, the offer, the persona, the sending domain and volume per day, the send time, and the follow-up steps.

Not part of the test, and not optional: add the 364 bounced addresses and the 9 unsubscribe requests to your suppression list, and stop contacting them. Check that the purchased list has a lawful basis for use; rules vary by country and need counsel review.

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

- `[assumption]` August bounces are 54, computed as sent minus delivered. August bounce and unsubscribe counts were not supplied.
- `[assumption]` The test uses 100 per group, 5% significance and 80% power.
- `[assumption]` Groups A and B can be built from your list; their source is not known.
- Counts are small: 4 and 9 positive replies, 2 and 5 meetings. Only the delivery drop is large enough to act on.
- It is not known whether September and August used the same message, persona and sending domain. If they differed, H4 or H3 might explain more than H1.
- It is not known whether the 9 unsubscribe requests are counted within the 21 replies.
- Most likely to make this wrong: that the bounces come from a sending problem (H3), not the list. Bounce reasons settle this before you spend on a new list.
