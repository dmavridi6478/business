## Exit scripts

Draft, needs approval. Not sent. Scripts are for hard disqualifiers only (D1 and D2). Soft ones (D3, D4) get a conversation, not an exit.

**D1: company too small**

> Thank you for talking with us and for explaining how you handle carrier invoices today.
>
> Our software is built for logistics companies with 200 to 2,000 employees. From what you've told me about your size and invoice volume, I don't think we would give you good value, and I'd rather say so now than take up more of your time.
>
> If your business grows, you're welcome to get in touch again.

**D2: native SAP connector required**

> Thank you for being clear about what you need.
>
> We don't currently have a native SAP connector. We support CSV and SFTP file import and a read-only REST API. If a native connector is a must for you, we aren't the right fit today.
>
> If your requirement changes, for example if file import would be acceptable, you're welcome to contact me again.

Referral or alternative: the inputs name no fair alternative resource, so none is offered. Naming a competitor such as Ledgerwise would not be fair advice without evidence of fit. If you have a partner or resource for very small businesses, add it to D1.

## Review before use

- All four items rest on 2 deals or fewer. Treat them as hypotheses and review after the next 10 lost deals.
- Criteria used are business facts (employees, ERP, budget owner, price priority). None relies on a protected characteristic.
- Refusing to sell on size or on missing product features is normally a commercial decision, but a refusal to deal can matter in some jurisdictions or if you hold a dominant position. Flag for your counsel if you plan to apply these as automatic rules.
- Exit scripts describe product facts (no native SAP connector, CSV and SFTP import, REST API) taken from the company file. Check them with product staff before use.
- D3 and D4 depend on asking the buyer to disclose budget or priorities; a buyer who declines to answer is not a failed answer.

## Assumptions and what could be wrong

- [assumption] The lost file lists all lost deals in the period.
- [assumption] "Days open" is a fair proxy for time spent; no hours were supplied.
- [assumption] The 200-employee floor in D1 comes from the company's stated range, not from the deal data.
- Unknown: ERP of won customers, support cost, discounts, which competitors beat us in the other lost deals, the full loss history.
- Most likely to be wrong: D2 and D1 as hard stops. With only two deals each, one exception from a future buyer (for example, a SAP user who accepts CSV import) would show the rule is too strict.
