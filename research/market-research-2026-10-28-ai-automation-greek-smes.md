# Market research: AI automation for Greek SMEs

All dates in this report are as of 2026-10-28.

Conventions: VERIFIED means I opened the page (or the PDF) and read the figure. UNVERIFIED means the page could not be opened, was unreadable, did not contain the figure, or the figure appeared only in a search-result summary. Unverified numbers appear only in the section "Not verified" and in the Sources list. They are not used as facts anywhere else. Source numbers in square brackets refer to the Sources section. "Inference" marks my own reasoning, as opposed to what a source states.

## Research topic

**Topic:** AI automation for Greek SMEs.

**Audience:** owner-managers of Greek small and micro businesses.

**Requester context (assumption):** The requester publishes AI and business content. No About Me file, content strategy or brand positioning was supplied. I assumed a general business-education publisher with no fixed sector focus, writing in English for Greek owner-managers. Correct this if the real positioning differs.

**Method and limits:**
- Searches were run in English and Greek with WebSearch, and sources were opened with WebFetch. Perplexity was not connected.
- Semrush and Ahrefs were not used, so there is no keyword-demand data.
- The competitor gap analysis is therefore qualitative. It rests on what the searches surfaced, not on a systematic crawl of publishers.
- I found no direct first-person owner quotes (forums, interviews). Every "voice" below is either a survey result or a statement by an organisation representing owners, and is labelled that way.
- Several Eurostat, ELSTAT and Commission primary pages could not be read directly. Where a figure comes through a news outlet, it is marked as secondary.

**Definitions that matter.** Eurostat counts enterprises with 10 or more employees, and "small" means 10 to 49 employees [1]. Most Greek micro businesses (0 to 9 employees) are not in Eurostat's AI series. The National Bank of Greece (NBG) study classes size by turnover instead: very small is up to €0.5m, small is €0.5m to €2.5m, medium is €2.5m to €10m, and its sample includes sole traders [3]. The two data sets measure different populations and should not be mixed.

## Main findings

### A. What the sources say about adoption

1. **EU-wide, AI use in enterprises with 10+ employees was 19.95% in 2025.** By size it was 17% for small, 30.36% for medium and 55.03% for large enterprises. The highest country was Denmark at 42.03%, and the lowest was Romania at 5.21%. The page does not state a 2024 figure directly, but the reported 6.47 percentage-point rise implies about 13.48% (the 13.48% is my arithmetic). Data extracted December 2025. [1] VERIFIED.

2. **Greece sits near the bottom of the EU on formal AI adoption.** All Greek enterprises: 8.93% in 2025, against 19.95% for the EU, and 9.81% in 2024. Greek SMEs: 8.51% in 2025, down from 9.53% in 2024. Greek large enterprises: 37.58% in 2025, up from 24.27%. These figures are reported by Naftemporiki on 23 June 2026, which cites Commission and Eurostat data. This is a secondary source, so I verified the article but not the Eurostat table itself. [2] VERIFIED (secondary). The 9.81% for 2024 and an EU rank of 18th are also in ot.gr, 24 January 2025. [4] VERIFIED.

3. **Basic digital intensity among Greek SMEs is rising but still well behind the EU.** In 2025, 55.95% of Greek SMEs had at least a basic level, against 71.39% for the EU. In 2023 the Greek figure was 43.26%. At the very high level the figures were 5.91% for Greece and 9.07% for the EU. The "Digital Tools for SMEs" scheme had supported 101,960 SMEs as of February 2026. [2] VERIFIED (secondary).

4. **A Greek bank survey paints a much more active picture than Eurostat.** The NBG Economic Analysis Division report, first half of 2026 (PDF dated 16 July 2026), is based on KANTAR telephone interviews. There were 600 interviews, run from 31 March to 17 May 2026, with a maximum error of ±4.15% at 95% confidence. 345 firms had turnover up to €1m and 255 had €1m to €10m. [3] VERIFIED. Results:
   - 33% of SMEs use AI professionally.
   - 41% use it only personally.
   - 17% plan to use it in future.
   - 10% say never.
   - By turnover size, professional use was 37% for very small firms and 28% for small firms. The report says use is almost independent of firm size.
   - Reported benefits among professional users: productivity 55%, decision-making 48%, quality of output 45%, sales 32%, cost 31%.
   - Customer management has the largest gap. 47% see potential, but only 14% use AI there.
   - On CRM and ERP, 45% of very small firms use them against 80% of medium firms.
   - The investment-willingness index fell to 45.0 from 46.5. 78% still plan some investment, and 54% are at the "simple thought" stage, up from 43%.

5. **The stated barriers are skills, legal clarity and data protection.** Among EU enterprises that had considered AI but did not use it, the reasons were lack of relevant expertise (70.89%), unclear legal consequences (52.52%), and data protection and privacy concerns (48.83%). "Not useful for the enterprise" was the lowest at 20.68%. [1] VERIFIED. This is EU-wide, not Greek-specific.

6. **Greek non-users are waiting for packaged help, not rejecting AI.** In the NBG survey, among non-users, the main triggers named were ready-made applications (36%), financial incentives (28%), market pressure (19%), proof of benefit (19%), data security (15%) and training programmes (13%). 17% named no trigger. [3] VERIFIED.

7. **Greek business bodies name cost, skills and liability.**
   - The GSEVEE Small Business Institute (IME GSEVEE) told the Ministry of Digital Governance in the AI Act consultation (article dated 28 July 2025) that cost and access to finance are key constraints for micro and small firms. It also named a serious shortage of skilled staff, and asked that SMEs using off-the-shelf AI tools not be liable for the tools' non-compliance. [10] VERIFIED.
   - SEV President Spyros Theodoropoulos, quoted on 12 November 2024: "Το μεγαλύτερο εμπόδιο είμαστε εμείς οι ίδιοι, οι επιχειρηματίες". That is, owners themselves are the biggest barrier. [11] VERIFIED. The accompanying Grant Thornton survey covered about 7,000 companies with turnover above €5m, so it is not a small-business source.
   - An opinion piece in To Vima (19 September 2024) attributes the gap to digital illiteracy among owners and staff, and to hardware, software and training costs. Its statistics are cited from third parties that I did not verify. [12] VERIFIED as an opinion, with the cited statistics not verified.

### B. E-invoicing and myDATA: the immediate deadline

8. **Mandatory B2B e-invoicing for roughly 700,000 businesses with turnover up to €1m now starts on 2 November 2026.** Decision Α.1197/2026 (dated 30 September 2026, ΦΕΚ Β' 5905) says second-phase entities must issue "αποκλειστικά ηλεκτρονικό τιμολόγιο" from 2 November 2026. A transition runs from 2 November 2026 to 31 January 2027. Entities must have filed a start declaration under Α.1112/2025, or declared use of the AADE timologio app, by 2 November 2026. [5] VERIFIED. Earlier reports gave 1 October 2026 [6][8][9]. See Conflicts.

9. **The Athens Chamber of Craftsmen and the Athens Accountants' Association asked AADE to delay the B2B mandate by five months, to 1 March 2027.** They also asked that the early-adopter tax-incentive deadline be extended to 31 December 2026. Powergame, 27 August 2026. [8] VERIFIED. This is an organisational request, not an owner quote, and the outcome of the request is not confirmed.

10. **Accepted channels and exposure.** Invoices go through an accredited provider or AADE's free timologio and myDATAapp. Recipients have had to accept e-invoices since 2 March 2026. [8][9] VERIFIED. Penalties are described differently in the two sources. See Conflicts.

### C. Regulation that touches AI use

11. **The EU AI Act's high-risk deadlines are being deferred.** Gibson Dunn reports a provisional agreement dated 6 May 2026, with stand-alone high-risk systems moved to 2 December 2027 and embedded systems to 2 August 2028. The Article 4 AI-literacy duty (in force since 2 February 2025) is proposed to be softened to a duty to support staff literacy. The fetched article said formal adoption and Official Journal publication were still expected, and it mentions no SME-specific relief. [13] VERIFIED as to the agreement. I could not confirm final adoption (see Not verified).

### D. Tourism and seasonality

12. **I found no verified, SME-level source on AI and tourism seasonality.** Hotel-sector surveys exist (ITEP/RIT, Deloitte with the Hellenic Hoteliers Federation), but I could not open their figures [15][16][17][22]. This is a content gap, not a finding.

### E. Patterns (inference)

- **Pattern 1: The headline gap and the lived gap differ.** Eurostat-based figures (about 9%) describe formal, business-level use in firms of 10 or more. The NBG survey (33% professional use) captures owners using consumer tools such as ChatGPT. Inference: many Greek micro owners are already experimenting, but not in ways official statistics count.
- **Pattern 2: Skills and "what do I do first" beat cost as the stated barrier.** Eurostat's top reason is expertise [1], and NBG's top trigger is ready-made apps [3]. Cost shows up in GSEVEE's and To Vima's framing [10][12] but not as a percentage in the verified surveys.
- **Pattern 3: Compliance deadlines crowd out discretionary digital projects.** Inference: with e-invoicing live from 2 November 2026, micro owners' limited attention will go to compliance first. The NBG finding that investment plans are shifting to "simple thought" [3] is consistent with this, but the report attributes it to geopolitical uncertainty, not e-invoicing.
- **Pattern 4: Intensity of use compounds.** NBG found that 75% of firms using six AI tools plan deeper use next year, against 47% of firms using one tool. Firms using AI intensively also scored 63 on its investment index, against 50 for limited users and 40 for non-users. [3] This is correlation, not proof of causation.

## Conflicts

1. **How many Greek SMEs use AI?**
   - Eurostat-based: 8.51% of SMEs in 2025, down from 9.53% in 2024 [2], and 17% for EU small enterprises [1].
   - NBG/KANTAR: 33% professional use and 74% any use (33% plus 41%) among firms with turnover up to €10m, fieldwork March to May 2026 [3].
   - Likely reasons for the gap (inference): different populations (10+ employees against sole traders and micro firms), different question wording ("uses AI tools" against Eurostat's list of specific AI technologies), and a phone survey with self-reported use. The NBG report itself says its result is consistent with available Eurostat data for Greece, and notes that Greece lags mainly on professional use by self-employed people and employees [3]. Neither figure is wrong. They answer different questions. Publish both with their definitions.

2. **E-invoicing start date for small businesses.**
   - 1 October 2026: Deloitte Greece page (undated, still shows 2 February 2026 for phase 1 and 1 October 2026 for phase 2) [6]; Powergame, 11 July 2026 [9] and 27 August 2026 (which gives a phase-in to 31 December 2026) [8].
   - 2 November 2026 with a phase-in to 31 January 2027: Decision Α.1197/2026, dated 30 September 2026 [5], and Taxually, 9 October 2026 [7].
   - Resolution: the later official decision [5] supersedes the earlier reporting. The Deloitte page appears out of date.
   - A request exists to move the date to 1 March 2027 [8]. I found no verified decision to do so.
   - The start of phase 1 (large firms) is given as 2 February 2026 by Deloitte [6]. A search-result summary of an AADE announcement says it moved to 2 March 2026. That summary is unverified [23]. The Powergame piece confirms only that recipients had to accept e-invoices from 2 March 2026 [8].

3. **E-invoicing penalties.**
   - Powergame, 27 August 2026: for VAT-bearing items, 50% of the tax, with a minimum of €250 or €500 depending on the accounting system. For items without VAT, €500 or €1,000. [8]
   - Powergame, 11 July 2026: fines up to €2,500, and repeat offences up to 100% of the VAT. [9]
   - Both are journalism, not the legal text. Do not state penalties as fact without checking the Tax Procedure Code and AADE guidance.

4. **Greek SME digital intensity.**
   - 55.95% with at least basic intensity in 2025, against 71.39% for the EU, and 43.26% in 2023 [2].
   - 39% for Greece against 55% for the EU, attributed in a September 2024 opinion piece to the Digital Decade National Roadmap [12].
   - These are not directly comparable. The vintages and indicator definitions differ, and the To Vima figure is second-hand. Use [2] for current claims.

5. **Greek AI growth.** ot.gr says Greek AI adoption nearly tripled in a year and also reports 5.83% annual growth [4]. These do not reconcile. Naftemporiki's data show the all-business share falling from 9.81% in 2024 to 8.93% in 2025 [2]. Do not repeat the "tripled" claim.

6. **Whether the problem is owners' mindset or structural barriers.** SEV frames owners as the main obstacle [11]. GSEVEE emphasises cost, liability and a skills shortage [10]. Eurostat shows expertise, legal uncertainty and data protection [1]. Inference: these are not strictly contradictory, but content aimed at owners should avoid blame framing.

7. **AI Act timing.** Gibson Dunn gives the provisional agreement date as 6 May 2026 [13]. Search-result summaries gave 7 May and conflicting adoption dates (16 and 29 June, or 27 July 2026), which I could not confirm. Verify against EUR-Lex before publishing.

## Content ideas

Ranked by urgency and by how well the evidence supports them. Each should carry the source dates above and avoid legal advice.

1. **"From 2 November: what the e-invoicing deadline means for a micro business, and where AI helps and does not."** Urgent, as the start date is days away. Cover the A.1197/2026 dates, the free timologio and myDATAapp routes, the 31 January 2027 transition, and the unresolved request for a delay. Position AI as a helper (matching invoices, drafting reminders to customers), not as a compliance substitute. Evidence base: [5][8][9].

2. **"Are 9% or 33% of Greek firms using AI? Both numbers are right."** Explains the Eurostat versus NBG discrepancy in plain language and shows owners they are probably not behind. Evidence: [1][2][3]. Strongest data-journalism angle, and few publishers appear to reconcile the two.

3. **"What to try first: a beginner's AI plan for firms without an IT person."** The NBG finding that non-users wait for ready-made apps (36%) and the benefits reported by users (productivity, decisions, quality) give a concrete outline. Use customer management as the open gap (47% see potential, 14% use) [3]. Frame the plan as inference from the survey, not a tested method.

4. **"GDPR and AI tools: what a small owner must check before pasting customer data into a chatbot."** Data protection concerns are the third-ranked EU barrier [1]. I did not find a verified Greek small-business source on practice, so this needs Hellenic Data Protection Authority primary sources before writing.

5. **"The AI Act for small users: what changes, what does not."** Use [13] and [10]. Explain that deployers of off-the-shelf tools are the target audience, and flag that the deferral and the AI-literacy softening depend on final adoption, which must be confirmed first.

6. **"Digital Tools for SMEs and Recovery Fund money: what has been funded?"** The 101,960 supported SMEs [2] is a hook, but I have no verified detail on eligibility, current calls or amounts. Needs the Ministry's or the Recovery Fund's primary pages.

7. **Tourism seasonality (hotels, rooms, tavernas).** Evidently under-covered, but I have no verified numbers. Commission or wait for the full HHF-Deloitte and ITEP/RIT reports before publishing. Do not publish the 22.7% or 59% figures until they are checked.

8. **Language support for Greek-language AI.** No grounded source was found. Treat as an open question. A practical test with Greek business texts would be original content, but it is not research.

**Content gaps (inference, from what the searches surfaced).**
- Coverage comes mostly from banks, consultancies, law firms and e-invoicing vendors. I saw little owner-level, plain-language guidance combining AI with Greek compliance (myDATA, GDPR, AI Act).
- Unanswered questions that no verified source addressed: cost of a first AI project for a micro firm, Greek-language quality, accountant roles in AI-assisted bookkeeping, and tourism seasonality.
- Advice worth challenging: "owners' mindset is the problem" [11]; "Greek adoption has tripled" [4]; relying on a single adoption number without stating the population.
